# L18 - The Song-Learning System (Learning Any Melody by Ear)

**Objectives:** SNG-01, SNG-06, EAR-10, THY-14, BRE-04, NOT-05, SNG-03, SNG-04 | **Time span:** first full run takes 1-2 weeks; then you use it for the rest of your musical life | **Prerequisites:** [L10](L10-reading-and-writing-sargam.md), [L15](L15-taal-basics.md), [L16](L16-shuddha-ma-and-half-holing.md), [L17](L17-intonation-and-drone.md), EAR Level 3 in [EX-EAR](../exercises/EX-EAR-ear-training-system.md)

[Conventions](../05-CONVENTIONS-AND-NOTATION-KEY.md) | [Knowledge tree D13](../knowledge-tree/D13-SNG-songs.md) | Milestone: [M6](../projects/M6-song-by-ear.md)

## 1. Learning objectives

- Follow a repeatable **12-step process** to learn an unfamiliar melody by ear (SNG-01).
- Find the **Sa (tonic)** of a recording and match it to a pitch (EAR-10).
- Decide whether a song **fits your flute's range**, and what to do if it does not.
- **Verify** uncertain notes with tools instead of guessing.
- Map a song's **structure** (mukhda, antara, interludes) (THY-14).
- Plan **breath marks** (BRE-04).
- Judge and correct **online notations** (NOT-05).
- Use **slow-down and loop tools** (SNG-06).
- Adjust the process for film, bhajan, folk, ghazal and classical music.

## 2. Prerequisites

- You can play the middle octave with `m` and `M`, plus mandra `.P .D .N`.
- You can identify S, R, G, P against a drone by ear (EX-EAR Levels 2-3).
- You can write rhythmic sargam and count Keherwa/Dadra/Teentaal.

**Honest note:** your first songs will be slow to learn. A 30-second melody may take a whole
week. That is normal. Speed comes from repetitions of this process, not from talent.

## 3. Simple explanation

Learning by ear is **detective work**. You collect clues (the tonic, the shape of the melody,
the rhythm), you make a guess, and you **test** each guess until the evidence agrees. The
golden rule:

> **Never write a note you have not verified.** If you are not sure, mark it with `?` and test it.

## 4. The 12 steps

### Step 1. Listen carefully (3-5 times, no flute)

- Listen to the whole song once just to enjoy it.
- Then listen for **structure** (THY-14): write timestamps for intro, **mukhda/sthayi** (the
  main recurring line, often the chorus), **antara** (verses, usually higher), **interludes**
  (instrumental parts between verses).
- Choose **one section** to learn first: usually the mukhda.

### Step 2. Find the tonal centre (Sa) and context (EAR-10)

1. Hum the **last note** of the mukhda or the song: it usually feels like "home".
2. Change the drone pitch (C, C#, D...) until your hummed "home" note **locks** with the drone
   Sa. Or hold a tuner app to the recording on a long "home" note and read the note name.
3. Test: with the drone on, does the melody feel stable? If many phrases sound sour, try a
   different Sa.
4. Clap along to find the **taal/meter**: Keherwa (8), Dadra (6) or Teentaal (16)? See
   [EX-RHY](../exercises/EX-RHY-rhythm-drills.md).

**If Sa is not C:** you have two choices, explained fully in [L26](L26-keys-transposition-and-limits.md):
(a) play in **your** Sa (C) and accept that you cannot play along with the original; or
(b) pitch-shift the recording to C with a tool ([R-A14](../02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md#72-tuner-metronome-recording-slow-down), [R-A15](../02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md#72-tuner-metronome-recording-slow-down)).
**Recommendation for now:** shift the recording to C so you can compare directly.

### Step 3. Break the melody into short phrases

- A **phrase** is a musical "sentence", usually the length of one line of lyrics or one breath.
- Use a loop tool to repeat one phrase at a time ([R-A14](../02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md#72-tuner-metronome-recording-slow-down), [R-A13](../02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md#72-tuner-metronome-recording-slow-down), or YouTube speed [R-A17](../02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md#72-tuner-metronome-recording-slow-down)).
- Number the phrases: P1, P2, P3...

### Step 4. Sing or hum each phrase

- Sing phrase P1 with the recording 5 times, then **without** it 3 times.
- If you cannot sing it, you cannot yet hear it clearly enough to play it. Slow it down.

### Step 5. Identify the notes by ear

1. Hum the phrase with the drone on.
2. Find the **first note** relative to Sa: is it Sa? Above or below? Sing upward from Sa
   (`S R G...`) until you match it.
3. Then follow the **direction** of each next note: up or down? Step or leap?
4. Write your guess in sargam. Mark doubtful notes with `?`, for example `G R? S`.

### Step 6. Find the notes on your bansuri

- Play your guessed notes. Compare with the recording **one note at a time**.

### Step 7. Practise slowly

- Use the slow-down tool at 60-75% speed **without changing pitch**.
- Play phrase P1 with the slowed recording until 3 clean in a row.

### Step 8. Compare your performance with the reference

- Record yourself playing P1, then play the recording's P1 straight after. Listen to both.

### Step 9. Correct pitch, rhythm, articulation and phrasing

Check in this order:

| Order | Check | Ask yourself |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pitch | Is every note the same? |
| 2 | Rhythm | Do notes start and stop at the same time? Where are the long notes? |
| 3 | Articulation | Is it slurred, tongued, glided (meend)? |
| 4 | Ornament | Are there quick extra notes (kan, murki)? Add these **last**. |
| 5 | Phrasing | Where does the singer breathe? Where is it loud or soft? |

### Step 10. Build up the whole melody

- Chain phrases: P1, P2, then P1+P2, then P3, then P1+P2+P3 ("snowball" method).
- Mark **breath points** with `,` at phrase ends (BRE-04). Never breathe inside a word.

### Step 11. Write and verify the notation

1. Write the full notation with rhythm in a taal grid ([L10](L10-reading-and-writing-sargam.md)).
2. **Verification test:** play from your notation **only** (no recording), record it, then compare
   with the original. Any difference = notation error.
3. If you started from an online notation ([R-S01](../02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md#8-song-notation-sources)),
   correct it now. Typical errors: missing rhythm, missing octave marks, wrong komal/shuddha,
   notes written for a different Sa (NOT-05).

### Step 12. Memorise and reach the intended tempo

- Play from memory, starting from any phrase number.
- Metronome ladder: 70% -> 80% -> 90% -> 100% of the original tempo, 3 clean runs at each.
- Play along with the (pitch-shifted) original at full speed (SNG-09).
- Add the song to your repertoire list with the date ([L01](L01-how-to-practise.md)).

## 5. Verifying uncertain notes (do not guess)

The most useful checks are: **slow down** the recording around the note, put a **tuner** on a
long note, **sing** the note and match it on the flute, and play your candidate **against the
drone** (a wrong note sounds sour). The table lists all six methods.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Reference: six ways to verify a note</summary>

| Method | How | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Slow-down** | 50% speed, loop 1-2 s around the note | Fast notes, ornaments |
| **Tuner on the recording** | Hold the phone to the speaker on a long note | Long notes; also confirms Sa |
| **Sing and compare** | Sing the note, hold it, then play your candidate notes until one matches your voice | Any note you can sing |
| **Drone test** | With drone on, play your candidate against the recording; a wrong note clashes (beating, sourness) | Komal vs shuddha decisions |
| **Context logic** | If the song is in a raga or scale you know, which notes are likely? (Then **check** it.) | Narrowing options, not final proof |
| **Ask someone** | A teacher or experienced player | When three methods disagree |

</details>

If after all methods a note is still uncertain, leave the `?` in your notation and note it in
your log. That is honest; a wrong confident note is worse.

## 6. Checking the range: will the song fit your flute?

Your flute's comfortable range is about **`.P` (G4) to `G'` (E6)**. `M'` (F#6) is the practical
ceiling: reachable, but often weak.

1. Find the **lowest** and **highest** notes of the song (in sargam, relative to the song's Sa).
2. If the song's Sa = C (or you shift it to C): lowest note must be `.P` or higher; highest
   should ideally be `G'` or lower.
3. If the song goes **below `.P`** (for example to `.m`), it does not fit with Sa = C. Options:
   choose a different Sa position on the same flute ([L26](L26-keys-transposition-and-limits.md)),
   play that phrase an octave higher, or use a different flute later.
4. Many film songs need notes above `G'` in the antara. Learn the mukhda first.

## 7. How the process changes by style

The 12 steps stay the same for every style; what changes is where the difficulty lies. In
film songs it is usually the **rhythm**; in bhajans, **breath and tone** on long notes; in
folk tunes, a **steady, lively pulse**; in ghazals, the **ornaments** (learn the plain melody
first); in classical compositions, **raga grammar and starting on the right beat**.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Reference: what to listen for in each style</summary>

| Style | What to listen for extra | Special care |
|---|---|---|
| **Film songs** | Western-style rhythm, syncopation (notes off the beat), interludes played by instruments | Rhythm is often the hard part. Tap the beat while listening. Often uses komal notes and wide range. Melody notation on websites rarely shows rhythm. |
| **Bhajans** | Long sustained notes, meend (glides), repetition of lines | Breath planning; tone. Usually simpler notes, often raga-based (Yaman, Bhairavi, Pahadi...). |
| **Folk tunes** | Dance rhythms (Dadra, Keherwa), tonguing, repeated notes | Steady pulse; lively articulation. |
| **Ghazal** | Many ornaments (murki, kan), free timing (rubato) | Learn the plain melody first, add ornaments last. |
| **Classical** (bandish, gat) | Raga grammar, exact taal position, ornaments are compulsory parts of the raga | Learn the raga first ([L19](L19-what-is-a-raga.md)); start the composition at the right beat (mukhda may begin before sam). |

</details>

## 8. Worked example: Mary Had a Little Lamb

Pretend you do not know it and you have a recording.

1. **Listen:** short, two phrases, simple. 4-beat feel. Use Keherwa.
2. **Sa:** the last note feels like home. Tuner on the recording says C. Good: Sa = C.
3. **Phrases:** P1 "Mary had a little lamb", P2 "little lamb, little lamb", P3 repeat of P1, P4 ending.
4. **Sing** each.
5. **Identify:** first note is above Sa. Singing up `S R G`: it matches G. Then it goes down
   by steps: G R S, then back up: R G G G. Guess: `G R S R G G G`.
6. **On flute:** matches. P2: "little lamb" = 3 same notes, then up a leap: `R R R - G P P -`.
   Is the leap to P or D? **Verify** by singing: the top matches P. Confirmed.
7-10. Practise slowly, compare, snowball P1-P4.

11. **Notation** (Keherwa):

```
 X                 0
 G   R   S   R  |  G   G   G   -   ||
 R   R   R   -  |  G   P   P   -   ||
 G   R   S   R  |  G   G   G   G   ||
 R   R   G   R  |  S   -   -   -   ||
```

12. **Memorise**, then play at full tempo with a Keherwa loop.

## 11. Common mistakes and corrections

| Mistake | Correction | Guide |
|---|---|---|
| Trying to learn the whole song at once | One phrase at a time, snowball | [TS-10](../troubleshooting/TS-10-ear-and-memory.md) |
| Guessing notes and memorising the guess | Mark `?`, verify with section 5 methods | [TS-10](../troubleshooting/TS-10-ear-and-memory.md) |
| Trusting online notation completely | Always play it against the recording | [L27](L27-bhatkhande-notation.md) |
| Ignoring rhythm (only notes) | Write the taal grid; clap the rhythm first | [TS-09](../troubleshooting/TS-09-rhythm-and-tempo.md) |
| Choosing songs far beyond your level | Start with bhajans/folk tunes with small range; pick from the staged [song suggestions](../09-SONG-SUGGESTIONS.md) | [EX-MEL](../exercises/EX-MEL-practice-melodies.md) |
| Adding ornaments too early | Plain melody first, ornaments last (step 9) | [L24](L24-kan-khatka-murki.md) |

## 12. Quiz

1. Which part of a song is usually learned first?
2. Name three methods to verify an uncertain note.
3. A song's lowest note (with Sa = C) is `.m`. Does it fit? What can you do?
4. Where should you take breaths?
5. In step 9, which do you correct first, pitch or ornaments?

<details markdown="1"><summary>Answer key</summary>

1. The mukhda (main recurring line).
2. Any three of: slow-down, tuner on the recording, sing and compare, drone test, context logic (then check), ask someone.
3. No, `.m` is below `.P`. Use a different Sa position (L26), move that phrase up an octave, or use another flute later.
4. At phrase ends (marked `,`), never inside a word.
5. Pitch first; ornaments last.

</details>
