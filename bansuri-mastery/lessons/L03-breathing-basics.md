# L03 - Breathing Basics

**Objectives:** BRE-01, BRE-02, BRE-03, BRE-04, BRE-06
**Time span:** Days 1-14 (a few minutes daily; BRE-04 is completed later, in Phase 2)
**Prerequisites:** [L02](L02-posture-and-holding.md) (sitting posture)
**Previous:** [L02](L02-posture-and-holding.md) | **Next:** [L04 - First Sound](L04-first-sound.md)

---

## 1. Learning objectives

- Breathe low, into the belly and lower ribs, with still shoulders (BRE-01).
- Take a quick, silent, full breath through the corners of the mouth (BRE-02).
- Release air in a steady, controlled stream for 20-30 seconds (BRE-03).
- Plan where to breathe in a melody and mark it (BRE-04).
- Recognise dizziness and know exactly what to do (BRE-06).

## 2. Prerequisites

The sitting posture from [L02](L02-posture-and-holding.md). Breathing exercises need an upright spine and a free belly.

## 3. Simple explanation

The flute is played with your breath, so your breath is your bow (as a violinist would say). Two things matter:

1. **How much air you can take in quickly.** Low breathing fills the lungs more fully than raising the shoulders.
2. **How steadily you let it out.** A steady air stream gives a steady tone and pitch. Uneven air gives wobbly, wavering notes.

The bansuri does not need a huge amount of force. It needs **steady, controlled** air. Beginners almost always blow too much air, too fast. That wastes breath, makes the tone airy, and makes them dizzy.

## 4. Step-by-step instructions

### Exercise A: Low breathing (BRE-01), 2 minutes

1. Sit tall. Put one hand on your belly (just below the ribs) and one on your upper chest.
2. Breathe in slowly through your nose for 4 counts. The **lower hand** moves out and your lower ribs widen sideways. The upper hand stays almost still. Shoulders do not rise.
3. Breathe out through your mouth for 6 counts. The lower hand moves back in.
4. Repeat 10 times.

**Note:** "diaphragmatic breathing" is the common name. Strictly, the diaphragm is used in every breath; what you are learning is to let the belly and lower ribs expand so the diaphragm can move freely (Fact).

### Exercise B: The hiss (BRE-03), 3 minutes

1. Take a low breath in 4 counts.
2. Breathe out through slightly parted teeth with an even "sssss" sound. Time it.
3. The hiss must stay **the same volume from start to end**. No burst at the beginning, no fading or straining at the end.
4. Stop when you can no longer keep it even. Do not squeeze out the last bit of air.
5. Rest 2 normal breaths. Repeat 3-5 times. Log your best even time.

**Targets:** Week 1: 15 s. Week 2: 20 s. End of Phase 1: 30 s.

### Exercise C: Quick inhale (BRE-02), 2 minutes

In music you rarely have 4 slow counts to breathe. You need about 1 beat.

1. Set the metronome to 60 BPM.
2. Breathe in through the **corners of the mouth** in **1 beat**, silently, and low (belly moves).
3. Hiss out for 7 beats.
4. Repeat 4 times. Then try breathing in for 1 beat and hissing for 3, four times.

If you hear a gasping noise, open the mouth corners slightly more and relax the throat.

### Exercise D: Breath marks (BRE-04)

Musicians plan their breaths like a speaker plans pauses. In this curriculum, a comma `,` in notation means **breathe here**.

Rules of thumb (Recommendation):

- Breathe at the end of a phrase (a musical "sentence"), usually after a long note or a rest.
- Never breathe in the middle of a fast run of notes.
- Breathe **before** you are empty. Breathing when your air is three-quarters used keeps the tone steady.
- Steal time for a breath from the end of the note before it, not from the start of the next note.

Example (8 beats): `S R G - , G R S - ,` means play S R G (G held 2 beats), breathe, play G R S, breathe.

You will apply this properly once you can play melodies ([L10](L10-reading-and-writing-sargam.md), [L18](L18-song-learning-system.md)).

### Exercise E: The dizziness protocol (BRE-06)

Feeling light-headed while learning a wind instrument is **common and usually harmless**. It comes from breathing out more air than usual and breathing faster than you need to (over-breathing).

If you feel dizzy, light-headed or tingly:

1. **Stop playing immediately.** Lower the flute.
2. Stay seated (or sit down).
3. Breathe **normally and gently** through your nose, not deeply, for 1-2 minutes.
4. Continue only when you feel completely normal. Use **less air** and take longer rests between long tones.

**When to get medical advice:** if dizziness comes with chest pain, fainting, a severe headache or shortness of breath at rest, or if it happens even with gentle playing, stop and consult a doctor.

## 7. Guided practice (daily, Days 1-14, about 6 min inside the warm-up)

1. Exercise A: 10 breaths.
2. Exercise B: 3-5 hisses, logging the best time.
3. Exercise C: 4 cycles of 1-in/7-out and 4 cycles of 1-in/3-out at 60 BPM.

## 8. Independent exercises

1. **Lying-down check:** lie on your back with a light book on your belly. Breathe so the book rises and falls. This shows you what low breathing feels like.
2. **Walking breaths:** during a walk, breathe in for 4 steps and out for 6.
3. From Phase 2: mark breath commas on every melody you learn before playing it.

## 9. Common mistakes and corrections

| Mistake | How you notice | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Shoulders rise on inhale | Mirror; hand on chest moves | Hand on belly; imagine filling from the bottom |
| Gasping inhale | Audible noise; throat tight | Open mouth corners wider; relax the throat |
| Uneven hiss | Starts loud, then fades | Start more gently; aim for a constant "sss" |
| Squeezing out the last air | Strain in neck and face | Stop when the hiss can't stay even |
| Dizziness | Light-headed, tingling | Dizziness protocol; use less air |

More: [TS-07](../troubleshooting/TS-07-breath-dizziness-and-endurance.md).

## 10. Quiz

1. Which hand should move during low breathing, the one on the belly or the one on the chest?
2. Through which part of the mouth do you take a quick breath?
3. What does `,` mean in this curriculum's notation?
4. You feel light-headed after 3 long tones. What do you do?
5. Should you breathe when your air has completely run out?

<summary>Answer key</summary>

1. The hand on the belly.
2. The corners of the mouth.
3. Breathe here.
4. Stop, stay seated, breathe gently and normally for 1-2 minutes, then continue with less air and longer rests. See a doctor if it comes with chest pain, fainting or a severe headache.
5. No. Breathe before you run out, at about three-quarters used.
