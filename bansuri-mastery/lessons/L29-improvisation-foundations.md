## 1. Learning objectives

1. Answer a short musical phrase with your own phrase (call and response).
2. Improvise for 1-2 minutes using 3 notes, then the 5 notes `S R G P D`, over a drone, keeping a pulse and ending on Sa.
3. Create at least 4 distinct variations of a known melody.

## 2. Prerequisites

You can play `.P` to `S'` cleanly (at least `S R G P D`), keep a steady pulse with a metronome
or keherwa loop, and play with a C drone.

## 3. Simple explanation

**Improvisation** means making up music while you play. Hindustani music is built on it: in a
raga performance, most of what you hear is improvised within strict rules.

The big misunderstanding is that improvisation means "anything goes". It is the opposite.
**Good improvisation comes from limits.** With all 12 notes and no rules, beginners freeze.
With 3 notes, a pulse and the rule "end on Sa", anyone can start.

Think of it like talking. You did not learn to speak by inventing sentences from nothing. You
copied phrases, then changed one word, then two. This lesson follows the same path:

1. **Copy and answer** (call and response).
2. **Limited notes** (a small "vocabulary").
3. **Variations** (change one thing in something you already know).

## 4. Step-by-step instructions

### 4.1 Call and response (IMP-01)

**Setup:** drone on C, keherwa loop or metronome at 70 BPM.

1. **Copy round.** Play a 4-beat phrase, record it, then play it back exactly. Or use a
   teacher, a friend or a video: they play, you copy. (This is EAR-09 practice too.)
2. **Answer round.** Now play a "question" phrase of 4 beats that ends on a note that feels
   *unfinished* (R, G or D). Then answer with 4 beats that end on S.

```
Question:  S R G -   (ends on G: feels open)
Answer:    G R S -   (ends on S: feels closed)

Question:  .D S R -
Answer:    G R S -

Question:  P G R G
Answer:    R S .D S
```

3. Make 8 question-answer pairs in a row **without stopping**. If you play a "wrong" note, keep
   going: the rule is "never stop the pulse".

### 4.2 Limited-note improvising over a drone (IMP-02)

**Level 1: three notes.** Use only `S R G`. For 1 minute:

- Vary **duration** (long notes, short notes).
- Vary **order** (`S R G`, `G R S`, `R G R`, `S G R`).
- Vary **repetition** (`G G R`, `S S S R`).
- Add **silence** (rests are part of music).
- End on `S`.

**Level 2: add `.D` and `P`.** Now use `.D S R G P` (5 notes). This pentatonic set has no
"clashing" notes, so almost any order sounds musical. It is the scale of Raga Bhupali, though
for now we are not following raga rules, only exploring.

**Level 3: shape.** A good 1-minute improvisation often has:

```
Start low and calm  ->  slowly rise  ->  reach a high point  ->  come back down to Sa
     (.D S R)              (R G P)          (P D S')               (G R S)
```

Record each improvisation. Listen back and score with the rubric ([L08](L08-recording-and-self-assessment.md)):
did you keep the pulse? Did you end on Sa? Did anything repeat too much?

### 4.3 Variations on a melody (IMP-03)

Choose a melody you know well (for example one from [EX-MEL](../exercises/EX-MEL-practice-melodies.md)).
Change **one thing at a time**:

| Variation type | How | Example on `S R G - | G R S -` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rhythm | Change durations, keep notes | `SR G - - | GR S - -` |
| Octave | Play part in another octave | `S R G - | G' R' S' -` (second half an octave up, after [L22](L22-taar-saptak.md)) |
| Repetition | Repeat a fragment | `S R G R | G R G R | S - - -` |
| Ornament | Add a kan or meend (after [L23](L23-meend.md)/[L24](L24-kan-khatka-murki.md)) | `S R {P}G - | G~R S -` |
| Passing notes | Fill gaps with neighbour notes | `S R G P G - | G R S .N S -` |
| Inversion | Turn the direction upside down | `S .D .P - | .P .D S -` |

Make four variations, write them down in sargam, record them. Then try mixing two types.

### 4.4 Simple practice games

- **Dice game:** assign notes to dice numbers (1 = S, 2 = R, 3 = G, 4 = P, 5 = D, 6 = rest). Roll 8 times, play the result as a phrase, then "fix" it to sound good.
- **Echo yourself:** improvise 4 beats, then repeat them exactly. Trains memory and control.
- **One-note solo:** play only Sa for 30 seconds, but make it interesting with rhythm, dynamics and silence. Shows how much expression rhythm alone carries.

## 9. Common mistakes and corrections

| Mistake | Why | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Stopping after a "wrong" note | Fear of mistakes | Rule: never stop the pulse; repeat the note as if you meant it |
| Playing endless fast notes | Nervousness | Use long notes and rests; limit to 1 note per beat |
| Every phrase sounds the same | No variety | Change one parameter each phrase: length, direction, register |
| Wandering without ending | No plan | Always end on Sa; plan the last 4 beats |
| Losing the beat | Concentrating on notes | Use a tabla loop; tap your foot ([TS-09](../troubleshooting/TS-09-rhythm-and-tempo.md)) |
| Not recording | "It was just practice" | Recording is how you learn what works ([L08](L08-recording-and-self-assessment.md)) |

## 10. Performance test

1. **IMP-01:** with a 70 BPM loop, play 8 question-answer pairs (4 beats each) without stopping. Each answer ends on S.
2. **IMP-02:** record a 2-minute improvisation on `.D S R G P D S'` over a drone and loop. Self-score with the rubric.
3. **IMP-03:** play a melody and 4 variations; write each in sargam.

<details markdown="1"><summary>What a passing performance sounds like</summary>

- The pulse never breaks, even if a note is unexpected.
- Phrases have a beginning and an end; you can hear "breathing points".
- There is variety: long and short notes, high and low, rest and movement.
- The final note is Sa, held and in tune with the drone.
- In variations, a listener still recognises the original melody.

</details>

## 11. Quiz

1. In call and response, which notes make a question phrase feel open, and where does the answer end?
2. You play an unexpected note in the middle of an improvisation. What is the rule?
3. Name four things you can vary when improvising with only `S R G`.
4. Why does the 5-note set `.D S R G P` sound musical in almost any order?
5. When making a variation of a melody, how many things should you change at a time? Name three variation types.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answer key</summary>

1. The question ends on an unfinished note such as R, G or D; the answer ends on S.
2. Never stop the pulse. Keep going, or repeat the note as if you meant it.
3. Duration, order, repetition and silence (rests). Always end on `S`.
4. It is pentatonic and has no clashing notes (it is the scale of Raga Bhupali).
5. One thing at a time. Any three of: rhythm, octave, repetition, ornament, passing notes, inversion.

</details>
