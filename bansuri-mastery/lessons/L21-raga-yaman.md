## 1. Learning objectives

- Play Yaman's aroh, avroh and pakad from memory, in tune.
- Play its characteristic movements: `.N R G`, `G M D N`, `M D N S'`.
- Play swar vistar phrases and an original practice composition in Teentaal.
- Explain how Yaman differs from Bhupali even though both are in Kalyan thaat.

## 2. Prerequisites

- Clean tivra Ma (all open) and the full middle octave ([L14](L14-tivra-ma-and-madhya-pa-dha-ni.md)).
- Bhupali skills ([L20](L20-raga-bhupali.md)).

## 3. Simple explanation

**Yaman** (also called Kalyan or Iman) is often the **first sampurna (7-note) raga** taught to
Hindustani students. It is serene, romantic and devotional, and is used in countless bhajans,
ghazals and film songs.

On the bansuri it is especially friendly: its one altered note, **tivra Ma**, is the
**all-holes-open** fingering. You can play the entire raga with no half-holes.

| Element | Yaman | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Thaat | Kalyan | Fact |
| Notes | All shuddha + tivra Ma: `S R G M P D N` | Fact |
| Aroh | `.N R G M D N S'` (Pa and Sa are often skipped going up) | Fact; some books write `S R G M P D N S'` (Disputed: simplified form) |
| Avroh | `S' N D P M G R S` | Fact |
| Vadi / samvadi | Ga / Ni | Fact (standard) |
| Pakad | `.N R G R , .N R S , P M G R , .N R S` | Widely cited version; others exist |
| Time | Early night | Tradition; sources differ on exact hours ([L19](L19-what-is-a-raga.md)) |
| Mood | Serene, prayerful, romantic | Tradition |

## 4. How Yaman moves (chalan)

1. **Start low:** phrases often begin from mandra Ni: `.N R G`. Going up, Yaman tends to skip
   Sa (`.N R`, not `.N S R`) and often skips Pa (`M D`, not `M P D`).
2. **Ga is the centre** (vadi). **Ni** is the strong partner (samvadi).
3. **Tivra Ma leans upward** and is rarely held long alone; it usually leads to Ga below or to
   Dha above (`G M G`, `M D N`).
4. **Descending is full:** `S' N D P M G R S`.
5. **Rest notes:** Ga, Pa, Ni, Sa (and Re in `.N R S`).

**Yaman vs Bhupali:** both are Kalyan thaat. Bhupali omits Ma and Ni and sounds open and
simple. Yaman includes them, and the tivra Ma and Ni add a bright, yearning quality.

## 5. Swar vistar phrases (slow, with drone)

```
1.  .N R G , R , .N R S
2.  .N R G M G , R G R , .N R S
3.  G M P , M G , R G R , .N R S
4.  G M D , M G , R G M D N D P , M G R S
5.  M D N S' , N D P , M G R , .N R S
6.  G M D N S' , S' N D P , M D N D P , M G , R G R , .N R S
7.  (pakad) .N R G R , .N R S , P M G R , .N R S
```

**What you should hear:** a smooth, flowing, slightly longing sound. Tivra Ma should sound
bright but never sour. If M sounds weak or airy (common on bansuri), steady the flute with
your thumbs and chin, and check it against the drone ([L17](L17-intonation-and-drone.md)).

## 6. Practice composition (ORIGINAL, written for this curriculum)

> **Note:** an **original practice composition** obeying Yaman's rules, not a traditional
> bandish. For traditional compositions see [R-T01](../02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md#5-theory-and-raga-references),
> [R-T04](../02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md#5-theory-and-raga-references), or a teacher.

**Teentaal, madhya laya (70-90 BPM). Starts on sam.**

**Sthayi:**

```
 X                 2                 0                 3
.N   R   G   R  | .N   R   S   -  |  G   M   D   N  |  D   P   M   G   ||
 G   M   D   N  |  D   P   M   G  |  R   G   R  .N  |  R   -   S   -   ||
```

**Antara:**

```
 X                 2                 0                 3
 M   D   N   S' |  S'  -   N   S' |  N   D   P   M  |  G   M   D   N   ||
 S'  N   D   P  |  M   G   R   G  |  R   G   R  .N  |  R   -   S   -   ||
```

Return to the sthayi; end with its first line.

## 9. Independent exercises

1. Contrast drill: play Bhupali's pakad, then Yaman's, 3 times each. Describe the difference in mood in your log.
2. Find 2 songs or bhajans said to be based on Yaman (search, then **verify** by ear that they
   use tivra Ma). Learn one phrase from each using [L18](L18-song-learning-system.md).
3. Write 4 new Yaman phrases that obey section 4.

## 10. Common mistakes and corrections

| Mistake | Correction | Guide |
|---|---|---|
| Playing `S R G M P D N S'` straight up like a scale | Use `.N R G M D N S'`; skip Sa and Pa going up | Section 4 |
| Weak, airy tivra Ma | Steady grip, slightly more focused air | [TS-01](../troubleshooting/TS-01-no-sound-or-airy-tone.md) |
| Accidentally playing shuddha Ma (half-hole) | Yaman uses **only** tivra Ma (all open) | [L16](L16-shuddha-ma-and-half-holing.md) |
| `M -> P` and `M -> D` changes messy | Practise the pairs slowly | [TS-06](../troubleshooting/TS-06-finger-leaks-and-transitions.md) |
| Ni flat in register 2 | Faster, focused air; drone check | [TS-05](../troubleshooting/TS-05-pitch-out-of-tune.md) |

## 11. Quiz

1. Which Ma does Yaman use, and what is its bansuri fingering?
2. Name the vadi and samvadi.
3. Which notes are often skipped in Yaman's ascent?
4. Write the widely cited pakad.
5. How is Yaman different from Bhupali?

<details markdown="1"><summary>Answer key</summary>

1. Tivra Ma; all holes open (`OOO OOO`).
2. Ga and Ni.
3. Sa (starting `.N R`) and Pa (`M D`).
4. `.N R G R , .N R S , P M G R , .N R S`.
5. Same thaat (Kalyan), but Yaman uses all 7 notes including tivra Ma and Ni; Bhupali omits Ma and Ni.

</details>
