## 1. Learning objectives

- Play Bhupali's **aroh, avroh and pakad** from memory, in tune with a drone.
- Play a set of **swar vistar** (slow note-development) phrases that sound like Bhupali.
- Perform a **short composition in Teentaal** with a tabla loop.
- Improvise a 2-3 minute slow exploration that keeps to the raga's rules.

## 2. Prerequisites

- Clean `.D S R G P D S'` including the register change to `P` ([L14](L14-tivra-ma-and-madhya-pa-dha-ni.md)).
- Teentaal recitation ([L15](L15-taal-basics.md)).
- Raga vocabulary ([L19](L19-what-is-a-raga.md)).

## 3. Simple explanation

Bhupali (also spelled Bhoopali, Bhoop) is one of the first ragas taught to Hindustani
students, and it is ideal for the bansuri:

- It uses only **5 notes**: `S R G P D`. No Ma, no Ni, so **no half-holes**.
- It is calm, open and devotional in mood.
- It is the same scale as the "major pentatonic" used in folk music around the world, so it
  will sound friendly to your ear.

| Element | Bhupali | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Thaat | Kalyan | Fact |
| Notes | `S R G P D` (Ma and Ni varjit) | Fact |
| Jati | Audav-audav (5 up, 5 down) | Fact |
| Aroh | `S R G P D S'` | Fact |
| Avroh | `S' D P G R S` | Fact |
| Vadi / samvadi | Ga / Dha | Fact (standard) |
| Pakad | `G R S .D S R G, P G D P G R S` | Recommendation: common version; phrases differ between sources |
| Time | Evening, first part of the night | Tradition; exact times vary |
| Mood | Peaceful, devotional | Tradition |

## 4. How Bhupali moves (chalan)

1. **Ga is the centre.** Phrases often rest on Ga. Give it time.
2. **`G R` then `S`, and `.D S`:** coming down from Ga to Sa often passes through Re,
   and Sa is often approached from mandra Dha (`.D S`).
3. **`P G` and `D P`:** descending steps that skip the missing Ma and Ni.
4. **Avoid sounding like Raga Deshkar** (same notes, different raga): Deshkar avoids resting on
   Re and Sa in the lower part and stresses Pa and Dha. In Bhupali, stress Ga and use
   `G R S` and `.D S` freely. (You do not need to know Deshkar yet; just keep Bhupali centred on Ga.)
5. **Gentle glides:** a light meend from Ga down to Re, `G~R`, is typical. Try it lightly
   now; it is taught properly in [L23](L23-meend.md).

## 5. Swar vistar phrases (play slowly, with drone, no tabla)

Play each phrase 3 times. Hold the last note of each for 3-4 seconds. Breathe at `,`.

```
1.  S , .D S , S R S , .D S
2.  S R G , G R , S R G , R S
3.  .D S R G , G R S , .D S
4.  R G P , G P , P G , G R S
5.  G P D P , G R , S R G
6.  P D S' , D P , G P D P , G R S
7.  G P D S' , S' D P , D P G , G R S , .D S
8.  (pakad) G R S .D S R G , P G D P G R S
```

**What you should hear:** a peaceful, open, slightly "bright" sound. If a phrase sounds like a
nursery rhyme, you are probably rushing; slow down and lean on Ga and Pa.

## 6. Practice composition (ORIGINAL, written for this curriculum)

> **Note:** this is an **original practice composition** written to obey Bhupali's rules. It
> is not a traditional bandish. Traditional compositions are learned from a teacher or from
> sources such as [R-T01](../02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md#5-theory-and-raga-references) and
> [R-T04](../02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md#5-theory-and-raga-references).

**Teentaal, madhya laya (about 70-90 BPM). Starts on sam for simplicity.**

**Sthayi** (main part, 2 cycles):

```
 X                 2                 0                 3
 G   G   R   S  | .D   S   R   G  |  P   G   D   P  |  G   R   S   -   ||
 G   P   D   S' |  D   P   G   P  |  G   R   S  .D  |  S   R   G   -   ||
```

**Antara** (upper part, 2 cycles):

```
 X                 2                 0                 3
 P   P   D   S' |  S'  -   D   S' |  D   P   G   P  |  D   P   G   R   ||
 G   P   D   S' |  D   P   G   R  |  S   R   G   P  |  G   R   S   -   ||
```

Return to the sthayi after the antara. End with the first line of the sthayi.

## 7. Watch and listen

- [R-L03](../02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md#9-listening-library) *The Raga Guide* Bhupali track: **the main reference recording**. Listen 5 times before playing.
- [R-T10](../02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md#5-theory-and-raga-references) Bhupali (Wikipedia) and [R-T01](../02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md#5-theory-and-raga-references) SwarGanga: compare aroh, avroh, pakad. Note any differences in pakad.
- **Bansuri recordings** ([Listening Library: Bhupali](../08-LISTENING-LIBRARY.md#bhupali)): start with [R-L10](../08-LISTENING-LIBRARY.md#bhupali) (Pravin Godkhindi, 4:29) and [R-L11](../08-LISTENING-LIBRARY.md#bhupali) (Call of the Valley, Bhoop, 6:20). Later, [R-L12](../08-LISTENING-LIBRARY.md#bhupali) (Hariprasad Chaurasia, full performance). Most are not in C: find the performer's Sa first, then check that you hear only `S R G P D`.

## 8. Guided practice (20 minutes per day, replacing part of the repertoire block)

| Minutes | Activity |
|---|---|
| 3 | Aroh/avroh with drone at 50 BPM, then free time (no metronome) |
| 2 | Pakad x 5, slowly |
| 5 | Swar vistar phrases 1-8 with drone |
| 6 | Composition: week 1 sthayi only, week 2 antara, week 3 both, with Teentaal loop at 70 BPM, then 80, 90 |
| 4 | Free exploration: 2-3 min of your own slow phrases using only `S R G P D`, ending on Sa. Record it. |

## 9. Independent exercises

1. Sing aroh, avroh and pakad with the drone before playing them.
2. Write 4 new swar vistar phrases of your own and test them against the raga rules: only
   `S R G P D`; centred on Ga; ending on Ga, Pa or Sa.
3. Listen to R-L03 and write down any phrase you can recognise.

## 10. Common mistakes and corrections

| Mistake | Correction | Guide |
|---|---|---|
| Playing aroh/avroh up and down repeatedly and calling it a raga | Use phrases (section 5); dwell on Ga and Pa | [L19](L19-what-is-a-raga.md) |
| Rushing the swar vistar | Hold phrase-ending notes 3-4 s; breathe | [TS-07](../troubleshooting/TS-07-breath-dizziness-and-endurance.md) |
| Sharp `S'` and `P` | Less air speed; drone check | [TS-05](../troubleshooting/TS-05-pitch-out-of-tune.md) |
| Starting the composition off sam | Listen 2 cycles first; count in aloud | [TS-09](../troubleshooting/TS-09-rhythm-and-tempo.md) |
| Accidentally playing Ma or Ni | Write the 5 notes on a card in front of you | - |

## 11. Quiz

1. Which notes are varjit in Bhupali?
2. Name the vadi and samvadi.
3. Write the aroh and avroh.
4. Which note should phrases often rest on?
5. Which raga has the same notes but a different character?

<details markdown="1"><summary>Answer key</summary>

1. Ma and Ni.
2. Ga (vadi), Dha (samvadi).
3. `S R G P D S'` / `S' D P G R S`.
4. Ga (also Pa and Sa).
5. Deshkar.

</details>
