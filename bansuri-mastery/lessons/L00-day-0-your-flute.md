## 1. Learning objectives

By the end of today you will:

- Name every part of your bansuri (INS-01).
- Have inspected it for cracks and problems (INS-03).
- Know the daily care and hygiene routine (INS-04, INS-06), and what your maker says about oiling (INS-05).
- Have set up the four tools you need: a drone, a tuner, a metronome and a recorder.
- Have chosen which side you hold the flute on (a permanent decision).
- Have checked, or planned to check, that your Sa really is C5 (INS-02).

## 2. Prerequisites

None. You do not need to make a sound today. If you can already make a sound, do the tuner check in step 6. If you can't, do it after [L04](L04-first-sound.md).

## 3. Simple explanation

A bansuri is a tube of bamboo. You blow across one hole (the **blowing hole**), and the air inside the tube vibrates and makes a sound. When you cover the **finger holes**, the vibrating column of air gets longer and the sound gets lower. When you open holes, it gets shorter and the sound gets higher. That is the whole instrument. There are no keys, pads or moving parts, so the flute's condition and your body do all the work.

Bamboo is a living material. It reacts to heat, dryness and moisture, so a little daily care keeps it playable for years.

## 4. Step-by-step instructions

### Step 1: Learn the parts (10 min)

Hold the flute and find each part in the diagram below. Say the names aloud.

### Step 2: Inspect the flute (10 min)

Work through this checklist in good light:

- [ ] **Cracks:** look along the whole length, especially near the ends, the blowing hole and the bindings. A crack is a thin dark line that follows the grain.
- [ ] **Bindings:** the thread or cord wraps (if any) are tight, not loose or unravelling.
- [ ] **Cork/stopper:** the closed end near the blowing hole is plugged. **Do not push or move the cork.** Its position tunes the flute.
- [ ] **Hole edges:** run a fingertip gently around each hole. The edges should be smooth, with no splinters.
- [ ] **Blowing hole:** the edge is clean and sharp, not chipped.
- [ ] **Straightness:** a slight curve is normal in bamboo. A twist or bend near the holes is worth asking the seller about.
- [ ] **Smell/moisture:** no mould or damp smell.

If you find a crack or a splinter, stop and read [TS-12](../troubleshooting/TS-12-flute-damage-and-care.md) before playing.

### Step 3: Learn the care routine (5 min)

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| Before playing | Wash your hands. Rinse your mouth if you have eaten. Do not play with food or sweets in your mouth. |
| After playing | Wipe the outside dry with a soft cloth. Hold the flute vertically for a minute so moisture drains, then wipe again. |
| Storage | Keep it in a cloth or padded bag, at room temperature. |
| Never | Leave it in sunlight, in a car, near a heater, cooler or AC vent, or on a cold window sill. Never let others play it without cleaning (and preferably not at all). |
| Seasons | When the weather changes sharply (for example the start of winter or of the dry season), play shorter sessions for a few days. Bamboo cracks most often when it dries or warms quickly. |

**Oiling (Disputed):** some makers recommend lightly oiling the inside of the flute occasionally; others advise against it, or say their finish does not need it. **Follow the advice of the maker of your flute.** If you have no instructions, ask the seller. If you cannot get an answer, do not oil it during your first months. A properly stored flute will not be harmed by going un-oiled for a short time.

### Step 4: Choose your side (5 min)

Most players hold the flute pointing to the **right**, with the left hand nearer the mouth on holes 1-3 and the right hand on holes 4-6. Many great players hold it to the **left**. Both are correct (Fact).

Try each way for 30 seconds while holding the flute without blowing. **Recommendation:** if neither feels clearly better, choose right-pointing, because most video teachers in the [Resource Library](../02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md) use it and copying them will be easier. **Once you choose, never switch.** Write your choice in your practice log.

### Step 5: Set up your tools (20 min)

| Tool | What it does | Day-0 choice |
|---|---|---|
| Drone (tanpura/shruti box) | Plays Sa continuously so you can hear whether you are in tune | [R-A01](../02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md#71-drone-tanpura--shruti-box) in a browser. Set it to **C**. Phone apps: R-A02, R-A03, R-A05 (iOS); on Android, see the note in the library. |
| Tuner | Shows which note you are playing and how far off you are | Any chromatic tuner app (R-A10, R-A11, R-A12) |
| Metronome | A steady click to keep time | Usually built into tuner apps |
| Recorder | Records you so you can listen back | Your phone's voice recorder. Name files by date: `2026-10-30-sa.m4a` |

Test each one now. Play the drone and listen for 1 minute: that low, humming sound is **Sa**, your home note. You will hear it every day.

### Step 6: The tuner check (5 min, only if you can already make a sound)

1. Open the tuner. Make sure it is a **chromatic** tuner (it shows all note names, not only guitar strings).
2. Close the top three holes (`XXX OOO`) and blow gently, as described in [L04](../lessons/L04-first-sound.md).
3. Read the display. **Expected:** the note **C**. If the tuner shows octave numbers, it should say **C5**, at about **523 Hz**.
4. A reading of C anywhere between -30 and +30 cents is normal for a beginner. Cents are hundredths of a semitone; [L07](L07-long-tones-tuner-and-drone.md) explains them.
5. Write in your log: "Sa = C5, reading ___ cents".

If you see C#, B or something else entirely, you are probably blowing too hard or too softly. Try again more gently. If it stays wrong, your flute may be a different key than labelled. See [Conventions section 7](../05-CONVENTIONS-AND-NOTATION-KEY.md#7-if-your-flute-is-different).

## 5. Diagrams

```
  cork/stopper end (closed)                                         open end
  |                                                                       |
  [==(o)========[1]===[2]===[3]=========[4]===[5]===[6]======================]
       ^          ^     ^     ^           ^     ^     ^
   blowing      finger holes 1-3       finger holes 4-6
     hole       (top hand)              (bottom hand)

  Holes are numbered from the blowing-hole end.
  Bindings (thread wraps), if present, sit between holes or near the ends.
  Nodes are the raised rings of the bamboo; they are natural, not damage.
```

Your flute, measured: about 48 cm (19 in) long. Sa = holes 1-2-3 closed = `XXX OOO`.

## 7. Guided practice

Today is about setup, not practice. The only "practice" is this 10-minute listening task:

1. Play the C drone (R-A01) for 3 minutes. Hum along quietly with it, matching its lowest pitch.
2. Listen to 7 minutes of a bansuri recording (R-L02).

## 8. Independent exercises

1. Draw your flute from memory and label all the parts. Check your drawing against the diagram above.
2. Write your care routine on a card and keep it in the flute bag.
3. Create your practice log (the template is in [L01](L01-how-to-practise.md)) and make the first entry.

## 9. Common mistakes and corrections

| Mistake | How you notice | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Pushing the cork "to see if it moves" | Sa reads wrong on the tuner; the tone changes | Do not touch it. If it has moved, ask the maker. See [TS-12](../troubleshooting/TS-12-flute-damage-and-care.md). |
| Leaving the flute in a hot car or in sun | A crack appears days later | Always store it in its bag at room temperature |
| Switching holding side after a few weeks | Your finger habits keep fighting you | Decide today and write it down |
| Using a guitar tuner | Only E A D G B E are shown | Use a chromatic tuner |
| Drone set to the wrong note | Your Sa never "fits" the drone | Check that the app says C (or Sa = C) |

## 10. Quiz

1. Which end of the flute is closed?
2. Which hole is "hole 1"?
3. Name two things that can crack a bamboo flute.
4. Should you oil your flute?
5. What note and octave should Sa read on the tuner?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answer key</summary>

1. The end near the blowing hole (the cork/stopper end).
2. The finger hole nearest the blowing hole.
3. Any two of: heat (sun, car, heater), rapid drying or temperature change, impact, AC vents.
4. Only if your maker recommends it. Follow the maker's instructions; if there are none, don't oil in the first months.
5. C, specifically C5 (about 523 Hz), with the top 3 holes closed.

</details>
