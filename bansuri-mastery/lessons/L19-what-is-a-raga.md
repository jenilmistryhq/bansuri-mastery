## 3. Simple explanation

A **scale** is just a set of notes. A **raga** is a set of notes **plus rules and habits**
about how to use them: which notes to stress, which to touch lightly, which phrases are
typical, which way to move, and which ornaments to use. Musicians often compare a raga to a
**person**: two people can wear the same clothes (scale) but walk, talk and behave
differently. Ragas Bhupali and Deshkar use exactly the same five notes and still sound like
two different ragas.

**Fact:** a raga must be learned mostly by **listening**, because many of its rules cannot be
written precisely.

## 4. The vocabulary of a raga

| Term | Meaning | Example (Bhupali) |
|---|---|---|
| **Aroh** | The ascending form (how the raga goes up) | `S R G P D S'` |
| **Avroh** | The descending form | `S' D P G R S` |
| **Varjit** | Notes that are omitted | Ma and Ni |
| **Jati** | Number of notes in aroh / avroh: **audav** = 5, **shadav** = 6, **sampurna** = 7 | Audav-audav |
| **Vadi** | The most important note (most emphasised) | Ga |
| **Samvadi** | The second most important note, usually a 4th or 5th away from the vadi | Dha |
| **Pakad** | A short "catch phrase" that identifies the raga | `G R S .D S R G, P G D P G R S` (versions vary) |
| **Chalan** | The typical movement or path through the raga, longer than pakad | See raga card |
| **Nyas** | Notes where phrases rest | Ga, Pa, Sa (and Dha) |
| **Thaat** | The parent scale family | Kalyan |
| **Time** | Traditional time of day for performance | Evening (first part of night) |

## 5. Thaats: the 10 parent scales (THY-07)

V. N. Bhatkhande (early 20th century) grouped ragas into **10 thaats**. A thaat is a 7-note
scale, written ascending, and has no rules of its own: it is a **filing system**.

| Thaat | Notes | Western mode (approx.) | Example ragas |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bilawal | `S R G m P D N` | Ionian (major) | Bilawal, Durga, Pahadi |
| Kalyan | `S R G M P D N` | Lydian | Yaman, Bhupali |
| Khamaj | `S R G m P D n` | Mixolydian | Khamaj, Des |
| Kafi | `S R g m P D n` | Dorian | Kafi, Bhimpalasi, Bageshri |
| Asavari | `S R g m P d n` | Aeolian (natural minor) | Asavari, Jaunpuri, Darbari Kanada |
| Bhairavi | `S r g m P d n` | Phrygian | Bhairavi, Malkauns |
| Bhairav | `S r G m P d N` | (no common mode) | Bhairav, Ahir Bhairav |
| Poorvi | `S r G M P d N` | (none) | Poorvi, Puriya Dhanashri |
| Marwa | `S r G M P D N` | (none) | Marwa, Sohni |
| Todi | `S r g M P d N` | (none) | Todi, Multani |

**Bansuri note:** with Sa = C on your flute, **Kalyan thaat needs no half-holes at all**.
Bilawal needs one (`m`). Bhairavi needs five. This is why the curriculum teaches ragas in the
order Bhupali, Yaman, Durga, Khamaj... ([D12](../knowledge-tree/D12-RAG-raga.md)).

**Disputed:** the thaat system is a convenience, not a law. **32** seven-note scales of this
kind are mathematically possible, and some ragas (for example Jaijaiwanti, Kirwani,
Charukesi) do not fit any of the 10 neatly. Some ragas are placed in different thaats by
different authorities ([R-T08](../02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md#5-theory-and-raga-references)).

## 6. Time and mood (RAG-14)

**Fact (tradition):** many ragas are associated with a time of day (prahar, a 3-hour part of
the day or night) or season, and with a mood (**rasa**): devotion, longing, peace, joy.

**Disputed:** sources disagree on exact times. Yaman is "first quarter of the night"
(Wikipedia), "about 9 pm to midnight" (attributed to The Raga Guide) or "19:00-22:00" (ITC SRA).
Some musicians and scholars treat time rules as aesthetic tradition, others as essential. Many
recordings and concerts ignore them. **Recommendation:** know the tradition, respect it in
performance when practical, and practise any raga at any time you need to.

## 7. Intonation and shruti (PIT-06 introduction)

Indian theory describes **22 shrutis**: finer pitch positions than the 12 notes. In practice,
the "same" note can be placed slightly differently in different ragas (for example komal Re in
Bhairav is often described as lower than in some other ragas, and is played with a slow
oscillation). **Disputed:** scholars disagree on the exact values and even on how the 22
shrutis should be interpreted. **What this means for you:** learn each raga's notes by
**listening to good recordings**, not only by tuner readings.

## 8. How to listen to a raga performance (RAG-01)

A typical instrumental raga performance moves through stages (details in [L31](L31-alap-and-swar-vistar.md) and [L37](L37-full-raga-presentation-and-independence.md)):

| Stage | What it sounds like | How to recognise it |
|---|---|---|
| **Alap** | Slow, no tabla, no steady beat, notes explored one by one | Only flute and drone |
| **Jod** | A steady pulse appears, still no tabla | Rhythmic but no drums |
| **Jhala** (in jod section, mainly on string instruments; flute players use equivalents) | Fast and exciting | Fast pulse, still no tabla |
| **Gat / bandish** | A fixed composition in a taal, tabla joins | Tabla starts; a phrase returns again and again |
| **Taans / improvisation** | Fast runs and variations returning to the composition | Speed rises, tabla answers |
| **Ending** | Often a tihai (phrase played 3 times landing on sam) | Three repeats, then a final beat |

**Listening exercise:** play a 10-20 minute performance from [R-L01](../02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md#9-listening-library) or
[R-L02](../02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md#9-listening-library) (both Yaman) and write timestamps for each stage you hear.

## 12. Common mistakes and corrections

| Mistake | Correction |
|---|---|
| Treating a raga as "just a scale" and playing notes in any order | Learn pakad and chalan; copy recorded phrases |
| Believing the thaat is the raga | Thaat = family; raga = individual |
| Learning raga intonation only from a tuner | Listen to masters; use the drone |
| Skipping listening because "I only want to play" | Listening is practice in this tradition ([L01](L01-how-to-practise.md)) |

## 13. Quiz

1. What is the difference between a scale and a raga?
2. What does "varjit" mean? Which notes are varjit in Bhupali?
3. What is the jati of a raga with 5 notes up and 7 down?
4. Write Kafi thaat.
5. Which thaat needs no half-holes on your flute?
6. Give one reason the thaat system is disputed.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Answer key</summary>

1. A scale is a set of notes; a raga adds rules: movement, emphasis (vadi/samvadi), characteristic phrases, ornaments, mood/time.
2. Omitted notes; Ma and Ni.
3. Audav-sampurna.
4. `S R g m P D n`.
5. Kalyan (`S R G M P D N`).
6. Some ragas fit no thaat (e.g. Jaijaiwanti, Kirwani); 32 scales are possible; authorities sometimes assign ragas differently.

</details>
