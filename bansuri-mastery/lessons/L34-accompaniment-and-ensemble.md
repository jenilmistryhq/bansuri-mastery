## 1. Learning objectives

1. Make drone and tabla loops your normal practice setting (ENS-01).
2. Play along with recordings, matching pitch, rhythm and phrasing (ENS-02).
3. Accompany a singer: match their Sa, follow them, fill gaps, never cover the words (ENS-03, IMP-06).
4. Play duets and in small groups: agree tempo, cue, balance (ENS-04).
5. Work with a live tabla player in a gat (ENS-05).
6. Play a simple harmony line (ENS-06, optional).

## 2. Prerequisites

You can play melodies in time with a loop, find a song's Sa, move Sa or pitch-shift, and
improvise short phrases.

## 3. Simple explanation

Playing alone and playing with others are different skills. Alone, you control everything.
With others, you must **listen more than you play**. The three rules of an accompanist:

1. **Same Sa, same pulse.** Nothing else matters if these are wrong.
2. **The soloist leads.** You follow their tempo, their phrasing, their changes.
3. **Space.** Fill gaps; do not fill everything. Silence is a valid accompaniment.

## 4. Step-by-step instructions

### Part A. Machines first (ENS-01, ENS-02)

**Step 1.** From Phase 2, every session uses a drone or a tabla loop for at least 80% of the
playing time. Log this in the tracker.

**Step 2. Play-along ladder.** Choose a song you have already learned by ear ([L18](L18-song-learning-system.md)).

1. Pitch-shift it to your Sa if needed ([L26](L26-keys-transposition-and-limits.md)).
2. Play along at 0.75x speed (without pitch change).
3. Then at full speed.
4. Record yourself together with the track. Listen for: pitch clashes, notes early or late,
   phrase endings that do not match.

Repeat with 3 songs (ENS-02).

### Part B. Accompanying a singer (ENS-03, IMP-06)

**Step 3. Match the singer's Sa.** Ask which key they sing in. Indian singers and harmonium
players often name keys by harmonium keys, for example "safed 1" (first white key) or
"kali 2" (second black key). **Commonly used mapping (verify with the musician):**

| Name | Pitch | | Name | Pitch |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Safed 1 | C | | Kali 3 | F# |
| Kali 1 | C# | | Safed 5 | G |
| Safed 2 | D | | Kali 4 | G# |
| Kali 2 | D# | | Safed 6 | A |
| Safed 3 | E | | Kali 5 | A# |
| Safed 4 | F | | Safed 7 | B |

> **Fact:** the table matches the safed/kali mapping given by Swarganga ("Patti (Scale)"), but
> not everyone counts this way: Sharda Music names Sa keys by their position on the harmonium,
> for example "5th Black" for A# and "8th White" for C (Verified: swarganga.org; sharda.org).
> Always confirm by playing Sa together with the singer or harmonium before you start.

If the singer's Sa is C, use your normal fingering. If it is G, use Pa as Sa. Otherwise use the
decision table in [L26](L26-keys-transposition-and-limits.md).

**Step 4. Learn the song's structure.** Know the mukhda, antara and the gaps between lines.
Mark where the singer breathes and where nobody sings.

**Step 5. Four roles you can play:**

| Role | When | What to play |
|---|---|---|
| Introduction | Before the singer starts | The mukhda melody or a short alap in the song's scale |
| Fill | Gaps between sung lines | A short echo of the last sung phrase, or a 2-4 beat answer (call and response, [L29](L29-improvisation-foundations.md)) |
| Interlude | Instrumental breaks between verses | The original interlude melody or your own variation |
| Shadow | Rarely, during singing | Long soft notes (often Sa or Pa) under the voice |

**Step 6. Rules for fills (IMP-06).**

- Start a fill only after the singer has finished the line.
- Stop **before** the singer starts again. Leave a breath of silence.
- Use notes from the song's scale; repeat the singer's last phrase if unsure.
- Play softer than the singer.

**Step 7. Practise with a recorded vocal.** Use a song recording: play only in the gaps. Record
yourself with the track and ask: did I ever cover a word?

**Step 8. Accompany a live singer** (a friend or family member). Ask them afterwards: "Was I too
loud? Did I get in your way? Did the fills help?" (ENS-03 test: the singer judges.)

### Part C. Duets and ensembles (ENS-04)

**Step 9.** Before playing:

- Agree Sa (tune together on Sa and Pa).
- Agree tempo (one person counts in, for example "1, 2, 3, 4").
- Agree structure (who plays the melody where, how it ends).

**Step 10. Cues.** Learn simple signals: a nod for "start", raising the flute for "last time",
eye contact before the final note. Practise endings until they are together.

**Step 11. Balance.** If you cannot hear the other musician, you are too loud.

### Part D. Working with a live tabla player (ENS-05)

**Step 12.** Before you play:

1. Tell the tabla player the tala and the starting tempo (or play the mukhda for them).
2. Tell them the start beat of your composition.
3. Agree an ending: usually a tihai landing on sam, then a final note.

**Step 13.** During the performance: return to the mukhda after each improvisation so the
tabla player can follow you; signal tempo increases with your playing (and a glance); when the
tabla player plays a solo section, keep the lehra (the repeated melody of the composition) steady
and do not speed up.

### Part E. Harmony (ENS-06, optional)

**Step 14.** In Western or fusion settings you may play a second part. The simplest harmony is a
line that moves **in parallel thirds** above or below the melody, using notes of the scale:

```
Melody:   S  R  G  m  P  -
Harmony:  G  m  P  D  N  -   (a third above, in Bilawal/major)
```

Check every harmony note sounds pleasant with the melody; adjust where it clashes. Drone notes
(long Sa or Pa) are an easy alternative.

## 7. Guided practice

| Phase | Minutes | Task |
|---|---|---|
| P2-P3 | Whole session | Drone or loop by default |
| P3 | 10 | Play-along ladder, one song per week |
| P4 | 10 | Fills on a recorded vocal; one live session per month with a singer if possible |
| P4-P5 | Occasional | Duet with another instrumentalist |
| P5 | Occasional | Rehearsal with a tabla player |

## 8. Independent exercises

1. Make a "structure map" (intro, lines, gaps, interludes, with timestamps) of 3 songs.
2. Write 4 different fills for the same gap in one song.
3. Write a harmony in thirds for an 8-bar melody from [EX-MEL](../exercises/EX-MEL-practice-melodies.md).

## 9. Common mistakes and corrections

| Mistake | Correction |
|---|---|
| Playing during the words | Fills only in gaps; stop before the next line |
| Wrong Sa | Tune together before starting; confirm the key name |
| Rushing ahead of the singer | Listen to the singer, not your own fingers; the soloist leads ([TS-09](../troubleshooting/TS-09-rhythm-and-tempo.md)) |
| Too loud | Play softer than the singer; check on a recording |
| Ending at different times | Rehearse the ending and agree a cue |
| Getting lost when the tabla player improvises | Keep the lehra steady; count cycles; listen for sam |

## 10. Quiz

1. What are the three rules of an accompanist?
2. A singer says "kali 3". What is their Sa, and how would you play it on your C flute?
3. When does a fill start and stop?
4. What three things should you agree with a tabla player before playing?

<details markdown="1"><summary>Answer key</summary>

1. Same Sa and pulse; the soloist leads; leave space.
2. F# (by the common mapping; confirm with the singer). Options: pitch-shift is not possible live, so move Sa (tivra Ma as Sa needs many half-holes; often better to use another flute) or ask the singer if another key is possible.
3. After the singer finishes a line; before the singer starts the next.
4. Tala and tempo, start beat of the composition, how to end (tihai and final note).

</details>

## 11. Mastery criteria

- [ ] ENS-01: drone or loop in 80% of sessions (tracker).
- [ ] ENS-02: 3 clean play-along recordings.
- [ ] ENS-03 / IMP-06: accompany a singer (live or recorded vocal) for a full song, judged appropriate by the singer.
- [ ] ENS-04: one duet performance, recorded.
- [ ] ENS-05: perform a gat with a tabla player, with clean starts and ending.
- [ ] ENS-06 (optional): harmony line for an 8-bar melody.
