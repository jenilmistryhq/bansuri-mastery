## 1. Learning objectives

- Use the standard daily session template (PRA-01).
- Practise deliberately: slowly, in small chunks, with a metronome ladder and the "3 clean in a row" rule (PRA-02).
- Review old material on a spaced schedule (PRA-03).
- Keep a practice log and a listening log (PRF-03, PRA-07).
- Warm up, take breaks and stop safely (BOD-06, BOD-07).
- Set process goals and get through plateaus by changing your approach (PRA-06).

## 2. Prerequisites

[L00](L00-day-0-your-flute.md): your tools are set up.

## 3. Simple explanation

Practice does not mean "playing the flute for an hour". It means **changing what you can do**. Playing what you already know feels pleasant but improves you very little. Improvement comes from working at the edge of your ability: slowly, attentively, and with feedback (a tuner, a metronome, a recording).

Three facts drive the whole design of this curriculum:

1. **Short, focused, daily practice beats long, occasional practice** (Recommendation, consistent with learning research). Six days of 45 minutes beats one 5-hour Sunday.
2. **Slow practice builds fast playing.** Your fingers learn exactly what you repeat. If you repeat mistakes at speed, you learn the mistakes.
3. **Forgetting is normal.** You keep skills by reviewing them on a schedule, not by hoping.

## 4. Step-by-step instructions

### 4.1 The standard daily session (45-60 minutes)

| Block | Minutes (45 / 60) | What | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Warm-up | 3 / 5 | Body warm-up (below) and 5 low breaths | Always, even on short days |
| B. Tone | 8 / 10 | Long tones with drone and tuner | The heart of bansuri practice |
| C. Technique | 10 / 12 | New notes, alankars, articulation | The "new skill" block |
| | **break 2 min** | Put the flute down, stand, drink water | Rest your lips |
| D. Repertoire | 10 / 15 | Melodies, songs, raga material | Includes spaced review |
| E. Ear / theory / rhythm | 8 / 10 | Ear drills, clapping, notation (often without the flute) | Lets your lips rest |
| F. Cool-down and log | 4 / 5 | Play one slow, easy, beautiful thing; write the log | End on something you enjoy |

Each phase file in [phases/](../phases/README.md) tells you what goes into each block on each day.

**Short day rule:** on a day with only 20 minutes, do A, B and a short C. Tone and a little technique are worth more than nothing. Do not skip the warm-up.

### 4.2 Warm-up routine (3-5 min)

1. Shoulder rolls: 5 backward, 5 forward.
2. Neck: tilt your ear slowly toward each shoulder, 3 times each side. No circles.
3. Wrists: gently circle them 5 times each way.
4. Fingers: spread them wide, then make a loose fist, 5 times. Shake out your hands.
5. Breathing: 5 slow, low breaths ([L03](L03-breathing-basics.md)).
6. Tension scan: jaw loose, shoulders down, lips relaxed ([L02](L02-posture-and-holding.md)).

### 4.3 Deliberate practice: the core rules

1. **One target at a time.** Before you play, say what you are fixing ("clean change from Re to Ga").
2. **Shrink the problem.** If a 16-note phrase has one bad change, practise only those 2 notes, then 4, then the whole phrase.
3. **Slow enough to be correct.** If you make the same mistake twice, slow down.
4. **The "3 clean in a row" rule.** A chunk is "done" for today only when you play it correctly **3 times in a row**. A mistake resets the count to zero.
5. **Listen, don't just play.** After each attempt, ask: was the tone clear? Was it in tune? Was it in time?
6. **Stop before you get sloppy.** Tired lips and fingers practise mistakes.

### 4.4 The metronome ladder

Use this for any passage you want to play faster.

1. Find your **clean tempo**: the speed at which you can play the passage 3 times in a row cleanly. Say it is 60 BPM (BPM = beats per minute, explained in [L06](L06-pulse-and-tempo.md)).
2. Increase the tempo by 4 BPM (to 64). Play it 3 clean times in a row.
3. Keep stepping up by 4 until you fail twice at a tempo.
4. Drop back 8 BPM and finish with 3 clean repetitions there.
5. Log your highest clean tempo. Tomorrow, start 8 BPM below it.

### 4.5 Spaced review

Material you have "finished" goes on the review schedule. Review it:

| Review | When |
|---|---|
| 1 | Next day |
| 2 | 3 days later |
| 3 | 1 week later |
| 4 | 2 weeks later |
| 5+ | Monthly |

If a review goes badly, restart that item's schedule from review 1. Track this in the Review column of the [Progress Tracker](../03-PROGRESS-TRACKER.md). Review happens in block D (repertoire) or block C (technique).

**Lighter days:** every 7th day is a lighter review day (30-40 minutes, no new material). Every 4th week, make that review day **cumulative**: play one item from every lesson you have completed so far (a long tone, an alankar, a melody, a raga phrase), score the weakest one with the rubric, and add it to next week's practice. The week before each milestone is also a review week (see [projects/README](../projects/README.md)).

### 4.6 Rest and safety (BOD-07)

- Take a 2-minute break at least every 15-20 minutes of playing.
- Stop immediately if you feel dizzy, sharp pain, numbness or tingling. See [TS-07](../troubleshooting/TS-07-breath-dizziness-and-endurance.md) and [TS-08](../troubleshooting/TS-08-body-pain-and-tension.md).
- Tired, buzzing lips mean it's time for the non-flute block E or the end of the session.
- One full rest day per week is allowed and healthy. Listening still counts.

### 4.7 Returning after a break (Recommendation)

Illness, travel and busy weeks happen. Your lips and breath lose condition faster than your
fingers or your knowledge, so come back gradually:

| Time away | How to restart |
|---|---|
| 1-3 days | Continue where you stopped. No catch-up sessions. |
| 4-14 days | First 2-3 sessions: 30 min, mostly long tones and familiar pieces at 80% of your previous tempo. Then return to the plan. |
| 2-6 weeks | Spend one week on a "re-entry" plan: long tones, your two easiest alankars, three repertoire pieces. Re-test the most recent milestone you passed before you continue. If it fails, use that milestone's remediation table. |
| More than 6 weeks | Go back to the start of the current phase for one week, then re-test the last milestone. Expect to recover in about a quarter to a half of the time you were away (Uncertain: this varies a lot between people). |

After any illness affecting the chest, throat or lips, check with a doctor before playing.
Then keep sessions short until breathing feels normal.

### 4.8 Goals and plateaus (PRA-06)

**Set process goals, not only outcome goals.** "Play Raga Bhupali well" is an outcome: you can't practise it directly. A process goal is something you can do and check today: "10 minutes of long tones on `.N` and `R`, three 15-second holds within +/-15 cents" or "`S R G R` at 72 BPM, 3 clean in a row". Keep one outcome goal per month (usually the next milestone) and break it into process goals for each week (Recommendation).

**Plateaus are normal (Fact for most learners).** Skill tends to grow in steps: weeks where nothing seems to change, then a sudden jump. Often the improvement is real but too gradual to notice from day to day.

**When you feel stuck for 2 weeks or more, change the approach, not only the effort.** More hours of the same practice rarely breaks a plateau. Instead:

1. **Check the evidence.** Compare a recording from 4 weeks ago with one from today, same exercise ([L08](L08-recording-and-self-assessment.md)). There is often a difference you did not notice.
2. **Check the difficulty.** Aim for tasks you get right about 70-80% of the time. Too easy means no growth; too hard means no success.
3. **Check the balance.** Does your log show tone, technique, repertoire and ear work, or mostly one thing?
4. **Look for a hidden problem.** A leak or tension can block progress. Diagnose it ([L08](L08-recording-and-self-assessment.md), [troubleshooting](../troubleshooting/README.md)).
5. **Change one thing for 2 weeks:** a new exercise for the same skill, a different tempo, a new piece, or a rest day you were skipping.

**Monthly reflection (5 minutes, last day of each month).** Add this to your log:

```
Month: ______
Outcome goal this month: ______        Reached? Y / partly / N
Best recording vs. last month's: what changed? ______
Where am I stuck? ______
What I will change (one thing): ______
Outcome goal for next month: ______
```

More help: [TS-11](../troubleshooting/TS-11-practice-plateaus-and-motivation.md).

## 5. Templates

### Practice log (copy one per day)

```
Date: 2026-__-__        Minutes: __       Energy (1-5): __
Warm-up done: Y/N
Tone:        notes ______  longest clean hold ___ s  tuner +/- ___ cents
Technique:   item ______   clean tempo ___ BPM
Repertoire:  pieces ______ (new / review #__)
Ear/theory:  ______        score ___/___
Recording:   file name ______  rubric: T_ P_ R_ Tr_ E_  (see L08)
Problem of the day: ____________________
Tomorrow's first target: ____________________
```

### Listening log (4+ entries per week)

```
Date | Artist / piece | Minutes | One thing I noticed (tone, ornament, rhythm, mood)
```

Listen with a purpose. One question per session is enough: "Where does the flute breathe?" or "Which note does it keep returning to?"

This is how Indian music has always been taught. In the **guru-shishya parampara** (the traditional teacher-to-student way of learning), students learn mostly by listening to the teacher and imitating, long before they read notation. Your listening log is your version of sitting with a guru.

## 7. Guided practice (today)

1. Do the warm-up routine once (5 min).
2. Set the metronome to 60 BPM and clap along for 1 minute. You'll use the metronome constantly.
3. Write your first log entry and your first listening log entry (10 min of listening).

## 8. Independent exercises

1. Decide your daily practice time slot and put it in your calendar.
2. Print or copy the log template (paper or a notes app).
3. Explain the metronome ladder in your own words, as if to a friend.

## 9. Common mistakes and corrections

| Mistake | How you notice | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Playing only what you already know | Log shows the same pieces, no new BPM records | Always start block C with the new item |
| Practising too fast | The same mistake keeps coming back | Metronome ladder; drop 8 BPM |
| Skipping the warm-up | Stiff fingers, tension, early fatigue | It takes 3 minutes; never skip it |
| Long sessions without breaks | Lips buzz, tone gets airy after 20 min | Breaks every 15-20 min |
| No log | You can't tell whether you are improving | Log takes 2 minutes; do it at the end of every session |
| Quitting during a plateau | "I'm not getting better" | Plateaus are normal: see [TS-11](../troubleshooting/TS-11-practice-plateaus-and-motivation.md) |

## 10. Quiz

1. Name the six blocks of the standard session.
2. What resets your "3 clean in a row" count?
3. You reached 72 BPM cleanly today. Where do you start tomorrow?
4. You learned a melody on Monday. When are its first three reviews?
5. List three signs that you should stop practising.
6. Your clean tempo on an alankar has been stuck at 80 BPM for three weeks, even though you practise it more. What should you do?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answer key</summary>

1. Warm-up, Tone, Technique, Repertoire, Ear/theory/rhythm, Cool-down and log.
2. Any mistake.
3. 64 BPM (8 below your best), then climb again.
4. Tuesday (next day), Friday (3 days later), and the following Friday (1 week later).
5. Any three of: dizziness, pain, numbness or tingling, lips too tired for a clear tone, repeated sloppy mistakes.
6. Treat it as a normal plateau and change the approach, not only the effort: compare old and new recordings, check the difficulty and balance, look for a hidden problem such as tension or a leak, and change one thing for 2 weeks (PRA-06).

</details>

## 11. Mastery criteria

- [ ] Review column in the tracker kept up to date (PRA-03).
- [ ] Practice log kept on 5+ days per week (PRF-03).
- [ ] Listening log has 4+ entries per week (PRA-07).
