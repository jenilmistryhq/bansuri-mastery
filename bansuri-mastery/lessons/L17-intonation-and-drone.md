## 1. Learning objectives

- Hear when a note **locks** with the drone and when it **beats** (wavers) (PIT-03).
- Raise or lower a note's pitch with **flute angle** and **air speed** (PIT-04).
- Know why the flute's pitch changes with temperature and warm-up (INS-09).
- Play **louder and softer without changing pitch** (TON-08, BRE-05).
- Begin to keep notes in tune across octaves (PIT-07).

## 2. Prerequisites

- Tuner and drone basics ([L07](L07-long-tones-tuner-and-drone.md)).
- Full middle octave including `m` and `M` ([L14](L14-tivra-ma-and-madhya-pa-dha-ni.md), [L16](L16-shuddha-ma-and-half-holing.md)).

## 3. Simple explanation

**Intonation** means playing each note at exactly the right pitch. On a bamboo flute nothing is
fixed: the player controls the pitch all the time.

### 3.1 Why the drone, not only the tuner

A tuner tells you "C, 8 cents flat". A **drone** (tanpura or shruti box holding Sa and Pa)
lets you **hear** the relationship between your note and Sa, which is how Indian music is
actually heard. When two pitches are slightly different, they create a slow pulsing called
**beating** ("wah-wah-wah"). As you get closer, the beating slows; when it disappears, the
note **locks** and sounds calm and rich.

**Recommendation:** use the tuner to **check**, the drone to **listen**. Within a few months,
your ear should lead and the tuner only confirm.

**Disputed (advanced):** Indian music does not always use the same tuning as a Western
tuner (equal temperament). Some notes, especially Ga and Dha, may sound best a few cents
away from the tuner's centre when heard against Sa. Trust a stable lock with the drone over
a tuner reading of exactly 0. You will learn more about **shruti** in [L19](L19-what-is-a-raga.md).

### 3.2 Three ways to move the pitch (PIT-04)

| Action | Effect on pitch |
|---|---|
| Roll the flute **toward** you (covering more of the blowing hole), or point air more downward | Lower (flatter) |
| Roll the flute **away** from you (uncovering more), or aim air more across | Higher (sharper) |
| Faster air | Higher |
| Slower air | Lower |
| Partly shading an open hole below the played note with a finger | Slightly lower (fine adjustment) |

### 3.3 Why pitch drifts (INS-09)

- **Cold flute = flat.** As your breath warms the bamboo, pitch rises. Play 5 minutes before
  tuning carefully.
- **Louder = sharper, softer = flatter** (because louder means faster air).
- **Taar notes tend to go sharp**, mandra notes tend to go flat.

## 4. Step-by-step instructions

### 4.1 Hearing beats

1. Start a drone on C (Sa and Pa) at a moderate volume ([R-A01](../02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md#71-drone-tanpura--shruti-box)).
2. Play Sa and deliberately roll the flute toward you so it goes about 20 cents flat (check
   tuner). Listen for the "wah-wah".
3. Slowly roll back. The beats slow down, then stop. That is the lock.
4. Repeat going sharp.

### 4.2 Pitch bend drill (PIT-04)

Hold Sa. Bend it 20 cents flat, back to centre, 20 cents sharp, back to centre. Use angle
first, then air speed. Repeat on Pa (`P`, register 2) and Ga.

### 4.3 Dynamics without pitch change (TON-08, BRE-05)

1. Hold Sa softly, centred on the tuner.
2. Over 4 seconds, gradually play louder (more air speed). The pitch will try to rise:
   **roll the flute toward you a tiny amount** as you get louder to keep it centred.
3. Over 4 seconds, get softer. The pitch will try to fall: **roll slightly away**.
4. Watch the tuner. A graph-type tuner ([R-A10](../02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md#72-tuner-metronome-recording-slow-down)) shows this clearly.

### 4.4 Octave checking (PIT-07 introduction)

Play `S` then `S'`, `.P` then `P`. Both notes of each pair should give the same tuner reading in
cents. Typical fault: `S'` sharp. Fix by slowing the air and rolling in very slightly.

## 7. Guided practice (10-12 minutes per day, inside the tone block)

| Minutes | Exercise |
|---|---|
| 3 | Drone long tones: `S`, `P`, `G`, `m`, `D` held 8 s each; find the lock before the tuner confirms |
| 2 | Pitch bend drill on Sa and Pa (4.2) |
| 3 | Swells on Sa and Ga: soft-loud-soft in 8 s, pitch within +/-15 cents (4.3) |
| 2 | Octave pairs `S S'`, `.P P`, `.D D` x 3 each with tuner |
| 2 | Slow scale with drone, stopping on any note that beats until it locks |

## 8. Independent exercises

1. **Warm-up log (INS-09):** on 3 days, tune Sa in the first minute (cold) and again after
   10 minutes. Write both readings. Explain the difference.
2. **Blind lock:** cover the tuner. Play each note of `S R G m P D N S'` against the drone until it
   feels locked, then uncover and check. Score how many were within +/-15 cents.
3. Record one slow melody (Twinkle) with the drone and listen for sour notes. Mark them in
   the notation.

## 9. Common mistakes and corrections

| Mistake | Correction | Guide |
|---|---|---|
| Staring at the tuner, not listening | Close eyes for the first 3 s of each note | [TS-05](../troubleshooting/TS-05-pitch-out-of-tune.md) |
| Drone too loud, cannot hear yourself | Drone a bit quieter than your flute | [TS-05](../troubleshooting/TS-05-pitch-out-of-tune.md) |
| Correcting pitch only with the lips (tone suffers) | Angle and air first; keep the embouchure shape | [TS-01](../troubleshooting/TS-01-no-sound-or-airy-tone.md) |
| Getting sharp on every loud note | Practise swells daily (4.3) | [TS-05](../troubleshooting/TS-05-pitch-out-of-tune.md) |
| Taar notes sharp | Less air speed, roll in slightly | [TS-04](../troubleshooting/TS-04-high-notes.md) |

## 10. Quiz

1. What is "beating"?
2. Your Sa is 15 cents sharp. Name two ways to fix it.
3. Why should you warm up before tuning carefully?
4. What happens to pitch when you play louder without compensating?
5. Why might a well-played Ga not read exactly 0 cents on a tuner?

<details markdown="1"><summary>Answer key</summary>

1. A pulsing "wah-wah" heard when two pitches are slightly different.
2. Roll the flute toward you; slow the air (or both).
3. A cold flute plays flat; pitch rises as the bamboo warms.
4. It goes sharp.
5. Tuners use Western equal temperament; Indian intonation against Sa may place some notes a few cents differently (Disputed detail; trust the stable lock with the drone).

</details>

## 11. Mastery criteria

- [ ] PIT-03: hold Sa and Pa against the drone with no audible beating for 8 s.
- [ ] PIT-04: bend Sa 20 cents flat and sharp and return to centre.
- [ ] TON-08 / BRE-05: crescendo-decrescendo on Sa over 8 s within +/-15 cents (recorded).
- [ ] INS-09: warm-up log done and explained.
- [ ] Blind lock score 6/8 or better.
