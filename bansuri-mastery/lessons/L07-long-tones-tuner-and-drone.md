# L07 - Long Tones, Tuner and Drone

**Objectives:** TON-04, TON-05, PIT-02, PIT-03, INS-02, BRE-07 (introduction)
**Time span:** Starts around Day 5; long tones continue every day for your whole musical life

## 1. Learning objectives

- Hold a steady note for 4 s, then 8 s, then 12 s or more (TON-04).
- Reduce air noise so the note is mostly tone (TON-05).
- Read a chromatic tuner: note name, cents, flat or sharp (PIT-02).
- Play with a drone and hear when your note locks with it or beats against it (PIT-03).
- Confirm your flute's Sa pitch and octave (INS-02).
- Begin building endurance toward 20-second tones (BRE-07).

## 2. Prerequisites

A clear Sa ([L04](L04-first-sound.md)); the hiss exercise ([L03](L03-breathing-basics.md)).

## 3. Simple explanation

A **long tone** is a single note held steadily for as long as you comfortably can. It's the most important exercise for wind players because it trains everything at once: breath, embouchure, tone and pitch. Daily practice in Indian music is called **riyaz**, and holding notes steadily against the drone is a core part of it in most teaching traditions: one note, steady, against the drone, every day.

### The tuner (PIT-02)

A **chromatic tuner** listens to your note and shows:

- the **note name** (for example C), sometimes with an **octave number** (C5);
- a needle or bar showing how far you are from the exact pitch, in **cents**. 100 cents = one semitone (the distance from one piano key to the next). 0 is perfectly in tune. Negative (-) means **flat** (too low); positive (+) means **sharp** (too high).

For a beginner, +/-15 cents is good, +/-30 is acceptable, and beyond that the note sounds noticeably out of tune against a drone.

### The drone (PIT-03)

The tuner shows you a number; the drone teaches your **ear**. When your Sa matches the drone's Sa, the two sounds blend into one smooth, still sound. When they are slightly different, you hear a slow **wavering or pulsing** (called "beats"): wah-wah-wah. The closer you get, the slower the wavering, until it stops. **Learning to hear this is more valuable than any tuner** (Recommendation), because ragas use subtle pitch shades that a tuner can't capture ([L17](L17-intonation-and-drone.md)).

## 4. Step-by-step instructions

### Step 1: Confirm Sa (INS-02)

Do the tuner check from [L00 step 6](L00-day-0-your-flute.md). Write down the reading. Expected: **C5** (about 523 Hz). If your relaxed Sa is consistently 20-40 cents flat or sharp, that may be how your flute sits at your breath and room temperature. Note it; [L17](L17-intonation-and-drone.md) covers adjusting.

### Step 2: The basic long tone (TON-04)

1. Sit in posture, tension scan, low breath.
2. Start Sa **softly**, with no tongue: just begin the air (a gentle "hoo").
3. Hold it **steady**: the same volume, the same tone, from start to finish.
4. End **cleanly**: stop the air gently. Don't let the note sag down in pitch or collapse into hiss at the end.
5. Rest for as long as you played. Breathe normally.

**Target progression (Sa first, then Re and Ga once learned in [L09](L09-fingering-sa-re-ga.md), then lower notes from [L11](L11-lower-notes-ni-dha-pa.md)):**

| Stage | Hold | Criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4 s | steady, clear |
| 2 | 8 s | steady, tuner within +/-30 cents |
| 3 | 12 s | tuner within +/-15 cents, no breaks |
| 4 | 20 s (BRE-07) | same, plus the drone does not beat |

### Step 3: Long tone with the tuner (PIT-02)

1. Tuner on, placed where you can see it.
2. Hold Sa for 8 s. Watch whether the needle stays still, drifts or wobbles.
3. Common patterns: the pitch **rises** if the air speeds up toward the end; it **falls** if the air weakens; it **wobbles** if the breath is uneven.

### Step 4: Long tone with the drone (PIT-03)

1. Drone on (C), at a volume about equal to your flute.
2. Hold Sa and **listen** rather than look. Do you hear wavering?
3. Tiny pitch adjustment: roll the flute very slightly in (lower) or out (higher) until the wavering slows and stops. [L17](L17-intonation-and-drone.md) covers this fully.
4. Once Pa is available ([L11](L11-lower-notes-ni-dha-pa.md) for `.P`, [L14](L14-tivra-ma-and-madhya-pa-dha-ni.md) for `P`), do the same with Pa: the drone contains Pa too, so it also locks.

### Step 5: Clarity (TON-05)

Record a long tone. Listen: what share of the sound is tone, and what share is hiss?

- To reduce hiss: make the lip opening **smaller**; use **slightly less** air; find the sweet spot by rolling in small steps.
- Hiss that remains at very soft volume is normal. Aim for a clear core in the sound.

### What you should hear

- A note that sounds like a **straight line**: no wobble, no swelling, no fading.
- Against the drone: a smooth blend with no "wah-wah".
- A tone where the **note is clearly louder than the air noise**.

## 7. Guided practice (block B, 8-10 min daily from now on)

**Week 1 of long tones:**

1. 3 x Sa, 4 s, tuner (rest 4 s between).
2. 3 x Sa, 8 s, drone only (eyes closed).
3. 2 x Sa, as long as steady; log the time.

**Later weeks:** add each new note as you learn it. Rotate: one day tuner-focused, one day drone-focused. Always include Sa and the newest note. The full set is in [EX-TON](../exercises/EX-TON-tone-and-breath-drills.md).

## 8. Independent exercises

1. Hold Sa while watching the tuner, then hold it again with your eyes closed and the drone on. Which is steadier?
2. "Find centre": deliberately roll out until the tuner shows about +20 cents, then roll back to 0. Then -20 and back. (This previews PIT-04.)
3. Record a 12 s Sa and score it with the tone rubric in [L08](L08-recording-and-self-assessment.md).

## 9. Common mistakes and corrections

| Mistake | How you notice | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Starting with a burst of air | Loud "pff" at the start; pitch jumps sharp | Begin softer, like starting a whisper |
| Pitch sags at the end | Needle drifts down | End the note earlier, before the air weakens |
| Wobble | Wavy needle or graph | Low breath; keep lips and flute still; relax the jaw |
| Staring at the tuner all the time | Can't hear the drone | Alternate tuner days and drone days |
| Holding too long, getting dizzy | Light-headed | Rest as long as you played; see [TS-07](../troubleshooting/TS-07-breath-dizziness-and-endurance.md) |
| Airy tone | Recording is mostly hiss | Smaller opening; less air; [TS-01](../troubleshooting/TS-01-no-sound-or-airy-tone.md) |

Pitch problems: [TS-05](../troubleshooting/TS-05-pitch-out-of-tune.md).

## 10. Quiz

1. What does "-20 cents" mean?
2. How many cents are in a semitone?
3. You hear a slow "wah-wah" between your Sa and the drone. What does it mean?
4. Your pitch rises at the end of each long tone. What's the likely cause?
5. How long should you rest after a 12-second long tone?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answer key</summary>

1. Your note is 20 hundredths of a semitone below the target (flat).
2. 100.
3. Your Sa is slightly out of tune with the drone (not yet matching). Adjust until the wavering stops.
4. The air is speeding up toward the end (blowing harder).
5. About 12 seconds, or as long as you played.

</details>

## 11. Mastery criteria

- [ ] Sa held 12 s, tuner within +/-15 cents, no breaks (TON-04). Re and Ga to the same standard after [L09](L09-fingering-sa-re-ga.md).
- [ ] A long tone scored 3 or more on the Tone rubric ([L08](L08-recording-and-self-assessment.md)) (TON-05).
- [ ] Correctly describe a tuner reading; bring a flat note up to within +/-10 cents (PIT-02).
- [ ] Hold Sa against the drone for 8 s with no audible beating (PIT-03).
- [ ] Sa octave and reading logged (INS-02).
