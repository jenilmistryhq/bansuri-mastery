# L10 - Reading and Writing Sargam

**Objectives:** NOT-01, NOT-02, NOT-03 (introduction), BRE-04 (application)
**Time span:** Days 10-25 (5-8 min a day, mostly in block E); NOT-03 is completed in Phase 2

## 1. Learning objectives

- Read sargam letters with octave marks and play them (NOT-01).
- Write down in sargam what you play or sing (NOT-02).
- Read and write **rhythmic** sargam: beats, holds, rests, several notes per beat and division lines (NOT-03).
- Add breath marks to notation (BRE-04).

## 2. Prerequisites

You know the swara names and octaves ([L05](L05-what-is-music-pitch-sa-sargam.md)), durations ([L06](L06-pulse-and-tempo.md)), and can play S R G ([L09](L09-fingering-sa-re-ga.md)).

## 3. Simple explanation

**Notation** is writing music down so you (or anyone) can play it later. Indian music has traditionally been learned by ear from a teacher, and notation is a **memory aid**, not a replacement for listening (Fact about the tradition). Even so, you need it to:

- remember exercises and songs;
- learn from books and websites;
- write down melodies you figure out by ear;
- compose.

This curriculum uses an ASCII version of the standard Indian **Bhatkhande** notation, defined in [Conventions section 4](../05-CONVENTIONS-AND-NOTATION-KEY.md#4-sargam-text-notation-ascii). [L27](L27-bhatkhande-notation.md) shows the original Devanagari forms later.

## 4. Step-by-step instructions

### Step 1: Letters and octaves

| Write | Note |
|---|---|
| `S R G m P D N` | the seven shuddha (natural) notes |
| `.N .D .P` | mandra (lower) octave: dot **before** |
| `S' R'` | taar (upper) octave: apostrophe **after** |

Lowercase letters (r, g, m, d, n) are the "lower versions" of notes (komal, and shuddha Ma). Uppercase `M` is tivra Ma. You'll meet these later; for now, the notes you play are `S R G` and, from [L11](L11-lower-notes-ni-dha-pa.md), `.N .D .P`.

### Step 2: Beats and rhythm (NOT-03)

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| space | separates beats |
| `-` | hold the previous note for this beat |
| `_` | silence for this beat |
| `SR` | two notes in one beat |
| `SRGR` | four notes in one beat |
| `|` | division line (makes reading easier, usually every 4 beats for now) |
| `||` | end of a line or section |
| `,` | breathe here |

Example:

```
S R G - | G R S - , | R G R S | .N S - - ||
```

Read it as: Sa (1 beat), Re (1), Ga (2 beats), Ga, Re, Sa (2 beats), breathe, Re, Ga, Re, Sa, mandra Ni, Sa (3 beats). 16 beats in total.

### Step 3: How to read a new line (NOT-01)

1. **Say** the note names in rhythm with the metronome, without the flute ("Sa, Re, Ga-a...").
2. **Clap** the rhythm while saying the names.
3. **Air-finger** while saying the names.
4. **Play** slowly (60 BPM or less).
5. Mark any trouble spot and practise it alone (deliberate practice, [L01](L01-how-to-practise.md)).

This "say, clap, finger, play" sequence will save you hours.

### Step 4: How to write down what you play (NOT-02)

1. Play or sing a short phrase you made up. Keep it to 4-8 notes.
2. Write the note names in order.
3. Add octave marks.
4. Add the rhythm: decide which notes are 1 beat, which are held (`-`), and which are fast (two per beat).
5. **Check**: play exactly what you wrote. Does it sound like what you meant? If not, fix the notation.

### Step 5: Breath marks (BRE-04)

Before playing any line of 8 beats or longer, pencil in `,` where you will breathe: at the ends of phrases, after long notes and before the next idea. Then play it and breathe only there.

## 5. Practice lines (S R G only, until L11)

```
1)  S  S  R  R  | G  G  R  -  ||
2)  G  R  S  -  | G  R  S  -  ||
3)  S  R  G  R  | S  -  _  _  ||
4)  SR G  SR G  | GR S  -  -  ||
5)  S  -  R  -  | G  -  -  -  , | G  R  S  R  | S  -  -  -  ||
6)  G  G  R  R  | S  R  G  -  , | GR SR G  -  | S  -  -  -  ||
```

## 7. Guided practice (5-8 min daily)

1. Lines 1-6 above: say, clap, finger, play. One or two new lines per day.
2. **Writing:** play a 4-note phrase you invent, write it, then play from your notation. 2 phrases per day.
3. From Week 3: write one 8-beat phrase per day **with rhythm** and breath marks.

## 8. Independent exercises

1. Copy lines 1-6 by hand from memory after playing them.
2. Write the rhythm of "Happy Birthday" as you sing it, using only `S` for every note (rhythm only, no pitches yet), then compare with your memory of the song. (Pitches come in [L11](L11-lower-notes-ni-dha-pa.md) and [L16](L16-shuddha-ma-and-half-holing.md).)
3. Write and play your own 16-beat melody using S, R, G with at least one held note and one two-notes-per-beat.

## 9. Common mistakes and corrections

| Mistake | How you notice | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Playing notes with no rhythm | Everything sounds the same length | Say and clap first, then play |
| Ignoring `-` holds | Line finishes "early" | Count every beat aloud during the hold |
| Confusing `SR` (one beat) with `S R` (two beats) | Wrong total beat count | Count the space-separated groups: each group is one beat |
| Forgetting octave marks when writing | Ambiguous notation | Check every note: lower, middle or upper? |
| Breathing wherever you run out | Broken phrases | Mark breaths **before** playing |

More: [TS-09](../troubleshooting/TS-09-rhythm-and-tempo.md).

## 10. Quiz

1. How many beats are in: `S R G - | SR G _ S ||`?
2. Write: "Sa for 2 beats, then Re and Ga in one beat, then Sa for 1 beat."
3. What does `.N` mean? What does `S'` mean?
4. What are the four steps for reading a new line?
5. Where should you breathe in this line? `S R G - G R S - R G R S S - - -`

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answer key</summary>

1. 8 beats: S, R, G, -, SR, G, _, S.
2. `S - RG S`
3. `.N` = mandra (lower) Ni. `S'` = taar (upper) Sa.
4. Say, clap, air-finger, play.
5. A good answer: after the first `S -` (beat 8): `S R G - G R S - , R G R S S - - -`. Breathing before the final long Sa would split the last phrase.

</details>
