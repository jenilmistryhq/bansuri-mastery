## 1. Learning objectives

- Understand what alankars are and why every Indian musician practises them.
- Play alankars 1-10 in **Version A** (pentatonic `.P .D S R G`) and **Version B** (`.P .D .N S R G`) at 60 BPM, one note per beat, slurred and clean.
- Play them at two notes per beat (RHY-03) while keeping time (RHY-04).
- Apply any alankar pattern to a new set of notes using its **number formula**.

## 2. Prerequisites

Clean `.P .D .N S R G` ([L09](L09-fingering-sa-re-ga.md), [L11](L11-lower-notes-ni-dha-pa.md)); reading rhythmic sargam ([L10](L10-reading-and-writing-sargam.md)).

## 3. Simple explanation

An **alankar** (also called **palta**) is a short note pattern that moves step by step through a scale, going up (**aroh**, ascent) and coming down (**avroh**, descent). Example: "1 2 3, 2 3 4, 3 4 5..." up the scale.

Why they matter (Fact about the tradition; Recommendation for you):

- **Fingers:** they train every transition between neighbouring notes.
- **Ear and memory:** you learn to see the scale as a ladder and think in patterns, as improvisers do.
- **Rhythm:** with a metronome at rising speeds, they build the steady time and speed you'll need for taans (fast passages).
- **Raga preparation:** the same patterns are later played inside each raga's notes.

**Number formulas.** Think of the notes of a scale as numbered 1, 2, 3... For Version A (`.P .D S R G`): 1 = `.P`, 2 = `.D`, 3 = `S`, 4 = `R`, 5 = `G`. A formula like "1 2 3 | 2 3 4 | 3 4 5" then becomes `.P .D S | .D S R | S R G`. Once you know the formula, you can play the alankar on **any** set of notes. That's why later versions unlock as you learn more notes.

## 4. Step-by-step instructions

### How to practise an alankar

1. **Read and say** it in rhythm (no flute).
2. **Air-finger** it while saying the names.
3. **Play at 60 BPM, one note per beat**, slurred. 3 clean times in a row.
4. **Ladder up** in steps of 4 BPM to 80 BPM ([L01](L01-how-to-practise.md)).
5. When 80 BPM is clean, go back to 60 BPM at **two notes per beat** (`SR` style) and ladder again. Two per beat at 60 = one per beat at 120.
6. Always do **aroh and avroh** (up and down).

**Breathing:** breathe at the turnaround between aroh and avroh, marked `,`. If you need more breaths, add them at the end of a group, never in the middle.

### Version A: pentatonic `.P .D S R G` (unlocked after L11)

Numbers: 1 = `.P`, 2 = `.D`, 3 = `S`, 4 = `R`, 5 = `G`. These five notes form a **pentatonic** (5-note) scale. It's the scale of Raga Bhupali ([L20](L20-raga-bhupali.md)) placed low, and it has no awkward notes, so everything sounds good.

| # | Formula | Aroh | Avroh |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | straight | `.P .D S R G - - -` | `G R S .D .P - - -` |
| 2 | pairs: 1 2, 2 3... | <code>.P .D &#124; .D S &#124; S R &#124; R G</code> | <code>G R &#124; R S &#124; S .D &#124; .D .P</code> |
| 3 | 1 2 3, 2 3 4... | <code>.P .D S &#124; .D S R &#124; S R G</code> | <code>G R S &#124; R S .D &#124; S .D .P</code> |
| 4 | 1 2 3 4, 2 3 4 5 | <code>.P .D S R &#124; .D S R G</code> | <code>G R S .D &#124; R S .D .P</code> |
| 5 | skip: 1 3, 2 4, 3 5 | <code>.P S &#124; .D R &#124; S G</code> | <code>G S &#124; R .D &#124; S .P</code> |
| 6 | 1 2 1, 2 3 2... | <code>.P .D .P &#124; .D S .D &#124; S R S &#124; R G R</code> | <code>G R G &#124; R S R &#124; S .D S &#124; .D .P .D</code> |
| 7 | repeat: 1 1 2 2... | <code>.P .P .D .D &#124; S S R R &#124; G G - -</code> | <code>G G R R &#124; S S .D .D &#124; .P .P - -</code> |
| 8 | 1 2 3 1, 2 3 4 2... | <code>.P .D S .P &#124; .D S R .D &#124; S R G S</code> | <code>G R S G &#124; R S .D R &#124; S .D .P S</code> |
| 9 | 1 3 2 4, 2 4 3 5 | <code>.P S .D R &#124; .D R S G</code> | <code>G S R .D &#124; R .D S .P</code> |
| 10 | up and back: 1 2 3 2 1 | <code>.P .D S .D .P &#124; .D S R S .D &#124; S R G R S</code> | <code>G R S R G &#124; R S .D S R &#124; S .D .P .D S</code> |

Notes:

- `|` here separates the pattern groups. Play every note for 1 beat, no gaps between groups.
- **Alankar 7** has repeated notes. Separate them with a small breath pulse ("hu-hu") for now; [L13](L13-articulation-tonguing-and-slurs.md) teaches tonguing.
- End every alankar on a held note (2-4 beats), usually Sa or the starting note.

### Two notes per beat (example: Alankar 3, Version B)

```
.P.D .N.D .N S  | .N S  R S  R G  ||   (aroh, 2 notes per beat)
```

Write the pairs together in one beat (`.P.D`, read as `.P` and `.D` in one beat). Count "1-and 2-and".

## 7. Guided practice (block C, 10-12 min daily)

| Week | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | Version A, alankars 1-5, one note per beat, 60 BPM |
| 2 | Version A 6-10; Version B 1-5; ladder to 72 BPM |
| 3 | Version B 6-10; A and B 1-5 at 2 notes per beat, 60 BPM |
| 4+ | Rotate: 3 alankars a day, mixed versions, tempo ladder. Log your clean tempo per alankar |

Each session: pick 3 alankars, 3 clean repetitions each (aroh plus avroh = one repetition).

## 8. Independent exercises

1. **Invent an alankar:** write a formula (for example "1 3 2, 2 4 3...") and apply it to Version B. Play it.
2. Play Alankar 3 from memory with your eyes closed.
3. Say Alankar 9 aloud (no flute) at 80 BPM. Thinking speed comes before finger speed.

## 9. Common mistakes and corrections

| Mistake | How you notice | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Speeding up in the "easy" parts | Recording drifts ahead of the click | Metronome always on; count groups |
| Slowing down at the turnaround | Late after the top note | Plan the breath; practise the turnaround alone |
| Blips on `.P` to `S` moves | Extra notes on the recording | Slow practice of that pair ([L11](L11-lower-notes-ni-dha-pa.md)) |
| Playing only aroh | Avroh is weaker | Always do both |
| Uneven two-per-beat | "Long-short" galloping | Say "1-and" aloud while clapping first |
| Rushing to fast tempos | Same mistakes repeated | 3-clean rule; ladder in steps of 4 |

More: [TS-06](../troubleshooting/TS-06-finger-leaks-and-transitions.md), [TS-09](../troubleshooting/TS-09-rhythm-and-tempo.md).

## 10. Quiz

1. What are aroh and avroh?
2. Apply formula "1 2 3" to Version A, starting from 2.
3. Write the avroh of Alankar 5 in Version B.
4. Why are later versions "unlocked" by later lessons?
5. At 60 BPM with two notes per beat, how many notes per second do you play?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answer key</summary>

1. Aroh = ascending (going up), avroh = descending (going down).
2. `.D S R` (2 = `.D`, 3 = `S`, 4 = `R`).
3. `G S | R .N | S .D | .N .P`
4. They need notes you haven't learned yet (madhya P D N, tivra and shuddha Ma, taar notes).
5. Two.

</details>
