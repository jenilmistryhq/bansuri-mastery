## 1. Learning objectives

- Play `S' R' G' m' M'` with a clear tone (FNG-09).
- Leap between octaves and across wide intervals cleanly (FNG-14).
- Keep upper notes in tune (they tend to go sharp) (PIT-07).
- Match tone quality across mandra, madhya and taar (TON-10).
- (Phase 5) Map your own flute's third-register notes (FNG-10).

## 2. Prerequisites

- Reliable overblowing to madhya `P D N S'` ([L14](L14-tivra-ma-and-madhya-pa-dha-ni.md)).
- Shuddha Ma half-hole ([L16](L16-shuddha-ma-and-half-holing.md)).
- Pitch control by angle and air ([L17](L17-intonation-and-drone.md)).

## 3. Simple explanation

You already use the **second register** for madhya `P D N` and taar `S'`. The taar notes
`R' G' m' M'` are also second register: same fingers as madhya `R G m M`, faster air.

Above `M'` you need the **third register**. Here the simple "same fingers, more air" rule
stops working, and each flute responds differently. Players find special fingerings for
`P'`, `D'` and higher.

**Fact:** high notes need **faster, more focused** air, a **smaller** lip opening and **firm
belly support**, not more force. Blowing harder mostly makes them louder, sharper and harsher.

**Uncertain (flute-specific):** no verified source gives a complete third-register chart for a
6-hole C bansuri ([Resource gaps](../02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md#gaps-found-during-research)). You will build your
own, verified with a tuner.

## 4. Step-by-step instructions

### 4.1 Taar Sa to taar tivra Ma

1. Play madhya `R` gently. Keep the fingers (`XXO OOO`), make the lip opening smaller, and
   speed up the air until it flips to `R'`. Same method for `G -> G'`, `m -> m'`, `M -> M'`.
2. Then approach from below in scale order: `D N S' R' G'`.
3. Hold each new note 4 s. Check the tuner. **Expect it to read sharp at first.** Correct by
   rolling in slightly and using **less** air speed until it is just enough to keep the note.

### 4.2 Octave leaps (FNG-14)

`S S'`, `R R'`, `G G'`, `.P P`, `.D D`, `.N N`. Fingers do not move; only air changes. The
leap should be instant, with no intermediate squeak. Then wider leaps that also change fingers:
`.P S'`, `S G'`, `.D R'`.

### 4.3 Soft high notes

Practise `S'` and `R'` **softly**. Soft high notes require the most control and are the best
test of a good embouchure. If the note drops to the lower octave when soft, keep the air
**fast but small** (less volume, not less speed).

### 4.4 Third register exploration (Phase 5, FNG-10)

Do **not** start this until taar Sa-Ga are reliable. Then:

1. Try `XXX XXX` with very fast, focused air: it may give `P'` (a 3rd-register note).
2. Try common variants such as lifting hole 1 (`OXX XXX`) or hole 1 and others, and record
   what note the tuner shows.
3. Fill in your chart (section 5). Keep the fingerings that are both **in tune** and
   **stable**.
4. Have a teacher check if possible: there are many variants, and a teacher knows which work
   well on your flute type.

## 8. Guided practice (12-15 minutes per day)

| Minutes | Exercise |
|---|---|
| 3 | Flips: `R -> R'`, `G -> G'`, `m -> m'` x 5 each (rest lips between sets) |
| 3 | Long tones `S' R' G'` 6 s each, tuner within +/-15 cents |
| 3 | Octave leaps (4.2) at 60 BPM, 10 clean each |
| 3 | Two-octave Bilawal scale from `.P` to `G'` and back at 50 -> 60 BPM |
| 2 | Soft taar Sa and Re (4.3) |

**Caution:** high notes tire the lips and can cause light-headedness. Take 30-60 s breaks
([TS-07](../troubleshooting/TS-07-breath-dizziness-and-endurance.md)).

## 9. Independent exercises

1. Play Yaman's antara ([L21](L21-raga-yaman.md)) and extend phrases up to `R'` and `G'`:
   `N R' G' R' S'`.
2. Tone-matching (TON-10): record `.P`, `P`, then `S`, `S'`, then `G`, `G'`. Do the pairs sound
   like the same voice? Score Tone on the rubric for each.
3. Fill in your chart section 5 for `S'` to `M'`.

## 10. Common mistakes and corrections

| Mistake | Correction | Guide |
|---|---|---|
| Forcing with tight lips and loud air | Smaller opening, relaxed corners, faster not louder | [TS-04](../troubleshooting/TS-04-high-notes.md) |
| Taar notes sharp | Roll in slightly, reduce air speed to the minimum that holds the note | [TS-05](../troubleshooting/TS-05-pitch-out-of-tune.md) |
| Squeak or wrong note on leaps | Prepare the embouchure before the leap; slower tempo | [TS-02](../troubleshooting/TS-02-squeaks-and-wrong-octave.md) |
| Thin, harsh tone | Open the throat ("oh" shape), support from belly | [TS-04](../troubleshooting/TS-04-high-notes.md) |
| Dizziness | Rest, breathe normally | [TS-07](../troubleshooting/TS-07-breath-dizziness-and-endurance.md) |

4. Why is there no universal chart for `P'` and above?
5. What is a good test of embouchure control in the upper octave?

1. `XXO OOO`, same as madhya Re, with faster air.
2. The second register.
