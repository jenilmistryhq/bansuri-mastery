# L23 - Meend: The Singing Glide

## 1. Learning objectives

- Understand why ornaments in Indian music are part of the **grammar**, not decoration (ORN-01).
- Play smooth descending and ascending **meends** (glides) by sliding fingers gradually (ORN-02).
- Land every meend **in tune** on the target note.
- Use meend musically in a raga phrase and a bhajan-style melody.

## 2. Prerequisites

- Half-holing by sliding ([L16](L16-shuddha-ma-and-half-holing.md)): meend is the same motion, done continuously.
- Drone and tuner listening ([L17](L17-intonation-and-drone.md)).
- Bhupali phrases ([L20](L20-raga-bhupali.md)).

## 3. Simple explanation

**Meend** (also spelled mind) is a smooth, continuous slide from one note to another, passing
through all the tiny pitches in between, like a singer's voice gliding. It is the most
characteristic sound of the bansuri in Hindustani music, because the flute imitates the voice
(**gayaki ang**, "singing style").

**ORN-01, why ornaments are grammar:** in a raga, **how** you reach a note matters as much as
**which** note. In Bhupali, a gentle glide from Ga down to Re is typical; sliding through
Ma there would break the raga. So you learn each ornament together with **where** it belongs.

**How it works on the bansuri:** a hole that is gradually covered or uncovered changes the
pitch gradually. So you **slide the finger slowly** onto or off the hole, instead of lifting
it. Very small meends (a few cents) can also be made by rolling the flute or changing air
speed ([L17](L17-intonation-and-drone.md)).

**Limit (Fact):** you can glide only between notes in the **same register**. A glide that
needs a register change (for example `M` to `P`) cannot be made by sliding alone.

## 4. Step-by-step instructions

### 4.1 Descending meend `G~R` (one step down)

1. Play Ga (`XOO OOO`): finger 1 down, finger 2 hovering just above hole 2.
2. Rest finger 2 on the **edge** of hole 2.
3. Over about 2 seconds, **slowly slide** finger 2 across until the hole is fully covered.
4. Keep the air completely steady. The pitch should fall smoothly from E to D.
5. Arrive and **hold** Re for 2 seconds. Check the tuner: Re must be in tune.

### 4.2 Ascending meend `R~G`

Reverse: from Re (`XXO OOO`) slide finger 2 **off** hole 2 slowly. Ascending meends are
usually harder: the pitch tends to jump at the end. Go even slower near the end.

### 4.3 Meend over two or more notes `G~S`

Slide finger 2 on, then finger 3 on, one after the other in one continuous motion. The pitch
should fall from Ga through Re to Sa **without steps**. At first you will hear steps; smooth
them out over weeks.

### 4.4 Speed and shape

- **Slow meend** (1-3 s): alap and bhajans.
- **Quick meend** (a fraction of a beat): connecting notes in compositions and songs.
- The **arrival** note gets the time; the glide is the journey.

**What you should hear:** like a voice sliding, smooth, with no bumps or stops, ending
clearly on the target note. If it sounds like a siren with no clear arrival, the target note
is not held long enough.

## 7. Guided practice (10-12 minutes per day)

| Minutes | Exercise |
|---|---|
| 3 | Slow (2 s) descending meends from the table, register 1, with drone. Hold each arrival 2 s. |
| 3 | Same, ascending |
| 2 | Two-note meends `G~S`, `S~.D` |
| 2 | Quick meends inside phrases (below) at 60 BPM |
| 2 | Record and listen: any steps? any sharp/flat arrivals? |

Phrases:

```
Bhupali:  S R G , G~R , S .D S          P G D P , G~R S
Yaman:    .N R G , M~G , R G R , .N R S
```

## 8. Independent exercises: meend in a bhajan-style melody

An **original** slow melody in Keherwa (about 60 BPM) to practise meend. Long notes `-`:

```
 X                 0
 S   R   G   -  |  G~R  -   S   -   ||
 .D  S   R   G  |  P   -   G   -   ||
 P   D   P   G  |  G~R  -   S   -   ||
 .D  S   R   G  |  R~S  -   -   -   ||
```

Play it plain first (no meend), then with the meends marked. Record both and compare which
sounds more like singing.

## 9. Common mistakes and corrections

| Mistake | Correction | Guide |
|---|---|---|
| Lifting/placing instead of sliding (steps, not glide) | Keep the finger in contact with the bamboo | [TS-06](../troubleshooting/TS-06-finger-leaks-and-transitions.md) |
| Air pressure changes during the glide | Steady air; only the finger moves | [TS-07](../troubleshooting/TS-07-breath-dizziness-and-endurance.md) |
| Arrival note out of tune | Slow down the end of the slide; drone check | [TS-05](../troubleshooting/TS-05-pitch-out-of-tune.md) |
| Using meend everywhere | Only where the raga/song uses it; copy recordings | ORN-01 |
| Jump at the end of ascending meend | Slower near the end; light finger | [TS-06](../troubleshooting/TS-06-finger-leaks-and-transitions.md) |

## 10. Quiz

1. What is meend?
2. Which finger slides for `R~S`?
3. Why can't you make a meend from `M` to `P` by sliding?
4. Why are ornaments called "grammar" in Indian music?
5. Which gets more time, the glide or the arrival note?

<details markdown="1"><summary>Answer key</summary>

1. A smooth, continuous glide from one note to another through all pitches in between.
2. Finger 3 (sliding onto hole 3).
3. `M` is register 1 (all open) and `P` is register 2 (all closed); the register change cannot be made by sliding.
4. Because how a note is approached is part of a raga's identity; the wrong ornament can change or break the raga.
5. Usually the arrival note.

</details>
