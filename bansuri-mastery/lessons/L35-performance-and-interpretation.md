## 1. Learning objectives

1. Play standing with good posture (BOD-02).
2. Memorise pieces reliably so you can start from any section (PRF-04).
3. Prepare for nerves with mock performances and a recovery routine (PRF-05).
4. Perform for others, from family to public events (PRF-06).
5. Use a microphone without excessive breath noise (PRF-07).
6. Shape phrases, dynamics, pacing and silence to express a musical intention (PRF-08).

## 2. Prerequisites

You have several pieces you can play correctly in practice, and you record and score yourself
regularly with the rubric (Tone, Pitch, Rhythm, Transitions/Technique, Expression, each 1-4).

## 3. Simple explanation

**Fact:** almost everyone plays worse in front of people than alone. Adrenaline makes hands
shake, breath shallow and tempo rush. The solution is not to "feel no nerves", it is to
prepare so well that 80% of your best is still good.

**Interpretation** means deciding *how* a piece should sound: where phrases rise and fall, which
notes are important, where to slow down, where to leave silence. Correct notes are the
starting point, not the goal.

## 4. Step-by-step instructions

### Part A. Preparation

**Step 1. Standing posture (BOD-02).** Feet hip-width apart, weight balanced, knees soft (not
locked), spine tall, shoulders relaxed, flute level. Elbows slightly away from the body. Check
on video from the front and side. Practise 3 minutes standing in every session for two weeks
before a performance.

**Step 2. Memorise in layers (PRF-04).**

| Layer | How |
|---|---|
| Ear | Sing the whole piece without the flute |
| Notation | Write the piece from memory; compare with the original |
| Fingers | Play it slowly with eyes closed |
| Structure | Know the sections by name (intro, mukhda, antara...) and mark **4 starting points** |
| Stress test | Have someone call out a starting point; start from it immediately |

**Step 3. Mock performances (PRF-05).** Three rules:

1. Record on video as if it were real; announce the piece first.
2. **Never stop.** If you make a mistake, keep going (rejoin at the next beat or phrase).
3. Afterwards, score with the rubric and note only the 2 most important fixes.

Do at least 3 mock performances before each real one.

**Step 4. A pre-performance routine.** Example (adapt it):

- 10 minutes before: warm-up (shoulders, wrists, 3 slow breaths, [L01](L01-how-to-practise.md)).
- 5 minutes before: long tones on Sa with the drone; check tuning.
- 1 minute before: slow breath out, relax shoulders, sing the first phrase in your head.

**Step 5. Recovery plan.** Decide in advance what you do if:

| Problem | Plan |
|---|---|
| A note cracks | Continue; do not repeat it |
| Memory slip | Jump to the next starting point you marked |
| Running out of breath | Shorten the phrase; breathe at the next comma |
| Dry mouth | Small sip of water between pieces (keep water nearby) |
| Flute goes flat when cold | Warm it under your arm or with gentle breath before playing |

### Part B. Performing (PRF-06, PRF-07)

**Step 6. Build up the audience gradually.**

| Level | Audience | Length |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | One family member | 1 piece |
| 2 | Family or friends | 3 pieces (Milestone M7) |
| 3 | Online (a private video shared with a few people) | 5-10 min |
| 4 | Community event, temple, school, open mic | 10-15 min (Milestone M9) |
| 5 | Formal recital | 30+ min (Milestone M10) |

**Step 7. Programme order.** Start with a piece you find easy (confidence), put the most
demanding piece in the middle, end with something you love playing.

**Step 8. Microphones (PRF-07).**

> **Recommendation (general practice; verify by listening):** place the microphone about
> 15-30 cm away, aimed toward the flute body near the blowing hole but slightly off to the side
> or below, so the air stream does not hit the microphone directly. Pointing a mic straight at
> the blowing hole records loud breath noise.

Do a sound check: play your softest and loudest notes and the lowest and highest notes. Ask
someone at the back whether they hear breath noise. Record yourself with a phone or external
mic at home first and compare positions.

### Part C. Interpretation (PRF-08)

**Step 9. Phrase shape.** Most phrases have a direction: they grow toward an important note and
relax after it. Mark each phrase in your notation with a simple arc:

```
  < growing ----- peak ---- relaxing >
  S  R  G  P  |  D  -  P  G  |  R  S  -  -
```

Play the growing part slightly stronger, the relaxing part slightly softer.

**Step 10. Dynamics.** Use at least three levels (soft, medium, strong) in every piece. On
bansuri, keep the pitch steady when changing volume (roll in slightly when louder, out when
softer, [L17](L17-intonation-and-drone.md)).

**Step 11. Pacing and silence.** In slow pieces, let long notes settle; allow a short silence
after a phrase ends. In fast pieces, hold the tempo steady and make the silences exact.

**Step 12. Intention.** Before playing, write one sentence: "This piece should feel ____."
(calm, joyful, longing, devotional, playful). Make every decision serve that sentence.

**Step 13. Two interpretations.** Record the same piece twice with two different intentions
(for example "calm and devotional" vs "bright and joyful"). Explain the differences in writing.

## 5. Diagram: performance preparation timeline

```
Week -3   choose programme; memorise in layers
Week -2   mock performance 1; fix top 2 problems
Week -1   mock performances 2-3; standing practice; mic test if needed
Day  -1   light practice only; play through once; rest
Day   0   routine; perform; never stop; enjoy
Day  +1   watch the recording; rubric; write 3 lessons learned
```

## 7. Guided practice

| Situation | Daily minutes | Task |
|---|---|---|
| 3 weeks before any performance | 10 | Memorisation layers; standing practice |
| 2 weeks before | 15 | Mock performance every 3-4 days |
| Any time in P4-P5 | 10 | One interpretation exercise per week (Step 13) |

## 8. Independent exercises

1. Mark 4 starting points in 3 memorised pieces and pass the stress test (Step 2).
2. Write your personal pre-performance routine and recovery plan.
3. Record two interpretations of one piece with written explanations.

## 9. Common mistakes and corrections

| Mistake | Correction |
|---|---|
| Stopping after a mistake | Mock performances with the "never stop" rule |
| Tempo rushes on stage | Sing the first phrase in your head at the right tempo before starting ([TS-09](../troubleshooting/TS-09-rhythm-and-tempo.md)) |
| Shaky, airy tone from nerves | Long tones before playing; slow exhale routine ([TS-01](../troubleshooting/TS-01-no-sound-or-airy-tone.md)) |
| Shoulders rise when standing | Video check; tension scan ([TS-08](../troubleshooting/TS-08-body-pain-and-tension.md)) |
| Everything played at one volume | Mark dynamics in the notation; exaggerate in practice |
| Breath noise in the mic | Move the mic off the air stream; test positions |
| Choosing pieces at the limit of your ability | Perform pieces you play at 90%+ success in practice |

## 10. Quiz

1. What are the three rules of a mock performance?
2. Where should a microphone **not** point?
3. What is the purpose of marking 4 starting points?
4. What is one sentence you should write before interpreting a piece?

<details markdown="1"><summary>Answer key</summary>

1. Record it as real; never stop; score afterwards and note the top 2 fixes.
2. Directly at the blowing hole / into the air stream.
3. To recover from memory slips and to prove deep memorisation.
4. "This piece should feel ____."

</details>
