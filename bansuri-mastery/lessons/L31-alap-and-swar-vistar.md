## 1. Learning objectives

1. Explain the forms of a Hindustani instrumental performance: alap, jod, jhala, gat/bandish (THY-12).
2. Explain purvang/uttarang and nyas notes, and identify nyas notes in a recording (THY-09).
3. Develop a raga slowly, note by note, without a fixed rhythm (swar vistar), following the raga's rules (RAG-09, IMP-04).
4. Assess your own alap with the raga rules checklist.

## 2. Prerequisites

You can play Raga Yaman's aroh, avroh and pakad cleanly and in tune with a drone, including
`S'` and `R' G'`, and you can play a smooth meend and kan.

## 3. Simple explanation

### Forms (THY-12)

A full Hindustani instrumental raga performance usually unfolds like this (**Fact**, with
many variations between artists):

| Section | Rhythm | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Alap** | No tala, no fixed pulse | The raga is introduced slowly, note by note, from low to high |
| **Jod** | A steady pulse but no tala cycle | More rhythmic movement; the raga continues to develop |
| **Jhala** | Fast pulse | Climax of the opening part (on bansuri, made with rapid tonguing or breath patterns) |
| **Gat / bandish** | With tabla, in a tala | A fixed composition plus improvisations (taans, tihais), often slow then fast |

Light forms (bhajan, thumri, dadra, ghazal, dhun, film song) are shorter and freer with raga
rules. This lesson focuses on **alap**.

### Swar vistar

**Swar vistar** means "expansion of the notes": building phrases around one note at a time and
gradually extending the range. Alap is the large-scale version of swar vistar.

### Purvang, uttarang and nyas (THY-09)

- **Purvang**: the lower half of the octave (roughly `S` to `m`/`P`).
- **Uttarang**: the upper half (roughly `P` to `S'`).
- **Nyas**: a note where phrases rest. In Yaman, common resting notes are `G`, `P`, `N`, `R` and
  `S` (Sa is the final home).

### Why this matters

A scale played up and down is not a raga. A raga is a set of **ways of moving**: which phrases
are used, where to rest, which notes are emphasised and how notes are approached. Alap is
where you show that you understand this.

## 4. Step-by-step instructions

### Step 1. Review the Yaman rules (from [L21](L21-raga-yaman.md) and the [Yaman card](../ragas/yaman.md))

| Element | Yaman |
|---|---|
| Thaat | Kalyan |
| Notes | `S R G M P D N` (tivra Ma, all others shuddha) |
| Aroh | `.N R G M D N S'` (Sa and Pa are often skipped when ascending; some versions include Pa) |
| Avroh | `S' N D P M G R S` |
| Pakad (one common version) | `.N R G R , .N R S , P M G R , .N R S` |
| Vadi / samvadi | `G` / `N` |
| Typical phrases | `.N R G`, `M D N`, `P R` (a leap or meend from Pa down to Re), `G M D N S'`, `N D P` |
| Time | Evening (sources differ on exact hours, see [L19](L19-what-is-a-raga.md)) |

> **Disputed / varies:** exact aroh and pakad wordings differ between teachers and books. The
> versions above are widely taught. If your teacher teaches a different version, follow your
> teacher and note the difference.

> **Instrument limit (Fact):** mandra tivra Ma (`.M`, F#4) is below your flute's lowest note
> (`.P`, G4). Phrases a vocalist sings through `.M` must be reshaped on your flute (start them
> from `.P` or `.D`). Pannalal Ghosh added an extra hole to his flute partly to solve this
> ([R-W08](../02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md#2-written-lessons-and-fingering-charts)).

### Step 2. How to read alap notation in this lesson

Alap has no beat. In the phrases below:

- `-` means "hold this note noticeably longer" (not exactly one beat).
- `,` means a breath or a pause.
- `~` is a meend, `{x}` a kan, as usual.

Play each phrase **slowly**, with the drone, and let each long note settle in tune before
moving.

### Step 3. Build the alap in stages

**Stage 1: establish Sa and the lower notes.** Stay below `G`.

```
S - - , .N - .D - .N R S - ,
.N .D .P - , .D .N R - S - ,
.N R S - , .D .N R .N - S - -
```

**Stage 2: reach Ga, the vadi.** Rest on `G` for the first time. Make it a moment.

```
.N R G - - , R G R - S ,
.N R G - , M G - R - , .N R S - ,
{M}G - R G - - , R .N R S -
```

**Stage 3: introduce tivra Ma and Pa.** Show the `P R` link.

```
G M P - - , M G R - , .N R S ,
G M P - , M G M P - , P~R - , .N R S - ,
R G M P - M G , M~R - S
```

**Stage 4: uttarang, Dha and Ni (the samvadi).**

```
M D N - - , D P - , M G R - S ,
G M D N - , D N D P - , M G R G - ,
P M D N D - P , M G R , .N R S -
```

**Stage 5: taar Sa and above.** This is the high point of the alap.

```
M D N S' - - , N D P - , M G R S ,
G M D N R' S' - - , N R' G' R' S' - , N D P - ,
S' N D P , M G R G - , R .N R S - - -
```

**Stage 6: descend and resolve.** Return slowly, ending on Sa with a long, steady note.

```
N D P - , M G R G - , P~R - , .N R S - - - -
```

### Step 4. Make it your own

The phrases above are examples, not a script. Once you can play them, **improvise** within each
stage: change lengths, add kan and meend, repeat a phrase with a small change, rest longer on
a nyas note. Stay in one stage until you have said something there.

### Step 5. Pacing

A beginner's 5-minute alap might spend about:

| Stage | Time |
|---|---|
| 1 | 45 s |
| 2 | 60 s |
| 3 | 60 s |
| 4 | 60 s |
| 5 | 45 s |
| 6 | 30 s |

Do not rush to taar Sa. Patience is part of the style.

## 5. Diagrams and checklist

### Alap shape

```
 S'/R'/G'                                  ****  (stage 5)
 D N                              ****     
 M P                     ****                      ***   (stage 6 descends)
 G               ****                                  **
 S/.N    ****                                            ***  final Sa
          st1    st2      st3      st4      st5     st6
```

### Raga rules checklist

Use this to assess any alap, improvisation or composition (also used by [L30](L30-composition-foundations.md)):

- [ ] Only the raga's notes were used (no shuddha `m` in Yaman).
- [ ] Ascending and descending phrases follow aroh/avroh shapes (no "scale runs" that ignore them).
- [ ] The pakad or characteristic phrases appear, especially near the start.
- [ ] Phrases rest on proper nyas notes (Yaman: `G`, `P`, `N`, `R`, final `S`).
- [ ] The vadi (`G`) and samvadi (`N`) are emphasised.
- [ ] The development is gradual (range expands stage by stage).
- [ ] Ornaments suit the raga (for example meend `P~R`, kan into `G`).
- [ ] Every long note is in tune against the drone.
- [ ] It ends on Sa, settled.
- [ ] A knowledgeable listener (or you, comparing with a master recording) would recognise the raga within 30 s.

## 7. Guided practice

| Week | Daily minutes | Task |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 15 | Stages 1-2 only: play the example phrases 3 times each, then improvise 1 min per stage |
| 2 | 15 | Add stage 3; record a 3-min alap (stages 1-3) |
| 3 | 15 | Add stage 4 |
| 4 | 20 | Add stages 5-6; record a full 5-min alap; checklist |
| 5-6 | 20 | Alternate days: Yaman alap and Bhupali alap (rules on the [Bhupali card](../ragas/bhupali.md)) |
| 7+ | 15 | Compare your recording with a master recording phrase by phrase; fix one weakness per week |

## 8. Independent exercises

1. Write your own 6-stage alap outline for Bhupali, using its pakad (`G R , S .D , S R G , P G , D P G R S`) and nyas notes.
2. Label the sections (alap, jod, gat, taans, jhala) with timestamps in a 10-minute recorded performance (THY-12).
3. Record yourself playing the Yaman alap without the drone, then with it. Compare tuning.

## 9. Common mistakes and corrections

| Mistake | Correction |
|---|---|
| Playing the scale up and down | Use phrases with rests; check against the checklist |
| Rushing to the high notes | Follow the stage timings; stay low longer |
| Every note the same length | Make nyas notes long and passing notes short |
| Long notes drift out of tune | Play with the drone; fix with angle and air ([TS-05](../troubleshooting/TS-05-pitch-out-of-tune.md)) |
| Running out of breath mid-phrase | Plan breaths with `,` marks; shorter phrases ([TS-07](../troubleshooting/TS-07-breath-dizziness-and-endurance.md)) |
| Using shuddha Ma by accident | Remember: in Yaman, Ma is all holes open |
| Forgetting what you just played | Repeat ideas deliberately; the alap should sound connected |

## 10. Quiz and performance test

1. What are the four main sections of an instrumental raga performance?
2. Name three nyas notes in Yaman.
3. Why can't you play `.M` on your flute?
4. What is the difference between alap and jod?

<details markdown="1"><summary>Answer key</summary>

1. Alap, jod, jhala, gat/bandish (with taans and tihais).
2. Any three of: G, P, N, R, S.
3. It is F#4, below the lowest note `.P` (G4).
4. Alap has no pulse; jod has a steady pulse but no tala cycle.

</details>

**Performance test:** record a 5-minute Yaman alap with a drone. Score it with the checklist
(all items must be checked) and the rubric from [L08](L08-recording-and-self-assessment.md)
(Pitch 3+, Tone 3+, Expression 3+).
