## 1. Learning objectives

1. Describe the typical tone, articulation, ornaments and rhythmic feel of six styles.
2. Play a folk tune and a dhun (SNG-05).
3. Play one melody convincingly in two different styles (SNG-07).
4. Ornament a plain melody appropriately for two styles (ORN-09).
5. Explain the main differences between Hindustani and Carnatic music and flute playing (THY-13, Enrich).

## 2. Prerequisites

You can learn a song by ear ([L18](L18-song-learning-system.md)) and play meend, kan and murki
cleanly. You can tongue and slur ([L13](L13-articulation-tonguing-and-slurs.md)).

## 3. Simple explanation

Think of styles as **accents** of the same language. The notes may be identical, but a film
song, a bhajan and a raga phrase are "pronounced" differently. Style is made of five things:

1. **Tone** (bright or dark, straight or with vibrato).
2. **Articulation** (tongued, slurred, breath-attacked).
3. **Ornaments** (which ones, how many, how fast).
4. **Rhythm feel** (strict or flexible, straight or swung, Indian tala or Western meter).
5. **Freedom with the melody** (exact copy, or improvised decoration).

**Recommendation:** the most reliable way to learn a style is to copy recordings closely
before adding your own ideas.

## 4. Style profiles

| Style | Tone | Articulation | Typical ornaments | Rhythm | Freedom |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Film song** | Bright, clear, often with some vibrato on long notes | Mixed: tonguing for repeated notes, slurs within phrases | Short kan, quick murki where the singer has them | Often Western meter (4/4, 3/4, 6/8) or keherwa/dadra; syncopation common | Low: listeners expect the recorded melody |
| **Bhajan / devotional** | Warm, sustained, calm | Mostly slurred; soft breath attacks | Meend (very important), gentle kan | Keherwa, dadra or free; often slow | Medium: some decoration in repeats |
| **Ghazal / light classical (thumri, dadra form)** | Soft, intimate | Slurred, delicate | Murki, khatka, meend; expressive and refined | Flexible; rubato (stretching time) in phrases | Medium-high: decoration and variation expected |
| **Folk** | Bright, strong, sometimes breathy on purpose | Tongued, rhythmic; repeated notes | Fewer ornaments; rhythmic flourishes, quick grace notes | Dance-like, strong pulse | Medium: repetitive patterns with variation |
| **Dhun** (light raga-based tune, often in Pahadi, Khamaj, Mishra ragas) | Warm | Slurred with some tonguing | Meend, kan, murki, light raga ornaments | Keherwa or dadra, lilting | High: improvise around the tune within the raga's flavour |
| **Hindustani classical** | Straight, centred tone; controlled | Mostly slurred; breath gamak; tonguing in jhala | Raga-specific: meend, kan, andolan, gamak | Tala-based; alap free | High but strictly within raga rules |

> **Uncertain / varies:** these are tendencies, not laws. Individual songs and artists vary.
> Always check against the original recording.

## 5. Step-by-step instructions

### Step 1. One melody, several styles (SNG-07, ORN-09)

Use this plain **original practice melody** (keherwa, Sa = C):

```
S R G - | P - G R || G P D P | G R S - ||
X         0          X         0
```

Play it four ways:

**Film-style (bright, tongued repeated notes, light vibrato on the last note):**

```
S R G G | P - GR G || G P D P | G R S - ||
(tongue the repeated G; vibrato only on final S)
```

**Bhajan-style (slow, 60 BPM, meend, sustained):**

```
S R G - | P - G~R - || G P D - | P~G R~S - ||
```

**Ghazal/light-classical style (flexible time, murki):**

```
S {G}R G - | P - (PGRG) R || G P {S'}D P | G (GRSR) S - ||
(allow small stretches of time before the long notes)
```

**Folk-style (faster, 100 BPM, tongued, strong pulse):**

```
SS RR G G | PP P G R || GG PP D P | G R S S ||
```

Record all four. Ask a listener to name each style. If they cannot, exaggerate the
differences.

### Step 2. A folk tune and a dhun (SNG-05)

1. **Folk tune:** choose a traditional folk melody you know from your region (traditional
   tunes are generally out of copyright, but check if a specific arrangement is recent). Learn it
   with the 12-step method ([L18](L18-song-learning-system.md)). Emphasise rhythm and tonguing.
2. **Dhun:** choose a raga with a light character (Pahadi or Khamaj, see the
   [raga cards](../ragas/README.md)). Make a 16-beat tune in keherwa from its typical phrases.
   Play the tune, then improvise variations around it, returning to the tune each time.

### Step 3. Study a style by transcription

Pick one recording per style. Transcribe 15 seconds of it **including ornaments and
articulation marks** ([L27](L27-bhatkhande-notation.md)). Then play along. This is how style
is really absorbed.

### Step 4. Carnatic overview (THY-13, Enrich)

| Aspect | Hindustani | Carnatic |
|---|---|---|
| Region | Mainly North India | Mainly South India |
| Swara names | 12 notes: S r R g G m M P d D n N | Same 7 names but 16 swara labels (for example R1 R2 R3, G1 G2 G3), because some pitches have two names |
| Scale system | 10 thaats (Bhatkhande) | 72 melakarta ragas |
| Ornaments | Meend, kan, andolan, gamak used according to raga | **Gamakas** are central; many notes are rarely played "plain" |
| Improvisation | Long alap, taans | Alapana, kalpana swaras, neraval |
| Common tala | Teentaal (16) | Adi tala (8 beats) |
| Flute | Bansuri, often long bass flutes, 6 holes (sometimes 7) | Venu, usually a shorter flute with **8 holes**, held and fingered differently |

**Fact:** both traditions share deep roots. Because the Carnatic venu uses different fingerings,
Carnatic flute lessons (for example parts of [R-W05](../02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md#2-written-lessons-and-fingering-charts))
may not transfer directly to your 6-hole bansuri.

## 8. Independent exercises

1. Take a bhajan you know. Make a "film arrangement" of it (intro, brighter tone, tongued rhythm) and a "classical-flavoured" version (alap-like intro, meend). Record both.
2. Write a one-paragraph style profile of your favourite bansuri player, based on listening.
3. (Enrich) Write a 1-page comparison of Hindustani and Carnatic flute (THY-13).

## 9. Common mistakes and corrections

| Mistake | Correction |
|---|---|
| Same ornaments in every style | Use the style profile table; copy a reference recording |
| Over-ornamenting film songs | Match the singer's ornaments only |
| Playing bhajans too fast | Slow down; sustain long notes; meend into important notes |
| Folk tunes without pulse | Tongue on beats; tap your foot ([TS-09](../troubleshooting/TS-09-rhythm-and-tempo.md)) |
| Dhun drifting out of the raga | Return to the tune often; check the raga card |
| Too much vibrato everywhere | Straight tone by default; vibrato as a choice ([L33](L33-gamak-andolan-advanced-ornaments.md)) |

## 10. Quiz

1. Name the five components of style.
2. Which ornament is most characteristic of bhajan playing on bansuri?
3. What is a dhun?
4. How many holes does a typical Carnatic venu have, and why does it matter to you?

<details markdown="1"><summary>Answer key</summary>

1. Tone, articulation, ornaments, rhythm feel, freedom with the melody.
2. Meend.
3. A light, raga-based tune (often in ragas such as Pahadi or Khamaj), played with variations.
4. Usually 8; its fingering differs from your 6-hole bansuri, so Carnatic fingering lessons may not transfer directly.

</details>
