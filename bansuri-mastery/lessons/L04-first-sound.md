# L04 - First Sound

**Objectives:** TON-01, TON-02, TON-03
**Time span:** Days 1-7 (some people get a sound in 5 minutes, others need a week; both are normal)
**Prerequisites:** [L02](L02-posture-and-holding.md), [L03](L03-breathing-basics.md)
**Previous:** [L03](L03-breathing-basics.md) | **Next:** [L05 - What Is Music?](L05-what-is-music-pitch-sa-sargam.md)

---

## 1. Learning objectives

- Form a small, relaxed lip opening and place the flute correctly on the lower lip (TON-01).
- Aim the air at the far edge of the blowing hole and find the "sweet spot" by rolling the flute (TON-02).
- Produce a clear Sa (`XXX OOO`) 4 times out of 5 (TON-03).

## 2. Prerequisites

Posture and hold ([L02](L02-posture-and-holding.md)); low breathing and the hiss ([L03](L03-breathing-basics.md)).

## 3. Simple explanation

A flute makes sound when a thin stream of air hits the **far edge** of the blowing hole and splits: some goes into the flute, some goes over it. That splitting makes the air inside the tube vibrate. You don't blow **into** the flute like a whistle; you blow **across** the hole, the way you blow across the top of a bottle.

Three things must be right at the same time:

1. **Lip opening (embouchure):** a small, flat, oval gap between your lips.
2. **Coverage:** how much of the blowing hole your lower lip covers.
3. **Angle:** where the air stream points.

Getting the first sound is a search, not a formula. You change one thing at a time until the flute "catches".

**Why Sa first?** `XXX OOO` (top three holes closed) is the easiest note on the bansuri. It needs only three fingers to seal, and it sits in the middle of the first register, where the flute responds most easily (Fact for most bansuris).

## 4. Step-by-step instructions

### Step 1: The bottle exercise (5 min, optional but very helpful)

Take an empty glass bottle. Rest its rim against your lower lip and blow a thin stream of air across the top, just as you would blow gently on hot tea. Move the bottle slightly, and change the air angle, until you get a hooting tone. This is exactly the bansuri skill, with a wider hole that is more forgiving.

### Step 2: Form the embouchure (TON-01)

1. Relax your face. Keep your teeth slightly apart.
2. Say "pu" softly, or "pooh" without rounding the lips much. The lips make a small, flat, oval opening in the centre. Keep the corners relaxed, not pulled into a smile and not pushed forward into a kiss.
3. Blow gently through the opening at your palm, held 10 cm away. You should feel a **narrow, cool stream** on one small spot, not a wide warm breath.
4. Check in a mirror: the opening is centred, the cheeks are not puffed, and the lips are not pressed hard together.

### Step 3: Place the flute (blowing hole only)

1. Hold the flute in playing position, but **close the top three holes** (`XXX OOO`): index, middle and ring fingers of the top hand. The bottom hand only supports the flute; holes 4-6 stay open.
2. Rest the flute against your lower lip so that the **lower lip covers roughly a quarter to a third of the blowing hole** (Uncertain: the right coverage varies by player and flute; start here and adjust).
3. The centre of your lip opening should be directly over the blowing hole.

### Step 4: Blow and search (TON-02, TON-03)

1. Take a low, relaxed breath.
2. Blow a gentle, steady stream, like a long, calm "pooh", for 3-4 seconds. **Less air than you think.**
3. Listen. You'll hear one of four results:

| You hear | Meaning | Change one thing |
|---|---|---|
| Only air (whoosh) | The air misses the edge | Roll the flute slightly **toward** you (more coverage), or aim the air a little lower/further across |
| A very faint tone inside the noise | Close | Make the lip opening smaller; keep the air steady |
| A high squeal or whistle | Too much air or too fast | Use slower, softer air; open the lips a tiny bit |
| A clear, soft note | Success | Hold still and do it again |

4. **Roll the flute** in very small steps: toward you (covering more of the hole) and away from you (covering less). Somewhere there is a sweet spot where the tone suddenly appears. Move in millimetres, not centimetres.
5. When you find the sound, play it 5 times, taking the flute away from your lips between attempts. The goal is to find the sweet spot again **quickly**, within 10 seconds.

### Step 5: Stabilise

Once you get a sound, hold it for 2-4 seconds, gently. Don't chase volume. A soft, clear note is the goal. Long tones come in [L07](L07-long-tones-tuner-and-drone.md).

**Rest often.** Practise in 2-3 minute bursts with a minute's rest. Searching for the sound uses a lot of air and can make you dizzy ([L03](L03-breathing-basics.md) dizziness protocol).

### What you should hear

- A **soft, round, slightly breathy** tone, like a gentle "hooo". Some air noise is normal at first.
- Steady, not wavering.
- On a tuner, the note **C** (C5, about 523 Hz). Now complete the tuner check from [L00 step 6](L00-day-0-your-flute.md).

If you get a high, piercing whistle instead, you are probably overblowing into the second register (the C one octave higher). Use slower air.

## 7. Guided practice (Days 1-7, about 10-15 min per day)

1. Bottle exercise: 2 minutes (until you get a tone on the bottle; then skip it).
2. Embouchure in the mirror: 1 minute.
3. **Sa search:** 5 sets of: blow 4 s, rest 10 s, adjust one thing. Rest 1 minute between sets.
4. Once you have a sound: **re-find drill**. Take the flute away, bring it back and get the sound within 10 s. 10 repetitions.
5. Record your best attempt every day ([L08](L08-recording-and-self-assessment.md)) and listen back.

## 8. Independent exercises

1. Keep a "sweet-spot note": once you find it, describe how it feels (for example, "lip covers about a third; air aimed toward the far edge").
2. Play Sa with your eyes closed, 5 times.
3. Try Sa at three volumes: very soft, soft and medium. Notice what changes. At this stage, don't try for loud.

## 9. Common mistakes and corrections

| Mistake | How you notice | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Blowing too hard | Squeal, or the higher octave; dizziness | Half the air speed; "warm tea" breath |
| Lip opening too large | Very breathy, weak tone | Smaller "pu" opening |
| Covering too much of the hole | Muffled tone or none; pitch low | Roll the flute outward slightly |
| Covering too little | Thin, airy, high-ish sound | Roll it inward slightly |
| Pressing the flute hard into the chin | Lip stiffness, sore chin | Rest the flute lightly; the thumbs support it |
| Puffed cheeks | Mirror | Relax; the air comes from the lungs, not the mouth |
| Fingers not sealing holes 1-3 | Squeaky, unstable sound | Pads centred on the holes ([L02](L02-posture-and-holding.md)) |
| Changing several things at once | Never sure what worked | Change one thing per attempt |

More help: [TS-01](../troubleshooting/TS-01-no-sound-or-airy-tone.md), [TS-02](../troubleshooting/TS-02-squeaks-and-wrong-octave.md).

## 10. Performance test

Record 5 attempts at Sa. Before each attempt, take the flute fully away from your lips.

- A pass needs **4 of 5** to produce a clear tone (more tone than air) within 10 s of placing the flute.
- Check one attempt on the tuner: it should read C.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>How to judge "clear"</summary>

Listen back to the recording. If you mainly hear a **note** with some air around it, that counts as clear. If you mainly hear **air** with a faint note inside it, it is not clear yet. Compare with the teacher's sound in R-V01. Yours will be weaker; that's fine. It only needs to be recognisably the same kind of sound.

</details>

## 11. Mastery criteria

- [ ] Mirror check: centred opening, no puffed cheeks, relaxed corners (TON-01).
- [ ] Sweet spot found within 10 s, 3 times from a fresh start (TON-02).
- [ ] Sa clear on 4 of 5 attempts (TON-03).
- [ ] Tuner reads C (C5) on a relaxed Sa (INS-02 completed).
