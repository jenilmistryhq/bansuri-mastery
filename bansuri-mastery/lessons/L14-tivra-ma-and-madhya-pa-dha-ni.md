# L14 - Tivra Ma and Madhya Pa, Dha, Ni (Completing the Middle Octave)

**Objectives:** FNG-04, FNG-05, TON-07, INS-07 | **Time span:** about 2-3 weeks | **Prerequisites:** [L07](L07-long-tones-tuner-and-drone.md), [L09](L09-fingering-sa-re-ga.md), [L11](L11-lower-notes-ni-dha-pa.md)

## 1. Learning objectives

- Play **tivra Ma** (`M`, all holes open) with a clear tone (FNG-04).
- Play **madhya Pa, Dha, Ni** (`P D N`) by **overblowing**: same fingers as mandra Pa, Dha,
  Ni, faster air (FNG-05, TON-07).
- Play the complete middle-octave scale `S R G M P D N S'`. This is the scale of **Kalyan
  thaat** and Raga Yaman.
- Explain in simple words why the same fingering gives two different notes (INS-07).

## 2. Prerequisites

- Mandra `.P .D .N` sound cleanly ([L11](L11-lower-notes-ni-dha-pa.md)). Perfect sealing of all
  six holes is essential here.
- Steady long tones ([L07](L07-long-tones-tuner-and-drone.md)).

## 3. Simple explanation

### 3.1 Tivra Ma: all holes open

When you lift every finger, the flute plays **tivra Ma** (sharp Ma, Western F# on your flute).
**Fact:** on a standard 6-hole bansuri, the all-open note is tivra Ma, not shuddha (natural)
Ma ([Resource Library sec. 1](../02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md#1-your-instrument-verified-facts)). Shuddha Ma needs a half-hole and comes in
[L16](L16-shuddha-ma-and-half-holing.md).

Tivra Ma is often a little weaker or airier than other notes because nothing is holding the
flute except your thumbs and lip contact. Grip stability matters.

### 3.2 Overblowing: how one fingering gives two notes (INS-07)

Inside the flute, the air vibrates in a pattern. With gentle, slow air it vibrates in its
simplest pattern (the low note). With **faster, more focused** air it switches to a pattern
that vibrates twice as fast, which sounds **one octave higher**. This is called the **second
register**.

- `XXX XXX` gentle = mandra Pa (G4). Faster air = **madhya Pa** (G5).
- `XXX XXO` gentle = mandra Dha. Faster = **madhya Dha**.
- `XXX XOO` gentle = mandra Ni. Faster = **madhya Ni**.
- `XXX OOO` faster = **taar Sa** (`S'`), the top of the middle octave.

Opening holes makes the vibrating air column shorter, which raises pitch. That is why each
finger you lift gives a higher note.

**Key idea:** overblowing is about **air speed**, not force. Faster air comes from a smaller
lip opening and slightly more support from the belly, not from blowing harder and louder.

## 4. Step-by-step instructions

### 4.1 Tivra Ma

1. Play Ga (`XOO OOO`) steadily.
2. Lift the first finger smoothly. Keep the flute still with both thumbs and the chin/lip.
3. Hold `M` for 4 seconds. Check the tuner: it should read **F#**.
4. Play `G M G R S` slowly, 1 note per beat at 60 BPM.

### 4.2 From mandra Pa to madhya Pa

1. Play mandra Pa (`.P`, all closed) with slow, warm air. Hold 3 seconds.
2. Without moving your fingers, make your lip opening **smaller** and say "hoo" into a
   narrower stream, slightly faster. Aim the air a little more **across** the hole (slightly
   less into it).
3. The note should "flip" up to madhya Pa. The tuner shows **G** again, but one octave higher.
4. Relax back to `.P`. Repeat 5 times.
5. Do the same with `.D -> D` and `.N -> N`, and with `S -> S'`.

### 4.3 The full middle octave

`S R G M P D N S'` and back down `S' N D P M G R S`.

The hardest change is **M to P**: you go from all fingers open to all fingers closed **and**
change register at the same moment. Practise that pair alone first: `M P M P`.

## 5. Fingering chart for this lesson

```
Note   Fingers (1-2-3 4-5-6)   Western   Air
 S        X X X  O O O           C        gentle
 R        X X O  O O O           D        gentle
 G        X O O  O O O           E        gentle
 M        O O O  O O O           F#       gentle, steady grip
 P        X X X  X X X           G        FASTER, smaller opening (register 2)
 D        X X X  X X O           A        register 2
 N        X X X  X O O           B        register 2
 S'       X X X  O O O           C        register 2
```

Scale with semitone steps (useful later):

```
S --2-- R --2-- G --2-- M --1-- P --2-- D --2-- N --1-- S'
```

This pattern (W W W H W W H) is **Kalyan thaat**. The Western major scale (Bilawal) is the same
except Ma is shuddha: you will get it in [L16](L16-shuddha-ma-and-half-holing.md).

**What you should hear:** madhya Pa should sound like the **same note name** as mandra Pa,
just higher, and as clear. If you hear a note that is **not** G (for example a high D), you
are overblowing too hard into the third register: slow the air.

## 7. Guided practice (15 minutes per day)

| Minutes | Exercise | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | Octave flips | `.P -> P` x 5, `.D -> D` x 5, `.N -> N` x 5, `S -> S'` x 5. Hold each 2 s. |
| 3 | Long tones on new notes | `M`, `P`, `D`, `N`, `S'`: 6 s each, check tuner |
| 3 | `M P` pair | `M P M P` slowly, then 1 per beat at 50 BPM, 10 clean pairs |
| 3 | Full scale | `S R G M P D N S'` / `S' N D P M G R S` at 50 -> 60 BPM |
| 3 | Alankar 1 on the full octave | from [EX-ALK](../exercises/EX-ALK-alankar-book.md) |

Rest your lips for 1 minute if the tone becomes airy. Overblowing tires the lips quickly at first.

## 8. Independent exercises

1. Leap drill: `.P P .P P` then `.D D`, `.N N` at 50 BPM. 10 clean leaps each.
2. Play `S R G M P - - - | M G R S - - - -` with a drone on C. Listen for M sounding
   bright and "leaning" upward to P.
3. Write your own 8-note melody using `S R G M P` and play it. (Preview of [L30](L30-composition-foundations.md).)

## 9. Common mistakes and corrections

| Mistake | What you hear | Correction | Guide |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blowing harder instead of faster | Loud, harsh, sharp madhya Pa or no flip | Smaller opening, same or less volume | [TS-02](../troubleshooting/TS-02-squeaks-and-wrong-octave.md) |
| Leaking hole when closing all six for P | Wrong note or squeak on `M -> P` | Close the bottom hand first in slow practice; check pads | [TS-06](../troubleshooting/TS-06-finger-leaks-and-transitions.md) |
| Flute wobbles on M | M wavers, airy | Firm thumb support, flute resting against the chin | [TS-01](../troubleshooting/TS-01-no-sound-or-airy-tone.md) |
| Madhya Pa sharp | Tuner reads + 20 cents or more | Roll the flute slightly toward you, slower air | [TS-05](../troubleshooting/TS-05-pitch-out-of-tune.md) |
| Jumping to the third register | High, thin, unexpected note | Much less air speed | [TS-04](../troubleshooting/TS-04-high-notes.md) |

## 10. Quiz

1. What is the fingering for tivra Ma?
2. Which two notes share the fingering `XXX XXX`?
3. What changes when you go from mandra Pa to madhya Pa?
4. Name the thaat whose scale is `S R G M P D N`.
5. Why is `M -> P` the hardest change in the octave?

<details markdown="1"><summary>Answer key</summary>

1. `OOO OOO`, all holes open.
2. Mandra Pa and madhya Pa.
3. Only the air: faster, narrower stream (smaller lip opening). Fingers stay the same.
4. Kalyan.
5. All six fingers move at once (open to closed) **and** the register changes.

</details>
