# L09 - Fingering Sa, Re, Ga

**Objectives:** FNG-01, FNG-02, FNG-06
**Time span:** Days 8-18 (roughly; move on when the mastery criteria are met)
**Prerequisites:** [L04](L04-first-sound.md) (clear Sa), [L06](L06-pulse-and-tempo.md), [L07](L07-long-tones-tuner-and-drone.md)
**Previous:** [L08](L08-recording-and-self-assessment.md) | **Next:** [L10 - Reading and Writing Sargam](L10-reading-and-writing-sargam.md)

---

## 1. Learning objectives

- Read the `X/O/H` fingering notation and the hole numbers (FNG-01).
- Play Sa, Re and Ga cleanly, and move between them in time (FNG-02).
- Make **clean transitions**: all moving fingers move at the same instant, with no "in-between" notes (FNG-06).

## 2. Prerequisites

A clear Sa at least 4 times out of 5, a basic long tone, and clapping in time at 60 BPM.

## 3. Simple explanation

You already play Sa with the top three holes closed. Re and Ga come from **lifting fingers off the top end, one at a time**:

| Note | Fingering | Western (Sa = C) | What changes from Sa |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sa `S` | `XXX OOO` | C | (start) |
| Re `R` | `XXO OOO` | D | lift the ring finger (hole 3) |
| Ga `G` | `XOO OOO` | E | also lift the middle finger (hole 2) |

Each lifted finger makes the air column shorter, so the pitch rises (Fact).

**Why transitions matter:** going from Ga down to Sa means putting **two fingers down at once** (holes 2 and 3). If one lands a split second before the other, you briefly play a third note. That tiny "blip" is the most common reason beginners sound messy. In music for listeners, clean changes matter as much as the notes themselves.

## 4. Step-by-step instructions

### Step 1: Read the chart (FNG-01)

`X` = closed, `O` = open, `H` = half (later). Holes 1-2-3 (top hand), then 4-5-6 (bottom hand). Practise "air fingering": finger these patterns without blowing, saying the note names aloud: `XXX OOO` (Sa), `XXO OOO` (Re), `XOO OOO` (Ga), `XXX XOO` (`.N`, next lesson). Do 3 rounds.

### Step 2: Long tones on Re and Ga

Hold Re for 8 s (tuner: D), then Ga for 8 s (tuner: E). Ga has two holes open and is slightly harder to keep clear. Use slightly less air than for Sa.

### Step 3: Slow changes, slurred

**Slurred** means you don't stop the air between notes; only the fingers change.

1. Play Sa. While the air continues, lift hole 3 for Re. Hold 4 s. Put it back for Sa. Hold 4 s.
2. Repeat 5 times.
3. Same for Re and Ga (lift/lower hole 2).
4. Hardest: Sa and Ga (lift or lower holes 2 and 3 **together**). 10 times.

### Step 4: Finger movement quality (FNG-06)

- Lift fingers only **1-2 cm** above the holes. Far-lifting fingers take longer to come back.
- Move fingers with a **quick, light, decisive** motion, like tapping a phone screen, not slow lowering.
- Keep the fingers you are **not** moving completely still.
- For two-finger moves (Sa and Ga), think "**one motion**", as if the two fingers were glued together.

### Step 5: In time

Metronome at 60 BPM. One note per click, slurred:

```
S R G R | S - - - ||
S R G - | G R S - ||
G R S R | G - G - ||
S G S G | S - - - ||
```

Then at 72 and 80 BPM (metronome ladder, [L01](L01-how-to-practise.md)).

### Step 6: Your first tune

**Mary Had a Little Lamb (opening)** needs S R G P. Pa (`P`) comes in [L14](L14-tivra-ma-and-madhya-pa-dha-ni.md), so for now play only the first two phrases:

```
G R S R | G G G - | R R R - ||
```

Play it at 60 BPM, one note per beat. Repeated notes (like `G G G`) need a small break in the air between them: stop the air for an instant with a tiny "hu" (or a light tongue "tu", see [L13](L13-articulation-tonguing-and-slurs.md)).

### What you should hear

- Three distinct pitches, each stepping up (S to R to G sounds like the first three notes of "do re mi").
- **No extra sound** between notes. Sa to Ga should go straight from one to the other with nothing in between.
- The tone stays the same on all three notes.

## 5. Fingering charts

```
        hole:  1   2   3     4   5   6
 Sa  S   C    [X] [X] [X]   [O] [O] [O]
 Re  R   D    [X] [X] [O]   [O] [O] [O]
 Ga  G   E    [X] [O] [O]   [O] [O] [O]

 Transitions:
   S <-> R : hole 3 only
   R <-> G : hole 2 only
   S <-> G : holes 2 + 3 together  (watch for blips!)
```

## 7. Guided practice (block C, 10-12 min daily)

1. Air fingering: 1 minute.
2. Long tones: Re and Ga, 2 x 8 s each (in block B).
3. Slow slurred changes (Step 3): 5 reps per pair, 10 reps for S-G.
4. In-time patterns (Step 5): each line 3 clean times in a row at the current tempo.
5. Mary (first phrases): 3 clean times in a row.

## 8. Independent exercises

1. Make up 4 lines of your own using only S, R, G, one note per beat, and play them at 60 BPM.
2. Play `S R G R S` with your eyes closed.
3. Record `S G S G S G S` slurred at 60 BPM. Listen at half speed (most players can slow playback) and count the blips.

## 9. Common mistakes and corrections

| Mistake | How you notice | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Blip between S and G | Tiny extra note on the slow recording | "Glued fingers"; move faster and lighter |
| Fingers lifting too high | Late notes; tiring | Hover 1-2 cm above the holes |
| Bottom hand gripping during S-R-G | Tension, flute wobble | Bottom-hand fingers rest lightly; the thumbs support |
| Ga airy or squeaky | Weak tone | Slightly less air; check holes 1 and 3 are sealed |
| Looking at the fingers | Head turns; embouchure moves | Use a mirror instead, or close your eyes |
| Stopping air between slurred notes | Gaps | Keep the air flowing; only fingers move |

More: [TS-06](../troubleshooting/TS-06-finger-leaks-and-transitions.md).

## 10. Performance test

At 60 BPM, record: `S R G R S - - - ||` three times in a row, slurred.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Pass standard</summary>

- All three repetitions have the correct notes and land on the clicks.
- No audible blips on the recording (listen at normal and half speed).
- Rubric: T3 P2 R3 Tr3 or better.

</details>

## 11. Quiz

1. Write the fingerings for Sa, Re and Ga.
2. Which hole or holes change between Sa and Ga, and why is this the hardest change of the three?
3. What does "slurred" mean?
4. How high should your fingers lift above the holes, and why?
5. How do you play two repeated notes, such as `G G`, so that they sound separate?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answer key</summary>

1. Sa `XXX OOO`, Re `XXO OOO`, Ga `XOO OOO`.
2. Holes 2 and 3 together. If one finger lands before the other you briefly play an extra note (a blip), so both fingers must move as one.
3. The air does not stop between notes; only the fingers change.
4. Only about 1-2 cm. Fingers that lift far take longer to come back, which makes notes late and causes blips.
5. Make a tiny break in the air between them with a light "hu" (or a light tongue "tu", see L13).

</details>
