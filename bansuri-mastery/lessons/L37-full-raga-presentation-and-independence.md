## 1. Learning objectives

1. Present a raga in full: alap, jod, slow and fast gat, taans, tihais and a climactic ending (RAG-12).
2. Keep time in vilambit (very slow) laya (RHY-13).
3. Shape a 10-20 minute improvisation with build-up, climax and resolution (IMP-07).
4. Distinguish closely related ragas in playing and listening (RAG-13).
5. Compose and record an original 3-5 minute piece (CMP-05).
6. Design your own 4-week practice plan for any new goal (PRA-08).
7. (Optional) Understand and try circular breathing (BRE-08).

## 2. Prerequisites

You have passed Milestone M9: a 10-15 minute raga performance with alap, bandish, taans and
tihai, and you can accompany a singer.

## 3. Simple explanation

At this stage, the question changes from "Can I play this?" to "What do I want to say, and how
do I build it over 20-30 minutes?" A full raga presentation is like a story:

| Part | Story equivalent |
|---|---|
| Alap | Introducing the world and characters slowly |
| Jod | The plot begins to move |
| Jhala (on bansuri: fast tonguing or breath patterns) | First climax |
| Vilambit gat (slow composition) | A deep, reflective chapter |
| Drut gat (fast composition), taans | Rising action |
| Final tihai, jhala-like ending | Climax and resolution |

Not every performance includes every part. **Fact:** many bansuri concerts present alap, jod,
jhala, then one or two gats; shorter recitals may skip the vilambit gat.

## 4. Step-by-step instructions

### Step 1. Plan the presentation (RAG-12, IMP-07)

For a 25-minute presentation, a possible plan:

| Section | Minutes | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Alap | 6-8 | Stages as in [L31](L31-alap-and-swar-vistar.md), but each stage longer and richer |
| Jod | 3-4 | Steady pulse, phrases develop; gradually faster |
| Jhala | 1-2 | Fast repeated-note patterns (tonguing, double tonguing) around Sa and the raga's notes |
| Vilambit or madhya gat | 6-8 | Composition with improvisation, returning to the mukhda |
| Drut gat with taans | 4-6 | Faster, taans, layakari, tihais |
| Ending | 1 | Final tihai or fast climax to Sa |

Write the plan with **energy levels** from 1 (calm) to 5 (peak). A good plan rises in waves,
not in a straight line.

### Step 2. Jod and jhala on bansuri

- **Jod:** play alap-like phrases with a steady pulse (try 60-80 BPM, no tala). Use more
  repeated notes and rhythmic groupings than in alap.
- **Jhala:** alternate a melody note with repeated Sa (or another drone note), with fast
  tonguing. Example pattern (4 notes per beat): `GSSS RSSS GSSS PSSS`. Use double tonguing
  ([L13](L13-articulation-tonguing-and-slurs.md), FNG-12).

### Step 3. Vilambit laya (RHY-13)

Slow teentaal (for example 30-40 BPM) is hard because each beat is long. Method:

1. Count subdivisions aloud: "1-and-a-uh" (4 per beat).
2. Use a tabla app set to vilambit teentaal. Clap and count for 2 cycles before playing.
3. Learn a vilambit composition from [R-T01 or R-T04](../02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md#5-theory-and-raga-references), or compose one.
4. Practise "filling" long beats with slow meend and swar vistar while always knowing which
   subdivision you are on.
5. Test: play one section with a tabla loop and arrive on sam exactly, 3 cycles in a row.

### Step 4. Similar ragas (RAG-13)

Study pairs. For each pair, learn 3 phrases that only one raga uses, and play them in
alternation.

| Pair | Same notes? | Key differences (Fact, widely taught; details vary by tradition) |
|---|---|---|
| Bhupali vs Deshkar | Yes: S R G P D | Bhupali (Kalyan thaat) centres on the lower half, vadi Ga, phrases like `G R S .D`; Deshkar (Bilawal thaat) centres on the upper half, vadi Dha, avoids Bhupali's `G R S` cadences, phrases like `G P D - P` |
| Yaman vs Yaman Kalyan | Almost | Yaman Kalyan adds shuddha Ma in specific descending phrases around Ga; Yaman uses only tivra Ma (**Disputed:** exactly how shuddha Ma is used varies) |
| Kafi vs Bageshri | Similar notes (komal g, n) | Bageshri centres on Ma, and Pa is weak or omitted in ascent; Kafi uses all seven notes more evenly |
| Khamaj vs Des | Similar notes | Khamaj omits Re in ascent and emphasises Ga; Des emphasises Re and Pa, with phrases like `R m P N S'` |

Use the [raga cards](../ragas/README.md) and [R-T05 Parrikar archive](../02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md#5-theory-and-raga-references)
for deeper study.

### Step 5. An original work (CMP-05)

Compose a 3-5 minute piece with a clear structure, for example:

```
Intro (free, alap-like, 30-45 s)
Theme A (composed, in tala)
Variation / development (partly improvised)
Theme B (contrast: new register or tala)
Return of A, shortened
Ending (tihai or slow final phrase)
```

Notate the composed parts, record the full piece, and write a short programme note (what it is
about, raga or scale used, structure).

### Step 6. Design your own 4-week plan (PRA-08)

Use this template for any new goal (a new raga, a difficult song, faster taans):

```
GOAL (measurable): ________________________________ by date ______
TEST at the end (how I'll know): ____________________________

Week 1  Learn:    ______   Daily drills: ______   Check: recording + rubric
Week 2  Build:    ______   Daily drills: ______   Check: tempo / accuracy target
Week 3  Apply:    ______   (in repertoire / with loop / with others)
Week 4  Perform:  mock performance x2; final test; reflection

Daily session (60 min):
  5 warm-up | 10 tone/long notes | 20 goal work | 15 repertoire review | 10 listening/ear
Review schedule for older material: day 1, 3, 7, 14, 30
Risks / fallback: ________________________________
```

Write one plan now, follow it, and evaluate it after 4 weeks: what worked, what to change.

### Step 7. Circular breathing (BRE-08, optional and Disputed)

**What it is:** breathing in through the nose while air stored in the cheeks keeps the sound
going. **Disputed:** it is rarely central to Hindustani bansuri style; many great players never
rely on it, and phrasing with natural breaths is considered part of the music. Learn it only
if you want it.

Basic method (practise away from the flute first):

1. With a straw in a glass of water, puff your cheeks and blow a steady stream of bubbles.
2. Keep the bubbles going using only the cheek muscles while you sniff in briefly through
   the nose.
3. Return to normal lung air without a gap in the bubbles.
4. Only then try on the flute with a single long note (often easiest on a middle note like `G`).

Stop if you feel dizzy or strain ([TS-07](../troubleshooting/TS-07-breath-dizziness-and-endurance.md)).

### Step 8. Lifelong independence

The final skills are habits:

- **Daily listening** with purpose (a listening log).
- **Regular recording** and honest self-assessment.
- **A teacher or mentor** for periodic checks, even when you are advanced.
- **Learning one new raga, one new song and one new composition per season.**
- **Teaching others** (explaining is a powerful test of understanding).
- **Playing with others** whenever possible.

## 8. Independent exercises

1. Write and follow a 4-week plan (Step 6).
2. Record a 15-minute presentation, then a 25-minute one, 2-3 months apart. Compare.
3. Play contrasting phrases for 2 raga pairs; identify each pair by ear from recordings.
4. Compose, notate and record your original piece (Step 5).

## 9. Common mistakes and corrections

| Mistake | Correction |
|---|---|
| Peaking too early | Plan energy levels; save taar saptak and fast taans for later |
| Losing time in vilambit | Count subdivisions aloud; tabla loop always |
| Repeating the same ideas for 20 minutes | Plan new material for each section; listen to masters for vocabulary |
| Mixing similar ragas | Practise the distinguishing phrases daily |
| Endurance fades (tone collapses late) | Build stamina gradually; rest days; breath work ([TS-07](../troubleshooting/TS-07-breath-dizziness-and-endurance.md)) |
| Practising without a plan | Step 6 template; evaluate every 4 weeks ([TS-11](../troubleshooting/TS-11-practice-plateaus-and-motivation.md)) |

## 10. Quiz

1. List the main sections of a full instrumental raga presentation in order.
2. How is jhala made on bansuri?
3. Name one pair of ragas with the same notes and one difference between them.
4. What five items must a 4-week plan include?

<details markdown="1"><summary>Answer key</summary>

1. Alap, jod, jhala, gat (vilambit and/or madhya/drut) with taans and tihais, ending.
2. Fast repeated-note patterns around Sa or drone notes, using tonguing or double tonguing (or breath patterns).
3. Bhupali and Deshkar: Bhupali centres on the lower half with vadi Ga; Deshkar on the upper half with vadi Dha.
4. A measurable goal, an end test, weekly stages, a daily session layout, and a review schedule (plus risks/fallback).

</details>
