# L08 - Recording and Self-Assessment

**Objectives:** PRF-01, PRF-02, PRA-04, PRA-05
**Time span:** Learn in Week 1; use at least 3 times a week for the whole course
**Prerequisites:** [L04](L04-first-sound.md) (a sound to record), [L07](L07-long-tones-tuner-and-drone.md)
**Previous:** [L07](L07-long-tones-tuner-and-drone.md) | **Next:** [L09 - Fingering Sa, Re, Ga](L09-fingering-sa-re-ga.md)

---

## 1. Learning objectives

- Record your practice with a phone and listen back with specific questions (PRF-01).
- Score recordings with the **standard 5-category rubric** used in every milestone of this curriculum (PRF-02).
- Turn a low score into a diagnosis: find the cause of a problem before you practise a fix (PRA-04).
- Know when and how to get a teacher's check, and prepare for it (PRA-05).

## 2. Prerequisites

You can produce a sound, and you have a recording app ([L00](L00-day-0-your-flute.md)).

## 3. Simple explanation

While you play, your brain is busy with fingers, breath and reading. You hear only part of your sound, and the flute sounds different at your ear than it does a metre away. A recording lets you become **your own listener**. Without a teacher in the room, **the recording is your teacher** (Recommendation).

But listening alone is vague ("it sounds bad"). A **rubric** turns vague impressions into specific scores and specific fixes.

## 4. Step-by-step instructions

### Step 1: How to record (PRF-01)

1. Place the phone **about 1-2 metres away**, in front of you, not right next to the blowing hole (close recording exaggerates breath noise).
2. Use the same position every time so recordings are comparable.
3. Say the date and what you are playing at the start ("30 October, Sa long tones").
4. Record the drone or metronome too if you were using it. It helps you judge pitch and time.
5. Name the file: `YYYY-MM-DD-item.m4a`. Keep a folder per month. Old recordings are your proof of progress; keep them.

### Step 2: How to listen

1. Wait at least a few minutes after playing (a day is even better for milestone recordings).
2. Listen **once all the way through** without stopping, as an audience would.
3. Listen again, **one category at a time**, using the rubric.
4. Write one **"keep"** (something good) and one **"fix"** (the single most important problem) in your log.

### Step 3: Score with the rubric (PRF-02)

Score each category from 1 to 4. Use **half points only** when you truly cannot decide.

## 5. The standard rubric (canonical for all milestones)

| Category | 1 - Not yet | 2 - Developing | 3 - Secure | 4 - Polished |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **T - Tone** | Mostly air; notes fail to sound or crack | Notes sound but are airy or unsteady; frequent wobble | Clear, steady tone on most notes; slight air noise; occasional weak note | Clear, full, even tone on every note and across registers; deliberate tone colour |
| **P - Pitch** | Many notes clearly wrong or far out of tune (beyond +/-40 cents) | Right notes, but several noticeably out of tune (+/-25-40); drone beats audible | Nearly all notes within about +/-15-20 cents; brief, self-corrected slips | All notes locked with the drone, including half-holes and octaves; raga-appropriate intonation |
| **R - Rhythm** | Pulse lost; stops and restarts | Pulse present but speeds up or slows; durations uneven | Steady with the metronome or tala; durations correct; small drifts recovered | Rock-steady; rhythm feels natural; correct sam arrivals and phrasing in time |
| **Tr - Transitions / Technique** | Frequent blips, squeaks, leaks or wrong fingerings | Notes correct, but changes are often unclean (blips between notes, late fingers) | Clean changes; leaps and half-holes mostly clean; occasional blip | Every change clean at the target tempo; articulation and ornaments precise |
| **E - Expression** | No shape; notes are just "produced" | Some phrase shape; breaths in odd places | Clear phrases, sensible breaths, some dynamics or ornament where appropriate | Musical, communicative phrasing; dynamics, ornaments and pacing serve the music and style |

**How milestones use it:** each milestone in [projects/](../projects/README.md) states minimum scores, for example "T3 P3 R3 Tr2 E2". **Expression is scored from M2 onward**; at M1 it is not scored.

**Write scores like this:** `T3 P2 R3 Tr2 E2`.

**Calibration:** have someone else (a teacher, or a musically experienced friend) score the same recording now and then. If their scores differ from yours by more than 1 point in any category, discuss why. Self-scores are usually too generous on pitch and too harsh on tone (Uncertain: a common teacher observation, not a measured fact).

### Listening checklist (use with the rubric)

- [ ] Do notes start cleanly, or with a "pff" or a scoop up to pitch?
- [ ] Does each note stay steady, or does it sag or wobble?
- [ ] Is there air noise around the tone?
- [ ] Between notes, are there tiny extra notes (blips)?
- [ ] Do notes start exactly on the click?
- [ ] Where are the breaths? Are they planned?
- [ ] Against the drone, do I hear beating on any note?
- [ ] Does the piece sound like music or like an exercise?

### Diagnose before you fix (PRA-04)

A low score tells you **what** is wrong, not **why**. A squeak can come from a leaking finger, from blowing too hard or from a tense lip, and each needs a different fix. Practising "harder" at the wrong cause wastes weeks. Use the five-step method from the [troubleshooting guide](../troubleshooting/README.md#2-the-general-diagnosis-method-use-it-for-any-problem):

1. **Describe it exactly.** Not "my Ga is bad" but "Ga cracks about 1 time in 3 when I slur up from Sa at 60 BPM". Take the description from the recording, not from memory.
2. **Isolate it.** Does it happen on one note only? In one register only? On one transition only? Only after 15+ minutes? Only at faster tempos? Each answer narrows the cause. The [diagnostic tree](../troubleshooting/README.md#1-the-diagnostic-tree) points you to the right TS guide.
3. **Pick one hypothesis.** Choose the single most likely cause from that guide. For beginners it is usually simple: a leak, blowing too hard, or tension.
4. **Change one thing only.** Finger pressure, OR air speed, OR flute angle, OR tempo. Change three things at once and you will not know which one helped.
5. **Verify with evidence.** Record again (or use the tuner for pitch). The fix is real only at 5 clean out of 5, on two different days. If it has not worked after about a week of correct practice, go back to step 3 with the next most likely cause.

Write each diagnosis in your log in one line:

```
Problem: Ga cracks 1 in 3 from Sa, slurred, 60 BPM | Isolated: one transition only | Cause tried: ring finger leak (TS-06) | Change: flatter pad on hole 3 | Result: 5/5 on Tue and Thu
```

**Recommendation:** after two hypotheses have failed, stop guessing and take the recordings to a teacher (section 9).

## 6. Watch and listen

- Compare your recording of Sa with the teacher's in [R-V01](../02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md#31-structured-beginner-series). Ignore volume; compare **steadiness** and **clarity**.
- Any time you score yourself 4 in a category, check it against a professional recording ([Listening library](../02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md#9-listening-library)). A 4 should sound in the same family as the professionals in that category.

## 7. Guided practice

1. Today: record 3 long tones on Sa. Listen and score Tone and Pitch only.
2. This week: record once every 2 days. Fill in the "Recording" line of the practice log ([L01](L01-how-to-practise.md)).
3. **Weekly comparison:** every Sunday, listen to this week's best recording next to last week's. Write down one change you can hear.

## 8. Independent exercises

1. Record the same long tone from 30 cm and from 2 m. Notice how breath noise changes.
2. Score one recording, wait 2 days, and score it again without looking at the first scores. Compare.
3. Make a monthly "progress reel": one 20-second clip from each week.

## 9. Getting teacher feedback (PRA-05)

**Why:** text and video can't see your lips or hear your sound. A teacher can spot in one minute what you might not find in a month (Fact for most beginners).

**When (strongly recommended checkpoints):**

| Checkpoint | Before milestone | What to ask |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | [M1](../projects/M1-first-stable-tone.md)/[M3](../projects/M3-middle-octave.md) | Embouchure, hold, tone, posture |
| 2 | [M5](../projects/M5-first-raga-bhupali.md) | Pitch, half-holes, first raga phrasing |
| 3 | [M7](../projects/M7-recital-one.md) | Ornaments, taar saptak, performance |
| Then | Every 2-3 months | Next goals |

**How to find one:** see [Courses and teachers](../02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md#4-courses-and-teachers-paid-or-structured) (R-C01, R-C03 offer online classes). Ask which flute key they teach on. Many teachers use bass flutes, and you need someone comfortable teaching on a C medium (or willing to).

**Prepare:** send or bring 2-3 short recordings scored with the rubric, plus 3 specific questions. Example: "My Ga is airy; is my embouchure too open?"

## 10. Common mistakes and corrections

| Mistake | How you notice | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Recording too close | Exaggerated hiss | 1-2 m away, same place each time |
| Only listening, never scoring | "It sounds bad" with no plan | Use the rubric and write one fix |
| Scoring everything 4 | No visible weaknesses | Compare with a professional recording; ask for calibration |
| Deleting old recordings | No evidence of progress | Keep everything in dated folders |
| Avoiding listening because it's uncomfortable | Few recordings in the log | Normal at first. Use the "keep" and "fix" format to stay constructive |

## 11. Quiz

1. How far should the phone be from you?
2. What are the five rubric categories?
3. A recording has right notes but lots of tiny extra notes between them. Which category scores low?
4. Your Sa is steady but beats against the drone. Which category is affected?
5. When should you get a teacher check?
6. Your recording shows squeaks on several notes, but only after about 20 minutes of playing. What is the likely cause, and what is the one change you try first?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answer key</summary>

1. About 1-2 metres, in front of you.
2. Tone, Pitch, Rhythm, Transitions/Technique, Expression.
3. Transitions/Technique (Tr).
4. Pitch (P).
5. Before M1/M3, before M5, before M7, then every 2-3 months.
6. Fatigue (lips, breath or hands; see TS-07/TS-08). Change one thing only: take a break every 15 minutes, then record again to check whether the squeaks disappear (PRA-04).

</details>

## 12. Mastery criteria

- [ ] 3 or more recordings per week in the log (PRF-01).
- [ ] Rubric used on every recording; scores agree with a second listener within 1 point at least once (PRF-02).
- [ ] Three of your own problems diagnosed in writing with the five-step method, each pointing to a TS guide (PRA-04).
- [ ] Teacher identified (or search started) for checkpoint 1 (PRA-05).
