## 1. Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

1. State the exact range of your C flute in sargam, in Western note names and roughly in Hz.
2. Decide quickly whether a song fits your flute.
3. Play a melody with a **different note acting as Sa** on the same flute ("moving Sa").
4. Transpose (rewrite) a notation into a new key.
5. Pitch-shift a recording so it matches your flute.
6. Explain how flute size affects range, tone and finger stretch, and choose a sensible second flute.
7. Test a flute before buying it.

## 2. Prerequisites

- You can play all 12 notes in the middle octave, including half-holes ([L25](L25-komal-swaras-and-12-notes.md)). Part A needs only the shuddha notes and tivra Ma.
- You can find the Sa of a song by ear ([L18](L18-song-learning-system.md), EAR-10).
- You know that sargam is **relative** (Sa can be any pitch), while C, D, E... are **fixed** pitches ([L05](L05-what-is-music-pitch-sa-sargam.md), THY-03).

## 3. Simple explanation

**Key** means "which pitch is home". When a singer says a song is "in D", the home note (Sa) of
that song is D.

Your flute is built so that `XXX OOO` gives C. That makes C the flute's **natural Sa**. With
Sa = C, the seven shuddha notes are C D E F G A B: the white keys of a piano.

Two problems happen all the time:

1. **The song is in another key.** If a song's Sa is D, playing it with your flute's Sa (C)
   puts every note one whole step too low. It sounds "correct" on its own, but it clashes with
   the recording.
2. **The song goes too high or too low.** Your flute cannot play below mandra Pa (G4). If a
   melody dips below Sa by more than a fifth, the bottom notes simply do not exist.

There are four solutions, used by every bansuri player:

| Solution | What you do | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Change the recording | Pitch-shift the track to C (software) | Easy; the original singer sounds slightly altered |
| Move Sa on your flute | Treat a different hole as Sa | Some notes become half-holes |
| Change octave | Play the song an octave higher or lower | Only works if the whole melody fits |
| Change flute | Use a flute whose natural Sa matches the song | Costs money; each flute feels different |

## 4. Step-by-step instructions

### Part A: Know your range (TRN-01, TRN-05)

**Step 1.** Learn the range chart in section 5 by heart: the lowest note (`.P` = G4), Sa (C5),
taar Sa (C6), your comfortable top (about `G'` = E6) and the practical ceiling (`M'` = F#6).

**Step 2. The song-fit check.** For any song you want to learn:

1. Find the song's Sa by ear and confirm with a tuner ([L18](L18-song-learning-system.md)).
2. While transcribing, write down the **lowest** and **highest** note of the melody *in
   sargam relative to the song's Sa*.
3. Look at the table:

| Lowest note in the song | Highest note in the song | Fits with Sa = C? |
|---|---|---|
| `.P` or higher | `M'` or lower | Yes, comfortably |
| `.P` or higher | `P'` to `S''` | Yes, but the top needs register-3 notes (hard) |
| `.m` or lower | anything | **No.** Move Sa up (Part B) or play an octave higher |

If the whole melody lies between `S` and `S'`, it may also work an octave higher: check that
the new top note is still at or below `M'`.

**Step 3. Know the hard spots (TRN-05).** Plan around these on a C medium flute:

| Hard area | Why | Workaround |
|---|---|---|
| Anything below `.P` | Not physically possible on a 6-hole flute | Octave change, move Sa, or a longer flute |
| `M` (tivra Ma, all open) tone | Least support from the bamboo, often weak | More focused air; accept a lighter tone |
| Fast passages through komal notes | Half-holes take time to place | Slow practice, or choose another Sa |
| Register 3 (`P'` and up) | Fingerings vary and tone is thin | Avoid in early repertoire |
| Very loud `.P` | Low notes crack into the upper octave when pushed | Keep low notes medium-soft |

### Part B: Moving Sa (TRN-02, TRN-04)

**Step 4. Pa as Sa (song in G).** The Western G major scale is G A B C D E F#. On your flute
those pitches are already available with **no half-holes**:

| New sargam (Sa = G) | Pitch | Old sargam (Sa = C) | Fingering |
|---|---|---|---|
| S | G | P | `XXX XXX` |
| R | A | D | `XXX XXO` |
| G | B | N | `XXX XOO` |
| m | C | S' (or S) | `XXX OOO` |
| P | D | R' (or R) | `XXO OOO` |
| D | E | G' (or G) | `XOO OOO` |
| N | F# | M' (or M) | `OOO OOO` |
| S' | G | P' (or P) | `XXX XXX` |

Two choices for where the new Sa sits:

- **New Sa = madhya P (G5).** The new mandra notes come from your old madhya octave: new `.P`
  = old R (D5), new `.D` = old G (E5), new `.N` = old M (F#5). This is the usual choice
  because it gives a full mandra-madhya range.
- **New Sa = mandra `.P` (G4).** You lose the whole lower octave (nothing exists below G4) but
  gain a low, dark sound for melodies that stay above Sa.

> **Fact:** Pa-as-Sa is the most useful "second key" on a C flute. Surprisingly, a major-scale
> (Bilawal) melody is *easier* with Pa as Sa than with the normal Sa, because the new Ma is
> old Sa (no half-hole) and the new Ni is the all-open tivra Ma. Practise the scale
> `S R G m P D N S'` with this mapping until your fingers stop thinking in the old names.

**Step 5. Ma as Sa (song in F).** F major is F G A Bb C D E. On your flute that is
`m P D n S' R' G'` in the old names. You need **two half-holes**: the new Sa itself (`m` =
`HOO OOO`) and the new Ma (`n` = `XXX XHO`). It is possible, but every Sa is a half-hole, so
it is harder. Use it only when needed.

**Step 6. Re as Sa (song in D).** D major is D E F# G A B C#. In old names: `R G M P D N r'`.
Only the new Ni (`r'`, a half-hole) is difficult. Fairly practical.

**Step 7. Transposing notation (TRN-04).** To rewrite a notation from Sa = C to Sa = G on
paper:

1. Convert every sargam note to its Western pitch using the old Sa (C).
2. Convert each pitch back to sargam using the new Sa (G).
3. Check octave marks: notes may cross the Sa boundary and need new dots/apostrophes.
4. Play the result. If it sounds like the same tune, you are done.

Short cut: the **pitches** stay the same; only the names change. C is 5 semitones above G, so
old `S` (C) is called new `m` when Sa = G. Old `P` (G) becomes new `S`. Always check with the
table in Step 4 rather than in your head.

### Part C: Pitch-shifting a recording (TRN-06)

**Step 8.** Find the song's Sa (say it is D). Count the semitones between D and your flute's
Sa (C): D down to C is 2 semitones. Shift the recording **down 2 semitones**. Now the song's
Sa is C and your normal fingering works.

Tools ([section 7 of the library](../02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md#7-apps-and-tools)): Transcribe!
(R-A14) has a pitch control; Audacity (R-A13) has "Change Pitch"; Moises (R-A15) has a key
control.

Rule: **shift by the smaller amount.** Shifting more than 3-4 semitones makes voices sound
unnatural. If the song is in A (3 semitones below C, or 9 above), shift up 3, or move Sa
instead.

### Part D: The flute family and a second flute (INS-08, INS-10)

**Step 9.** Learn how size changes the flute:

| Longer flute (lower key) | Shorter flute (higher key) |
|---|---|
| Deeper, breathier, more "classical" sound | Brighter, lighter, cuts through a band |
| Wider finger stretch (may need the "piper's grip": flat middle finger joints) | Fingers close together |
| Needs more air volume, slower air | Needs faster, more focused air |
| Low notes are slower to respond | Responds quickly, high notes can be shrill |

Names: "medium" and "bass" describe the octave. A **C medium** has Sa = C5. A **C bass** (much
longer) has Sa an octave lower. Hindustani concert performers often play long bass flutes.

**Step 10. Choosing a second flute (Recommendation).** Buy a second flute only after
Milestone M7, when you know what you want to play:

| Your aim | Common choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Classical concert sound | A longer bass flute (E bass and G bass are common concert sizes; check stretch first) | Deeper tone used by most Hindustani performers |
| Songs in sharp keys | A flute a few semitones away from C (for example D or E medium) | Covers keys that need too many half-holes on C |
| Accompanying a particular singer | A flute whose Sa equals the singer's Sa | No moving Sa needed |

> **Uncertain:** which second flute is "best" depends on your hands, your teacher and your
> repertoire. Ask a teacher to watch you try a bass flute before buying one: the stretch can
> cause hand strain ([TS-08](../troubleshooting/TS-08-body-pain-and-tension.md)).

**Step 11. Flute test checklist (INS-10).** Use this when trying any flute:

- [ ] No cracks, splits or loose bindings; cork end sealed ([L00](L00-day-0-your-flute.md))
- [ ] Sa at the stated pitch (tuner, relaxed blowing, after 2 min warm-up)
- [ ] Mandra `.P` sounds easily and does not crack upward
- [ ] Madhya octave in tune: each note within about +/-15 cents
- [ ] Octaves match: `S` vs `S'`, `.P` vs `P` within about +/-15 cents
- [ ] Half-holes `m` and `n` reachable and tunable
- [ ] Taar `S' R' G'` sound without forcing
- [ ] Finger stretch comfortable for 10 minutes
- [ ] Tone: clear, not excessively breathy, similar across registers

Write a one-paragraph verdict.

## 7. Guided practice

| Block | Minutes | Task |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5 | Range check: play `.P` to `M'` slowly at 60 BPM, tuner on, note any weak notes |
| 2 | 10 | Pa-as-Sa scale `S R G m P D N S'` (new names) up and down at 60 BPM, 5 times. Say the **new** names aloud |
| 3 | 10 | Play "Mary had a little lamb" (`G R S R G G G - R R R - G P P -`) with Sa = C, then with Sa = G (Pa as Sa). Same tune, different fingers |
| 4 | 5 | Pitch-shift one song recording and play its first phrase along with it |

Repeat block 2-3 daily for one week, then add Re-as-Sa.

## 8. Independent exercises

1. Take three melodies from [EX-MEL](../exercises/EX-MEL-practice-melodies.md) and play each with Sa = G.
2. Transpose one 8-bar notation from Sa = C to Sa = G **on paper**, then play it. (See [EX-NOT](../exercises/EX-NOT-notation-drills.md).)
3. List 5 songs you want to play. For each, record the song's Sa, its lowest and highest sargam notes, and your decision (normal, moved Sa, pitch-shift, octave change, other flute).
4. Rewrite one passage that goes below `.P` so that it fits, keeping the shape (TRN-05).

## 9. Common mistakes and corrections

| Mistake | Why it happens | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Saying old note names while playing in a moved Sa | Habit | Sing the new names first, then play; write new names on the notation |
| Pitch-shifting in the wrong direction | Counting semitones the wrong way | Always count from the **song's** Sa to C, then check by ear with one note |
| Assuming a song fits because the first line fits | Antara often goes higher | Find the lowest and highest notes of the **whole** song first |
| Buying a bass flute too early | Excitement | Wait until M7; try before buying; check stretch ([TS-08](../troubleshooting/TS-08-body-pain-and-tension.md)) |
| New Sa (half-hole) out of tune | Half-hole coverage drifts | Drone on the new Sa; tuner check every minute at first ([TS-05](../troubleshooting/TS-05-pitch-out-of-tune.md)) |

## 10. Quiz

1. What is the lowest note on your flute in sargam and Western names?
2. A song's Sa is A. Which is easier: shifting the recording up or down to C, and by how much?
3. On a C flute with Pa as Sa, which fingering is the new Ni?
4. A melody's lowest note is `.m`. Does it fit with Sa = C?
5. Name two things that change when you move to a longer flute.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Answer key</summary>

1. `.P` = G4.
2. A up to C is 3 semitones; A down to C is 9 semitones. Shift **up 3**.
3. Old `M` (tivra Ma), all holes open (`OOO OOO`), or `M'` in the upper octave.
4. No. `.m` (F4) is below `.P` (G4). Move Sa up or play an octave higher.
5. Any two of: lower pitch/deeper tone, wider finger stretch, more air volume, slower response in low notes.

</details>
