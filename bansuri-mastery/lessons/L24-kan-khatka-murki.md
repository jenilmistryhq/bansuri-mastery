# L24 - Kan, Khatka and Murki: Grace Notes and Quick Turns

**Objectives:** ORN-03, ORN-04 | **Time span:** kan 2-4 weeks; khatka/murki 1-3 months to be fluent | **Prerequisites:** [L23](L23-meend.md), [L13](L13-articulation-tonguing-and-slurs.md), clean transitions ([FNG-06](../knowledge-tree/D05-FNG-fingering.md))

[Conventions](../05-CONVENTIONS-AND-NOTATION-KEY.md) | [Knowledge tree D11](../knowledge-tree/D11-ORN-ornaments.md)

## 1. Learning objectives

- Play a **kan** (grace note) from above and from below, crisp and light (ORN-03).
- Play **khatka** and **murki** patterns (quick clusters around a note) cleanly at slow and medium speed (ORN-04).
- Place these ornaments inside phrases without disturbing the rhythm.

## 2. Prerequisites

- Clean, synchronised finger changes on all middle-octave notes.
- Meend ([L23](L23-meend.md)), so you already think about **how** notes are approached.

## 3. Simple explanation

### 3.1 Kan

A **kan** (also kan swar, "particle note") is a **very short touch** of another note just
before the main note. It is so quick that the listener hears it as a "flavour" of the main
note, not as a separate note. Written `{G}R` = touch G, land on R.

- **Kan from above:** `{G}R`, `{R}S`, `{S}.N`.
- **Kan from below:** `{S}R`, `{R}G`, `{.N}S`.

### 3.2 Khatka and murki

Both are **quick groups of 3-5 notes** circling around a main note, played inside the time
of that note. Written `(RSNS)` style in this curriculum.

- **Khatka:** usually a more **forceful**, accented cluster.
- **Murki:** usually **lighter and more delicate**, very common in thumri, ghazal, bhajan and
  film songs.

**Disputed:** teachers and books define khatka and murki slightly differently, and the boundary
between them is not fixed. Some use "khatka" for a 4-note turn and "murki" for any fast light
cluster. What matters in practice is to copy the ornament you **hear** in a recording.

### 3.3 Why sequence matters

These ornaments are **fast finger movements**. If your basic transitions leak or blip, the
ornament will sound like a mistake. That is why they come after clean transitions and meend.

## 4. Step-by-step instructions

### 4.1 Kan from above, `{G}R`

1. Finger Re (`XXO OOO`), but start with finger 2 **lifted** (that gives Ga).
2. Start the note and **immediately** (as fast as a blink) drop finger 2. You hear a tiny "G"
   flick into a long R.
3. Practise as "flick-land": the flick is about one-tenth of the time, the landing note
   nine-tenths.

### 4.2 Kan from below, `{S}R`

1. Start with fingers on Sa (`XXX OOO`).
2. At the same instant as the note starts, **lift** finger 3 very quickly. You hear a tiny S
   before R.

### 4.3 Khatka / murki, step by step

Example: murki on Re, `(GRSR)` landing on R.

1. **Slow motion first:** play `G R S R` as 4 equal notes, 1 per beat, at 60 BPM.
2. Then 2 per beat, then 4 per beat: `GRSR` in one beat, followed by a held `R`.
3. Now make the group **light**: the first notes softer and shorter, the arrival note full.
4. Final form: the whole group fits in a fraction of a beat before the held R.

## 5. Diagrams

```
Kan from above, {G}R

 finger 2:  up  |down............................
 sound:     G   |R===============================
            ^ very short

Murki (GRSR) landing on R, at slow, medium, final speed

 Slow:    G    R    S    R   | R - - -
 Medium:  GR   SR            | R - - -
 Final:   (GRSR)R - - - - - - -
```

**Common patterns to learn** (Sa = C, middle octave):

| Ornament | Written | Notes in order |
|---|---|---|
| Kan from above on R | `{G}R` | G (tiny) - R |
| Kan from above on S | `{R}S` | R (tiny) - S |
| Kan from below on G | `{R}G` | R (tiny) - G |
| Kan from above on P | `{D}P` | D (tiny) - P (register 2) |
| Turn on S | `(RS.NS)` | R S .N S |
| Turn on G | `(mGRG)` | m G R G (half-hole `m`; avoid `P` here, because `P` is register 2 and `G` register 1, which is very hard at speed) |
| Turn on R | `(GRSR)` | G R S R |
| Turn on D (Yaman, Bilawal) | `(NDPD)` | N D P D (all register 2) |

## 7. Guided practice (10-12 minutes per day)

| Minutes | Exercise |
|---|---|
| 3 | Kan drill: each kan in the table x 8, with drone, slow |
| 2 | Kan in phrases at 60 BPM: `S {G}R G , P G {G}R S` |
| 4 | Murki ladder: pick one turn; 1 note/beat -> 2 -> 4 -> final form; 3 clean at each step |
| 2 | Put one murki into a melody you know (EX-MEL or a bhajan) |
| 1 | Record one minute and listen for clarity |

Add **one** new pattern per week. Keep the earlier ones in spaced review.

## 8. Independent exercises

1. Ornament an **original** plain phrase in Bhupali in three ways: plain; with kan; with kan and one murki:

```
 plain:   G   R   S   .D |  S   R   G   -
 kan:     G  {G}R S   .D |  S   R  {R}G  -
 murki:   G  (GRSR)R  S  .D |  S   R  {R}G  -
```

2. Find one bhajan or film song phrase with a clear murki. Using [L18](L18-song-learning-system.md), slow it to 50% and write the exact notes of the murki. Verify by playing along slowly.
3. Ear: listen to a ghazal or thumri recording and count the murkis in one minute.

## 9. Common mistakes and corrections

| Mistake | Correction | Guide |
|---|---|---|
| Kan too long (sounds like two notes) | Faster finger flick; think "sparkle" | [TS-06](../troubleshooting/TS-06-finger-leaks-and-transitions.md) |
| Ornament makes you late | Ornament takes time **from** the main note, not extra time | [TS-09](../troubleshooting/TS-09-rhythm-and-tempo.md) |
| Murki notes unclear (smudged) | Go back to 1 note/beat; clean fingers first | [TS-06](../troubleshooting/TS-06-finger-leaks-and-transitions.md) |
| Tense hand | Light fingers; the movement is small | [TS-08](../troubleshooting/TS-08-body-pain-and-tension.md) |
| Over-ornamenting every note | Use only where the style/raga uses it | [L36](L36-style-adaptation.md) |

## 10. Quiz

1. What does `{R}S` mean?
2. Is `{S}R` a kan from above or from below?
3. What is the practical difference between khatka and murki?
4. Does an ornament add extra time to the bar?
5. Why do these ornaments come after clean transitions?

<details markdown="1"><summary>Answer key</summary>

1. A very short touch of R, landing on S (kan from above).
2. From below.
3. Commonly, khatka is more forceful/accented and murki lighter and more delicate; definitions vary (Disputed).
4. No; it takes time from the main note.
5. They are fast finger movements; leaky or unsynchronised fingers would make them sound like mistakes.

</details>
