## 1. Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you can:

- Explain what **articulation** means (how each note starts and how notes connect).
- Play a phrase **slurred** (one breath, fingers change, no new attack).
- Separate notes with a soft **breath attack** ("hu") without the tongue (FNG-16).
- Separate notes with a light **single tongue** "ta" or "da" (FNG-11).
- Choose which of the three to use for a given style.
- (Later, Phase 4) Understand **double tonguing** "ta-ka" for fast repeated notes (FNG-12).

## 2. Prerequisites

- Clean Sa, Re, Ga and mandra Ni, Dha, Pa ([L09](L09-fingering-sa-re-ga.md), [L11](L11-lower-notes-ni-dha-pa.md)).
- You can hold Sa steady for 8 seconds ([L07](L07-long-tones-tuner-and-drone.md)).
- You can play with a metronome ([L06](L06-pulse-and-tempo.md)).

## 3. Simple explanation

**Articulation** is the "consonant" of music. Words have vowels (the long sound) and
consonants (how the sound starts). On the flute the vowel is your steady tone and the
consonant is how the note begins.

There are three main ways to start or connect notes on the bansuri:

| Method | How | Sounds like | Used mostly in |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Slur** | Keep blowing; only the fingers change | One smooth line, like singing one vowel "aaa" while changing notes | Hindustani classical (alap, slow compositions), bhajans |
| **Breath attack** | A small push from the belly, as if whispering "hu" | Soft, gentle separation | Hindustani classical repeated notes; basis of breath gamak later |
| **Tonguing** | The tip of the tongue briefly touches behind the upper teeth, as in "ta" or "da" | Clear, crisp start | Folk tunes, film songs, fast repeated notes, Western music |

**Fact:** Hindustani classical bansuri playing is mostly slurred and breath-articulated,
because it imitates the human voice, which glides between notes.
**Recommendation:** learn all three. Film and folk music use tonguing a lot, and your goals
include both.

**Disputed:** some classical teachers discourage tonguing almost completely; others use it
freely in fast taans. You will hear both on recordings. Neither is "wrong"; it is a style choice.

## 4. Step-by-step instructions

### 4.1 Slur (review and refine)

1. Breathe in low ([L03](L03-breathing-basics.md)).
2. Start Sa with a soft "hu" and keep the air flowing steadily.
3. Change to Re, then Ga, without any change in the air. Only the fingers move.
4. Listen: there should be **no gap** and **no bump** in volume between notes.

### 4.2 Breath attack ("hu")

1. Hold the flute in playing position, fingers on Sa.
2. Whisper "hu" into the flute, then stop the air completely for a moment.
3. Repeat: "hu - hu - hu - hu", one per beat at 60 BPM.
4. The push comes from the belly, **not** from the throat. Put a hand on your belly: it should
   pulse inward slightly with each "hu".
5. Between the notes, the air stops but the lips stay in shape.

### 4.3 Single tonguing ("ta" / "da")

1. Say "ta ta ta ta" out loud. Notice the tip of your tongue touches the ridge just behind the
   upper front teeth.
2. Now whisper "tu" (no voice) while blowing into the flute on Sa.
3. Keep the air **always flowing**. The tongue only interrupts it for an instant, like a tap
   on a water hose.
4. Start with "da" (softer) if "ta" sounds harsh.
5. Practise "da da da da" on Sa at 60 BPM, one note per beat, then two per beat.

### 4.4 Double tonguing (Phase 4 preview, do not drill yet)

Double tonguing alternates the front of the tongue ("ta") with the back of the tongue ("ka"):
"ta-ka-ta-ka". It allows very fast repeated notes. The "ka" stroke is weaker at first, so
the practice is to make "ka" sound exactly like "ta". **Start this only when single tonguing
is even at 4 notes per beat at 60 BPM** (Phase 4, see [Phase 4](../phases/phase-4-intermediate-artistry.md)).

**What you should hear:** tonguing gives a clear "edge" at the start of each note, but the
tone in the middle of the note must sound **the same** as a slurred note. If tonguing makes
the tone thin or airy, your tongue is too heavy or you are stopping the air.

## 7. Guided practice (12 minutes per day, inside your technique block)

| Minutes | Exercise | Tempo |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Slurred `S R G R S .N S` x 4 | 60 BPM, 1 note/beat |
| 3 | Breath attacks: `S S S S | R R R R | G G G G | R R R R | S - - -` x 3 | 60 BPM |
| 3 | Single tongue ("da"): same line x 3 | 60 BPM |
| 2 | Single tongue, 2 notes per beat: `SS RR GG RR | S - - -` x 4 | 60 -> 72 BPM |
| 2 | Mixed: play [L12](L12-alankars.md) alankar 1 slurred once, tongued once, breath-attacked once | 60 BPM |

Record the mixed exercise once per week and compare the three versions.

## 8. Independent exercises

1. Play "Mary Had a Little Lamb" (`G R S R G G G - | R R R - | G P P -`, see
   [EX-MEL](../exercises/EX-MEL-practice-melodies.md)) three ways: fully slurred; tongued only on repeated notes;
   fully tongued. Decide which sounds best and write why in your log.
2. Repeated-note ladder: tongue `S` 4 times per beat. Start at 50 BPM. Add 4 BPM only after 3
   clean runs. Stop at the speed where tone suffers.
3. Listen to one folk/film bansuri recording and one classical alap. Write which articulation
   each mostly uses.

## 9. Common mistakes and corrections

| Mistake | What you hear | Correction | Guide |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stopping the air with the tongue for too long | Choppy, gaps between notes | Think "tap" not "block"; keep air pressure constant | [TS-06](../troubleshooting/TS-06-finger-leaks-and-transitions.md) |
| Tongue moves the lips | Tone changes or cracks after each tongued note | Keep the tongue movement small and behind the teeth; mirror check | [TS-01](../troubleshooting/TS-01-no-sound-or-airy-tone.md) |
| Breath attack from the throat ("kh" sound, coughing) | Harsh click | Belly push, open throat, "hu" not "uh" | [TS-07](../troubleshooting/TS-07-breath-dizziness-and-endurance.md) |
| Fingers and tongue not together | A wrong short note at each change | Practise slowly; the finger must arrive **before** the tongue | [TS-06](../troubleshooting/TS-06-finger-leaks-and-transitions.md) |
| Overblowing on the attack | Note jumps to the upper octave briefly | Softer attack; slower air | [TS-02](../troubleshooting/TS-02-squeaks-and-wrong-octave.md) |

## 10. Quiz

1. Which articulation is most typical in a Hindustani alap?
2. Where does the tongue touch for "ta"?
3. Should the air stop completely between tongued notes?
4. When should you start double tonguing?
5. You hear a short extra note each time you tongue a new note. What is the likely cause?

<details markdown="1"><summary>Answer key</summary>

1. Slurs (and gentle breath attacks).
2. The ridge just behind the upper front teeth.
3. No. The air keeps flowing; the tongue only interrupts it for an instant.
4. In Phase 4, after single tonguing is even at 4 notes per beat at 60 BPM.
5. Fingers and tongue are not synchronised; the finger arrives late.

</details>

**Performance test:** record `S S R R G G R R | S - - -` at 80 BPM tongued, then slurred.

## 11. Mastery criteria

- [ ] FNG-11: `S S R R G G` at 80 BPM, each note separated, tone unchanged (rubric Tone 3+).
- [ ] FNG-16: repeated `S S S S` separated with breath only, clean, at 60 BPM.
- [ ] You can explain when you would choose each method.
- [ ] (Phase 4) FNG-12: 4 repeated notes per beat at 80 BPM with "ta-ka", even.
