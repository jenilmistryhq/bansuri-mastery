## 1. Learning objectives

1. Count semitones between any two swaras and name the interval in Western terms.
2. Build the major scale (= Bilawal) from its step pattern.
3. Read notes on the treble staff in your flute's range.
4. Read note and rest durations, time signatures (4/4, 3/4, 6/8) and simple syncopation.
5. Read the key signatures of C, G and F major.
6. Convert staff notation to sargam (and back) for a given Sa.
7. Match the main thaats with Western modes.
8. Keep a personal notation book (paper, text file or MuseScore).

## 2. Prerequisites

You know the 12 notes in sargam ([L25](L25-komal-swaras-and-12-notes.md)) and that on your
flute Sa = C ([conventions](../05-CONVENTIONS-AND-NOTATION-KEY.md)).

## 3. Simple explanation

**Recommendation:** you do not need Western notation to become an excellent Hindustani
bansuri player. You need it to: read sheet music for films, Western songs and fusion; talk to
keyboard, guitar and band musicians; use apps like MuseScore. This lesson gives you the
essentials only.

Two ideas link the systems:

- **Western note names are fixed pitches.** C is always C.
- **Sargam is relative.** Sa is whatever the home note is. On your flute, Sa = C, so with no
  key change: `S R G m P D N` = C D E F G A B.

## 4. Step-by-step instructions

### Step 1. Semitones and intervals (THY-05)

A **semitone** (half step) is the smallest step in the 12-note system (for example `S` to `r`).
A **whole tone** (whole step) is two semitones (`S` to `R`).

| Semitones from Sa | Swara | Western interval name | Pitch on your flute |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | S | Unison | C |
| 1 | r | Minor 2nd | C#/Db |
| 2 | R | Major 2nd | D |
| 3 | g | Minor 3rd | D#/Eb |
| 4 | G | Major 3rd | E |
| 5 | m | Perfect 4th | F |
| 6 | M | Tritone (augmented 4th) | F#/Gb |
| 7 | P | Perfect 5th | G |
| 8 | d | Minor 6th | G#/Ab |
| 9 | D | Major 6th | A |
| 10 | n | Minor 7th | A#/Bb |
| 11 | N | Major 7th | B |
| 12 | S' | Octave | C (higher) |

Practice: name the interval from `G` to `D` (count: G=4, D=9, 9-4 = 5 semitones = perfect
4th).

### Step 2. The major scale = Bilawal (THY-06)

The major scale has this step pattern: **W W H W W W H** (W = whole tone, H = semitone).

```
S --W-- R --W-- G -H- m --W-- P --W-- D --W-- N -H- S'
```

That is exactly Bilawal thaat. Starting the same pattern on G gives G A B C D E F#: this is
why the key of G needs an F# (tivra Ma on your flute, see [L26](L26-keys-transposition-and-limits.md)).

### Step 3. The treble staff (NOT-06)

Five lines. Notes sit on lines or in spaces. The treble clef (G clef) curls around the 2nd line,
which is G4.

```
                                         C6 -- 2nd ledger line  ---o---
                               B5 (space above 1st ledger)
                     A5 -- 1st ledger line --o--
 ----------------------------------------------------------  F5 (5th line)
                                                              E5 (4th space)
 ----------------------------------------------------------  D5 (4th line)
                                                              C5 (3rd space)  <- your Sa
 ----------------------------------------------------------  B4 (3rd line)
                                                              A4 (2nd space)
 ----------------------------------------------------------  G4 (2nd line)    <- your lowest note .P
                                                              F4 (1st space)
 ----------------------------------------------------------  E4 (1st line)
```

Memory aids: lines from bottom E G B D F ("Every Good Boy Does Fine"); spaces F A C E.

Your flute's range on the staff: from G4 (2nd line) up to F#6 (above the 3rd ledger line, with
a sharp). Notes above the staff use short extra lines called **ledger lines**: A5 on the 1st,
C6 on the 2nd, E6 on the 3rd.

**Sharp** (#) raises a note one semitone; **flat** (b) lowers it one semitone; **natural**
cancels them.

### Step 4. Durations (NOT-06)

In 4/4 time a **beat** is a quarter note.

| Note | Beats | ASCII sargam equivalent (one note, Sa) |
|---|---|---|
| Whole note | 4 | `S - - -` |
| Half note | 2 | `S -` |
| Quarter note | 1 | `S` |
| Eighth note | 1/2 | `SR` (two in one beat) |
| Sixteenth note | 1/4 | `SRGm` (four in one beat) |
| Dotted note | 1.5 x its value | dotted half = `S - -` |

Every note has a matching **rest** (silence) of the same length, written `_` in our ASCII.

### Step 5. Time signatures and syncopation (RHY-12)

| Time signature | Means | Indian relative |
|---|---|---|
| 4/4 | 4 quarter-note beats per bar | Keherwa (8 beats) = 2 bars of 4/4 |
| 3/4 | 3 quarter-note beats per bar | Waltz feel; some film songs |
| 6/8 | 6 eighth notes per bar, felt as 2 groups of 3 | Dadra (6 beats, 3+3) |

**Syncopation** means accents falling *between* beats or on weak beats. Clap this in 4/4,
counting "1 and 2 and 3 and 4 and" and clapping only the bold syllables:

```
1   and  2   and  3   and  4   and
X        .   X    .   X    .
```

(Claps on 1, the "and" after 2, and the "and" after 3.) Film songs use this constantly.

### Step 6. Key signatures (THY-10)

The sharps or flats at the start of each line apply to the whole piece:

| Key | Signature | Sa | Sargam on your C flute |
|---|---|---|---|
| C major | none | C | normal, Sa = C |
| G major | 1 sharp (F#) | G | Pa as Sa, no half-holes |
| F major | 1 flat (Bb) | F | Ma as Sa, two half-holes |

**Key** = which note is home. **Major and minor chords:** a major triad stacks Sa-Ga-Pa
(0-4-7 semitones); a minor triad stacks Sa-komal Ga-Pa (0-3-7). You will hear them in
[EX-EAR](../exercises/EX-EAR-ear-training-system.md) Level 8.

### Step 7. Modes and thaats (THY-11)

| Thaat | Notes | Western mode |
|---|---|---|
| Bilawal | S R G m P D N | Ionian (major) |
| Kalyan | S R G M P D N | Lydian |
| Khamaj | S R G m P D n | Mixolydian |
| Kafi | S R g m P D n | Dorian |
| Asavari | S R g m P d n | Aeolian (natural minor) |
| Bhairavi | S r g m P d n | Phrygian |
| Bhairav, Poorvi, Marwa, Todi | (see [L19](L19-what-is-a-raga.md)) | No standard church mode |

**Fact:** a mode is a scale; a raga is much more (see [L19](L19-what-is-a-raga.md)). Yaman
is not "just Lydian".

### Step 8. Converting between staff and sargam (NOT-07)

1. Write the Western letter under each staff note.
2. Decide the Sa (from the key signature and the home note).
3. Convert letters to sargam relative to that Sa, with octave marks.
4. Convert durations to beats, then to ASCII rhythm.

Reverse the steps to go from sargam to staff.

### Step 9. Your notation book (NOT-10)

Keep every piece you learn in one place: a paper notebook, a text file per piece, or MuseScore
(R-A16) for staff notation. Include: title, source, Sa, tala/meter, tempo, notation, date
learned, and notes on difficult spots.

## 8. Independent exercises

Convert these to sargam (Sa = C unless stated). Durations: q = quarter, h = half, e = eighth.

1. C(q) D(q) E(q) C(q) | E(q) F(q) G(h)
2. G(q) G(q) A(q) G(q) | C5(q) B4(h) (key of C)
3. In G major: G(q) A(q) B(q) G(q) | D5(h) D5(h)
4. Sargam to letters, Sa = C: `P D N S' | N D P -`
5. Count semitones: `R` to `P`; `g` to `n`; `.N` to `G`.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Answer key</summary>

1. `S R G S | G m P -` (note `m` = F, half-hole on your flute)
2. Starting G4 = `.P`: `.P .P .D .P | S .N -` (C5 is Sa, B4 is `.N`)
3. With Sa = G: `S R G S | P - P -` (on your C flute: G4 `XXX XXX`, A `XXX XXO`, B `XXX XOO`, D5 `XXO OOO`)
4. G A B C(6) | B A G - (C6 is taar Sa)
5. R(2) to P(7) = 5 semitones (perfect 4th); g(3) to n(10) = 7 semitones (perfect 5th); `.N` (-1) to G (4) = 5 semitones.

</details>

More drills: [EX-THY](../exercises/EX-THY-theory-quizzes.md) and [EX-NOT](../exercises/EX-NOT-notation-drills.md).

## 9. Common mistakes and corrections

| Mistake | Correction |
|---|---|
| Thinking C = Sa always | C = Sa only when the song is in C (and on your flute's normal fingering) |
| Forgetting the key signature halfway through | Circle every affected note on the page in pencil |
| Counting intervals by letters instead of semitones | Use the semitone table; E to F is only 1 semitone |
| Reading ledger lines wrong | Count lines up from the top line (F5): 1st ledger = A5, 2nd = C6 |
| Rushing syncopated notes | Count "and" aloud; use [EX-RHY](../exercises/EX-RHY-rhythm-drills.md) ([TS-09](../troubleshooting/TS-09-rhythm-and-tempo.md)) |

## 10. Quiz

1. How many semitones are in a perfect 5th? Which swara is it from Sa?
2. Write the major-scale step pattern.
3. Which note is on the 2nd line of the treble staff, and what is it on your flute?
4. What does 6/8 mean, and which tala feels similar?
5. Which thaat matches Dorian mode?

<details markdown="1"><summary>Answer key</summary>

1. 7; Pa.
2. W W H W W W H.
3. G4; mandra Pa (`.P`, all holes closed).
4. Six eighth notes per bar felt as 3+3; Dadra.
5. Kafi.

</details>
