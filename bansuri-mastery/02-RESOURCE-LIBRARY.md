# Resource Library

Researched 2026-10-29. Every link below was checked at that time. Links change, so if one is
dead, search for the exact title and creator.

## How to read this library

**Verification status**

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **V-page** | The page was fetched and its content matched the description. |
| **V-title** | For YouTube: the exact title and channel were confirmed through YouTube's oEmbed service, but **nobody watched the video** for this curriculum. Quality is not assessed. |
| **Seen** | The title and URL appeared in search results but the page was not opened. Treat with more caution. |

**Quality notes** describe only what was actually seen. "Not assessed" means exactly that.
A video in this list is **not** a recommendation that its teaching is correct. Always compare
it with the lessons in this curriculum, and when they disagree, see
[Known disagreements](#known-disagreements-between-sources).

**Important:** a video is not a lesson. Each original lesson in [lessons/](lessons/README.md) tells you
which resources to use, what to watch for, and what to practise after watching.

## 1. Your instrument: verified facts

| Fact | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|
| A C natural medium bansuri is about 19 in (48 cm) long. Retailers list roughly 45-51.5 cm depending on bamboo. | [flute99.com C natural medium](https://flute99.com/) product description, Musicmaster.in, retailer listings | V-page (flute99, Musicmaster); Seen (others) |
| Sa (top 3 holes closed) is **C5, about 523.25 Hz**, tuned to A = 440 Hz. | flute99.com ("tuned to sound C5 pitch (523.26 Hz) when the upper three holes are closed") | V-page. Only one maker named the octave, but acoustics agree (lowest note G4). Confirm with your tuner on Day 0. |
| All holes open = tivra Ma; shuddha Ma = half-cover hole 1 | [R-W02](#2-written-lessons-and-fingering-charts) bansuriflute.co.uk advanced page ("Tivra ma, with no holes covered on flute, is sharp"; shuddha Ma by "covering only half of the Ga hole"); [R-W03](#2-written-lessons-and-fingering-charts) notesandsargam | V-page |

## Known disagreements between sources

1. **The all-open note.** Some sources call it just "Ma". On a standard 6-hole bansuri it is
   **tivra Ma** (R-W02 states this). Shuddha Ma needs a half-hole. Some videos for other
   flutes reverse this; ignore them for your flute.
2. **Where sources start counting.** Some describe fingering from "all closed = Pa", others
   from "3 closed = Sa". These are the same system. One site (Radhe Flutes) uses "all closed
   = Sa", which conflicts with the standard and is excluded.
3. **Number of thaats.** Bhatkhande defined 10. 32 are theoretically possible, and some
   ragas (for example Jaijaiwanti, Kirwani, Charukesi) fit none of them neatly.
4. **Notation symbols.** Komal is underlined in Bhatkhande but lowercase in many ASCII
   systems (including this curriculum). Tivra Ma is a vertical stroke (Bhatkhande), "Ma+"
   (SwarGanga) or `M` (this curriculum). Octave dots are consistent everywhere.
5. **Raga times.** Yaman: "first quarter of the night" (Wikipedia), "about 9 pm to midnight"
   (attributed to The Raga Guide), 19:00-22:00 (ITC SRA).
6. **Teentaal theka.** The final vibhags vary by gharana.
7. **Octave of a flute's Sa.** FluteSangam's tuner table (R-A18) lists C Natural medium at 261.63 Hz (C4). A maker's statement (section 1) and the physics of a 19-inch tube give C5 (523.25 Hz). This curriculum uses C5. The swara name and cents are unaffected; only the octave label and reference tone differ.
8. **Oiling bamboo flutes.** Makers differ. Follow your maker's advice (see [L00](lessons/L00-day-0-your-flute.md)).
