# Song Suggestions for a C Bansuri

Songs to learn **by ear** with the [song-learning system](lessons/L18-song-learning-system.md),
sorted by when your skills allow them. Researched 2026-10-29.

**No notation is given here.** Film songs are copyrighted, and working out the notes yourself is
the skill this curriculum trains. For each song you get: its range, the special notes it needs,
whether it fits your flute, and the earliest lesson after which it is realistic.

## How the "fit" was worked out

On a C flute you play every song **with Sa = C**, whatever key the original recording is in. To
play along with the recording, pitch-shift it to C ([L26](lessons/L26-keys-transposition-and-limits.md)).
So what matters is the song's **range relative to its own Sa**:

| Position | Fits if the song's notes stay within | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Normal (Sa = C)** | `.P` to `G'` (comfortable); `M'` is the ceiling | The default for everything below |
| **Pa-as-Sa** (mandra Pa, G4, as Sa; [L26](lessons/L26-keys-transposition-and-limits.md), Phase 4) | `S` to about `D'`, never below Sa | The song's shuddha `m` becomes an open note (no half-hole). Its komal `n` becomes a half-hole `m` |

**Earliest lesson** = the lesson that unlocks the hardest note the song needs: `.P .D .N` [L11](lessons/L11-lower-notes-ni-dha-pa.md),
`M` and anything up to `S'` [L14](lessons/L14-tivra-ma-and-madhya-pa-dha-ni.md), shuddha `m` [L16](lessons/L16-shuddha-ma-and-half-holing.md),
`R'` and above [L22](lessons/L22-taar-saptak.md), komal `r g d n` [L25](lessons/L25-komal-swaras-and-12-notes.md). The full song-learning method itself
comes in [L18](lessons/L18-song-learning-system.md), so treat L18 as the minimum for any song here.

## Data quality: read before choosing

- **Ranges** come from [Notes & Sargam](https://notesandsargam.com/) ([R-S01](02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md#8-song-notation-sources)), a
  self-taught author's site. They were read through page summaries, and they sometimes include
  instrumental preludes or interludes, not only the vocal line.
- **Original keys** come from automatic key-detection databases (tunebat, songbpm, musicstax). These
  often disagree with each other and with the notation site. Treat any key as approximate, and
  **always find Sa by ear** ([L18](lessons/L18-song-learning-system.md) Step 2).
- **Confidence:** **High** = range has clear octave marks and sources agree; **Medium** = range
  read but with a caveat; **Low** = conflicting or unclear data, so check the whole range by ear first.
- Before committing to any song, **check its lowest and highest notes yourself** (L18 range
  check). If a song turns out not to fit, that check is a successful use of the method, not a failure.

## Probably does NOT fit a C medium flute

**Caution (Uncertain):** both ranges below are unusually wide for a sung melody (about two
octaves). The source may include instrumental passages or mark octaves wrongly. Map the vocal
line's range by ear before deciding. If it is narrower, the song may fit after all.

| Song | Why (as reported) | What you can do |
|---|---|---|
| Lag Jaa Gale (Woh Kaun Thi, 1964; Madan Mohan; Pahadi) | Range `.P` to `D'`: too low for Pa-as-Sa, and too high for the normal position (`D'` is above `G'`) | Play the mukhda only, or wait for Phase 5 third-register work. A lower flute would not help: the problem is the width of the range, not the key |
| Abhi Na Jao Chhod Kar (Hum Dono, 1961; Jaidev) | Range `S` to `N'`: beyond `G'` (normal) and beyond `D'` (Pa-as-Sa) | Mukhda only; or an arrangement that moves the highest phrase down an octave (L26 transposition, Phase 4) |
