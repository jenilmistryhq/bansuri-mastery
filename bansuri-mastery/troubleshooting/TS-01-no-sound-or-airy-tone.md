# TS-01: No Sound, or an Airy (Hissy) Tone

[Troubleshooting home](README.md) | Related: [TON-01](../knowledge-tree/D03-TON-tone.md), TON-02, TON-03, TON-05 | Lessons: [L04](../lessons/L04-first-sound.md), [L07](../lessons/L07-long-tones-tuner-and-drone.md)

## Symptoms

- You blow and hear only air, like wind across a bottle without the note.
- A faint note appears, but it is buried under a loud "shhh" hiss.
- The note comes and goes as you hold it.
- The tone was fine yesterday but airy today.

This is the **most common problem in the first two weeks**. It is normal. Almost everybody
needs several days, and some people need two or three weeks, before Sa sounds reliably.

## Quick checks (30 seconds each)

1. **Mirror check.** Look at your lips while blowing. Is the opening small and centred over
   the blowing hole? Are your cheeks puffed? (They should not be.)
2. **Hole coverage check.** Without blowing, look at how much of the blowing hole your lower
   lip covers. Roughly a quarter to a third is a common starting point (this varies by
   player and flute).
3. **Hand-in-front check.** Put your palm 10 cm in front of your mouth and blow as you do
   for the flute. You should feel a narrow, focused stream, not a wide warm breath.
4. **Roll check.** Roll the flute slowly towards you, then away, while blowing. Does a clear
   note appear at one angle?
5. **Fingers check.** Play Sa (`XXX OOO`) only. If Sa sounds but other notes do not, the
   problem is fingers (see TS-06), not tone.

## Causes, most likely first

| # | Cause | How to recognise it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Air aimed wrongly** (too far into the hole or over the top of it) | Pure air noise; a note appears suddenly when you roll the flute |
| 2 | **Lip opening too large** | Lots of hiss; you run out of breath in 3-4 seconds |
| 3 | **Too much or too little of the hole covered by the lower lip** | Too much: weak, thin, choked note. Too little: airy, loud hiss |
| 4 | **Blowing too hard** | A harsh rush of air, sometimes a squeak (see TS-02) |
| 5 | **Tense lips or jaw** | Lips feel tight; tone is thin and unsteady |
| 6 | **Finger leaks** (when Sa is fine but other notes are airy) | See TS-06 |
| 7 | **Dry lips or flute moving on the lip** | Tone changes as the flute slips |
| 8 | **A crack or a moved cork** (rare) | Suddenly airy on all notes after being fine; see TS-12 |

## Fixes and drills

### Fix A: Find the sweet spot (5 minutes, daily until solved)

1. Hold the flute with **only the top three holes closed** (Sa). Ignore the bottom hand at
   first if you need to; support the flute with the bottom hand's thumb.
2. Say the syllable **"pooh"** softly and let it turn into a gentle, steady blow.
3. Blow a slow, steady stream for 3 seconds while **slowly rolling the flute** a few degrees
   towards you and away. Listen for the moment the note "lights up".
4. Stop, breathe, and repeat 10 times. Each time, try to start at the angle where it lit up.
5. Then do 10 attempts where you **start the note directly** at that angle.

**Goal:** a clear Sa on 4 attempts out of 5 ([TON-03](../knowledge-tree/D03-TON-tone.md)).

### Fix B: The bottle drill (3 minutes)

Practise on the mouth of an empty glass bottle. Blowing across a bottle needs the same
narrow stream and angle as the bansuri, and it is easier. When you can make the bottle sing
3 times in a row, go back to the flute.

### Fix C: Shrink the lip opening (3 minutes)

Make a gentle smile, then bring the lip corners slightly forward until the opening is like a
small grain of rice. Blow at the palm of your hand to feel the narrow stream. Then try Sa
again. **Do not pinch hard**: the lips stay soft.

### Fix D: Blow softer, not harder (2 minutes)

Play Sa as **softly** as you can while it still sounds. Most beginners blow far too hard.
A soft but clear note is a better starting point than a loud airy one.

### Fix E: Long tones to clean the tone (5-10 minutes daily, from Week 2)

Once Sa sounds, hold it for 4, 6, then 8 seconds, listening only to **the amount of hiss**.
Make tiny changes of angle and lip opening to reduce it. Then do the same on Re and Ga. See
[L07](../lessons/L07-long-tones-tuner-and-drone.md).

## How to verify the fix

- Record 5 attempts at Sa. Count clear starts: target **4/5**, then **5/5**.
- For airiness, compare a recording from today with one from a week ago at the same volume.
  Use the tone row of the rubric in [L08](../lessons/L08-recording-and-self-assessment.md).
- A tuner showing a steady reading means the tone is stable; a jumping or missing reading
  means it is not.

## When to seek a teacher

- After **3 weeks** of daily practice you still cannot produce Sa on most attempts.
- You get sound only with a strange posture (head twisted, flute far down the chin).
- Your lip shape is unusual (for example a pronounced lip "tear drop" in the middle), and
  standard advice does not work. A teacher can adjust the flute position for you in minutes.
