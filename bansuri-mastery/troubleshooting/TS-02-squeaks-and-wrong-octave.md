# TS-02: Squeaks and Notes Jumping to the Wrong Octave

[Troubleshooting home](README.md) | Related: [TON-04](../knowledge-tree/D03-TON-tone.md), TON-07, [FNG-06](../knowledge-tree/D05-FNG-fingering.md), BOD-04 | Lessons: [L04](../lessons/L04-first-sound.md), [L09](../lessons/L09-fingering-sa-re-ga.md), [L14](../lessons/L14-tivra-ma-and-madhya-pa-dha-ni.md)

## Symptoms

- A sudden high, thin squeal while playing.
- You aim for mandra Pa (`XXX XXX`) and get madhya Pa instead (an octave higher).
- You aim for Sa and the note "flips" up to taar Sa.
- A note starts correctly, then breaks upward.
- Notes go the other way: you want madhya Pa and get mandra Pa (this is the reverse
  problem; see TS-04).

## Quick checks

1. **Softness test.** Play the same note at half your usual breath. If the squeak disappears,
   you were blowing too hard or too fast (cause 1).
2. **Seal test.** Play the note, then press each finger slightly more firmly in turn. If
   pressing one finger cures it, that finger was leaking (cause 2).
3. **Which notes?** Squeaks on low notes (`.D`, `.P`) point to air speed or leaks; squeaks on
   any note point to the lip or air.
4. **Tongue test.** Do squeaks happen only at the start of notes? Then it is the attack
   (cause 4).

## Causes, most likely first

| # | Cause | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Air too fast or too forceful** | Faster air makes the flute jump to the next register. This is exactly how you *deliberately* play madhya Pa; doing it by accident is a squeak. |
| 2 | **A leaking finger** | A tiny gap under a finger, especially on holes 4-6, makes the flute unstable and it jumps. |
| 3 | **Lip opening too small/tight** | A tight, pinched opening speeds the air up. |
| 4 | **Explosive attack** | Starting with a hard "TU!" or a burst of breath kicks the note up. |
| 5 | **Flute rolled outward** | Uncovering more of the blowing hole raises pitch and can flip the register. |
| 6 | **Fingers moving at different times** | During a note change, a half-made fingering for a moment can squeak (see TS-06). |

## Fixes and drills

### Fix A: The "slow warm air" drill (5 minutes)

1. Imagine fogging a cold mirror with warm breath: "haaah". That is the slow air of the
   lower register.
2. Play Sa with that slow warm air for 4 seconds. Then `.N`, `.D`, `.P`, each 4 seconds.
3. If a note jumps up, stop, relax, and use **even slower** air with a slightly bigger,
   relaxed lip opening.
4. 3 rounds.

### Fix B: Register control drill (5 minutes, from Phase 2)

Finger mandra Pa (`XXX XXX`). Blow slow warm air: low Pa. Without moving the fingers,
gradually speed up the air and narrow the lips until it flips to madhya Pa. Slow down again
until it falls back. Do it 10 times. **Learning to cause the jump deliberately is the best
way to stop it happening by accident.** Repeat on Dha and Ni fingerings.

### Fix C: Seal check routine (2 minutes)

Play `.P` and hold it. Lift and replace one finger at a time, from hole 6 to hole 1, and
listen for squeaks or wobbles as each finger returns. A finger that causes a wobble when
placed back is not sealing well. Work on that finger's position ([L02](../lessons/L02-posture-and-holding.md),
[TS-06](TS-06-finger-leaks-and-transitions.md)).

### Fix D: Gentle attack (3 minutes)

Start notes with a soft "doo" or with breath alone ("hoo") instead of a hard "TU". Play
`S - S - R - R -` at 60 BPM with soft starts. See [L13](../lessons/L13-articulation-tonguing-and-slurs.md).

## How to verify the fix

- 10 attempts at `.P` with no squeak: target **10/10** on two days in a row.
- A 1-minute recording of an alankar with **no** squeaks.
- If squeaks happen only in the last 10 minutes of a session, the fix is rest, not technique.

## When to seek a teacher

- Squeaks persist on every note even at very soft air: your lip position may need adjustment.
- You cannot ever get mandra Pa without it flipping, after checking seals carefully. The
  flute could have a leak or a defect (see TS-12); a teacher or the maker can test it.
