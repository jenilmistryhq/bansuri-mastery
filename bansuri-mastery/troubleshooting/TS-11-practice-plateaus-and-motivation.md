# TS-11: Plateaus, Low Motivation and Unproductive Practice

[Troubleshooting home](README.md) | Related: [PRA-01](../knowledge-tree/D19-PRA-practice.md) to PRA-08 (especially PRA-06 goals and plateaus), [PRF-03](../knowledge-tree/D18-PRF-performance.md) | Lessons: [L01](../lessons/L01-how-to-practise.md), [L08](../lessons/L08-recording-and-self-assessment.md)

## Symptoms

- You practise every day but feel nothing has improved for 2-4 weeks or more.
- You keep playing the same pieces you already know instead of new material.
- You skip practice or dread it.
- You jump between many videos and songs and finish nothing.
- You feel behind "where you should be".

**Plateaus are normal.** Skill grows in steps: long flat periods, then sudden jumps. Often
the improvement is happening (for example tone getting more stable) but is too gradual
to notice day to day. The fix is evidence and a changed approach, not just more hours.

## Quick checks

1. **Compare recordings.** Listen to a recording from 4 weeks ago and one from today, same
   exercise. Is there truly no difference? Often there is.
2. **Read the log.** Is your practice balanced (tone, technique, repertoire, ear), or mostly
   one thing?
3. **Difficulty check.** Is your current material too easy (boring, no growth) or far too
   hard (frustrating, no success)? Aim for tasks you succeed at about 70-80% of the time.
4. **Sleep, stress, health.** These strongly affect motor learning and motivation.

## Causes, most likely first

| # | Cause |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Mindless repetition**: playing through pieces instead of working on specific problems |
| 2 | **No measurement**: progress is invisible without recordings, tempos and scores |
| 3 | **Wrong difficulty**: too easy or too hard |
| 4 | **Imbalanced practice**: only songs, or only exercises |
| 5 | **Unrealistic expectations**: comparing yourself to professionals or online videos |
| 6 | **Fatigue/burnout**: practising too long, or no rest days |
| 7 | **Hidden technical problem**: for example a leak or tension blocking further progress (use the [diagnostic tree](README.md)) |

## Fixes

### Fix A: Make progress visible

- Keep the practice log ([PRF-03](../knowledge-tree/D18-PRF-performance.md)) with numbers:
  long-tone seconds, highest clean BPM, ear-training scores.
- Record the same "benchmark" (Sa long tone, one alankar, one piece) every 2 weeks and keep
  the files. Listen to the first one when you feel stuck.

### Fix B: Deliberate practice (see L01)

For each session, write **one specific goal** ("`.N` to `R` with no blip at 72 BPM"), not "practise
alankars". Spend at least half of the technique block on your weakest point.

### Fix C: Adjust the difficulty

- Too easy: raise the tempo by 4 BPM, add an ornament, play from memory, play with the drone
  and tabla, or move to the next lesson if you meet its mastery criteria.
- Too hard: halve the tempo, shorten the passage, go back to the prerequisite objective in
  the knowledge tree.

### Fix D: Change the activity, keep the goal

If you have been stuck on the same drill for 2 weeks, approach the skill differently: sing it,
clap it, practise it in a song instead of an exercise, or play it in a different octave.

### Fix E: Rest and lighter days

The roadmap includes lighter revision days. Use them. A weekly lighter day often results in
better playing afterwards (Recommendation; common practice advice).

### Fix F: Reconnect with music

Spend a session just **listening** to great bansuri recordings ([R-L01 to R-L05](../02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md#9-listening-library))
or playing your favourite song badly for fun. Motivation comes from music, not from drills.

### Fix G: Social and outside input

Play for a friend. Join an online bansuri community. Book one lesson with a teacher
([R-C01 to R-C03](../02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md#4-courses-and-teachers-paid-or-structured)): a fresh
pair of ears often finds the hidden problem.

## How to verify

- Benchmark recordings show improvement over 4-8 weeks.
- The log shows 5+ practice days per week with specific goals.
- You are reaching milestones, even if more slowly than planned. The roadmap timings are
  estimates; reaching milestones in order is what matters.

## When to seek help

- Two months with no measurable improvement in any benchmark.
- Practice consistently makes you anxious or unhappy. Talking to a teacher or another
  musician helps; if low mood affects your whole life, talk to a doctor.
