# TS-03: Low Notes (`.N .D .P`) Will Not Sound

[Troubleshooting home](README.md) | Related: [TON-06](../knowledge-tree/D03-TON-tone.md), [BOD-04](../knowledge-tree/D02-BOD-body.md), [FNG-03](../knowledge-tree/D05-FNG-fingering.md) | Lesson: [L11](../lessons/L11-lower-notes-ni-dha-pa.md)

## Symptoms

- `.N` sounds but `.D` is weak and `.P` gives only air.
- The low note sounds for a moment, then squeaks up to the octave above.
- The low note "gurgles" or wobbles.
- The note sounds but is very quiet and thin.

Mandra Pa (`XXX XXX`) is the hardest note for many beginners. It needs **all six holes sealed
perfectly** and **slow, relaxed air**. Expect it to take one or two weeks longer than the
middle notes.

## Quick checks

1. **Step-down test.** Play Sa, then add one finger at a time: `.N`, `.D`, `.P`. The first
   note that fails tells you which new finger (hole 4, 5 or 6) is the likely problem.
2. **Pressure test.** On the failing note, press the bottom-hand fingers a little more firmly.
   If it suddenly sounds, it is a leak.
3. **Look at your fingers.** Check in a mirror or with your phone camera: are the pads
   centred on the holes? A finger off-centre often leaves a tiny gap at one edge.
4. **Air test.** Play the note softly with warm, slow "haaah" air. If it works softly but not
   at normal strength, you were blowing too fast.

## Causes, most likely first

| # | Cause | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Leak under a bottom-hand finger (holes 4-6)** | The bottom hand stretches further; fingertips instead of pads leave gaps. Low notes are the most sensitive to leaks. |
| 2 | **Air too fast** | Fast air pushes the note into the next register. Low notes need slow air. |
| 3 | **Lip opening too small** | A tight opening makes fast air. Low notes like a slightly larger, relaxed opening. |
| 4 | **Flute rolled outward / angle too high** | Uncovering the blowing hole makes low notes unstable. Roll in very slightly. |
| 5 | **Tension in the bottom hand** | Squeezing lifts the finger pads at the edges and shakes the flute. |
| 6 | **Flute defect or crack below the holes** | Rare, but check if the problem appeared suddenly (TS-12). |

## Fixes and drills

### Fix A: Pad placement (2 minutes before every session in Phase 1)

1. Put the flute on your lap. Place the bottom-hand fingers on holes 4-6 using the **flat
   part of the finger between the tip and the first joint**.
2. Press, then lift: look at the marks the holes left on your fingers. The ring-shaped mark
   should be **complete and centred** on each pad. A partial ring shows where the gap was.
3. Adjust and repeat until all three marks are complete circles.

### Fix B: Step-down drill (5 minutes)

At 60 BPM, play 4 beats each, slurred:
`S - - - | .N - - - | .D - - - | .P - - - ||` then back up.
If any note fails, stop at that note and repeat only the pair of notes before and including
it (for example `.N - .D -`) 10 times. Then continue.

### Fix C: Low long tones (5 minutes daily)

Hold `.P` for 4 seconds, rest 4 seconds, 8 times. Use the softest air that keeps the note
alive. Then try the same with `.D`. Build to 6-8 seconds.

### Fix D: "Echo" drill (3 minutes)

Play madhya Pa (overblown) briefly, then without changing fingers slow the air down until it
drops to mandra Pa. This shows you exactly how slow the air must be.

### Fix E: Relax the bottom hand (1 minute, every 10 minutes of practice)

Shake out the hand. Then place the fingers with the **least** pressure that still seals.
Squeezing harder is not the answer; correct placement is.

## How to verify the fix

- `.P` held 6 seconds, clean, **5 out of 5 attempts** ([TON-06](../knowledge-tree/D03-TON-tone.md)).
- `S .N .D .P .D .N S` at 60 BPM with no failures, recorded.
- Tuner shows G (for `.P`) with a steady needle.

## When to seek a teacher

- Your fingers are too short to cover holes 4-6 comfortably even with the "piper's grip"
  (flat middle sections of the fingers, see [L02](../lessons/L02-posture-and-holding.md)).
  A teacher can suggest a hand position, or a smaller flute may suit you better later.
- The step-down test points to a hole that seems fully covered: the flute may need checking.
