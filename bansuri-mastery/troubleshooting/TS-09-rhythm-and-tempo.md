# TS-09: Rhythm and Tempo Problems

[Troubleshooting home](README.md) | Related: [RHY-01](../knowledge-tree/D07-RHY-rhythm.md), RHY-03, RHY-04, RHY-05, RHY-07, RHY-09, RHY-14, [EAR-07](../knowledge-tree/D10-EAR-ear.md) | Lessons: [L06](../lessons/L06-pulse-and-tempo.md), [L15](../lessons/L15-taal-basics.md), [L32](../lessons/L32-bandish-taans-layakari-tihai.md)

## Symptoms

- **Rushing:** you speed up, especially on easy or exciting passages.
- **Dragging:** you slow down on hard notes or before a breath.
- **Uneven notes:** notes meant to be equal are not.
- **Losing the cycle:** you do not know where sam is; you come back to the composition at
  the wrong beat.
- **Holding notes for the wrong length** (for example a 2-beat note played as 1 beat).
- With a metronome you are fine; without it you drift.

## Quick checks

1. **Record with the metronome audible.** Listen back: do your notes start **on** the clicks,
   just before them (rushing) or after them (dragging)?
2. **Clap test.** Clap the rhythm of the passage without the flute. If clapping is also wrong,
   the problem is rhythm understanding, not fingers.
3. **Hard-spot test.** Does the timing go wrong exactly where the fingering is hard? Then the
   fingers are setting the tempo instead of the pulse.
4. **Silent-bar test.** Mute the metronome for 4 beats (many apps have this feature, or
   turn the volume down by hand). Are you still with it when it returns?

## Causes, most likely first

| # | Cause | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Tempo too fast for the passage** | Hard notes then take longer, so you drag there and rush elsewhere. |
| 2 | **Not counting** | Without an inner count, long notes get cut short and rests disappear. |
| 3 | **Never practising with a pulse** | Practising "freely" all the time builds unsteady habits. |
| 4 | **Fingers leading, ears following** | You play the next note when the fingers are ready, not when the beat arrives. |
| 5 | **Not feeling the tala structure** | Counting 1-16 without feeling vibhags, sam and khali makes it easy to get lost. |
| 6 | **Breathing in the wrong place** | You steal time for breaths. |

## Fixes and drills

### Fix A: Clap, say, then play (5 minutes)

1. **Clap** the rhythm with the metronome at 60 BPM.
2. **Say** the sargam in rhythm ("Sa Re Ga -").
3. **Sing** it in rhythm.
4. **Play** it.
Only move to the next step when the previous one is perfect. Rhythm problems almost always
disappear once you can say the passage correctly.

### Fix B: Slow down and count aloud (5 minutes)

Reduce the tempo until **every note is easy**. Count beats aloud between phrases or in
your head ("1 and 2 and"). Long notes: count every beat of their length. Rests: count them
too.

### Fix C: Click on 2 and 4 / sparse metronome (5 minutes, Phase 2+)

Set the metronome to half speed so it clicks only every second beat. Then try clicks only on
beat 1 of each 4. You must keep the pulse yourself between clicks. This builds inner time.

### Fix D: Tala recitation (5 minutes daily during Phase 2-3)

Recite the theka of teentaal (or keherwa/dadra) with claps on tali and a wave on khali, for 4
cycles without stopping ([L15](../lessons/L15-taal-basics.md)). Then play an alankar while a
tabla loop plays ([R-A04, R-A05](../02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md#71-drone-tanpura--shruti-box)) and
nod your head on sam.

### Fix E: Find sam drill (3 minutes)

With a tabla loop: play a phrase, stop, **count silently** for one full cycle, then come in
exactly on sam. 5 times. Then do the same starting from the mukhda point of a composition
([RHY-09](../knowledge-tree/D07-RHY-rhythm.md)).

### Fix F: Plan breaths in rests

Mark the breath places ([BRE-04](../knowledge-tree/D04-BRE-breath.md)). If there is no rest,
shorten the last note slightly rather than delaying the next phrase.

## How to verify the fix

- Recording with the metronome: every note start sounds together with the click.
- Silent-bar test passed 3 times in a row.
- A composition with a tabla loop for 8 cycles, arriving on sam each time ([RHY-14](../knowledge-tree/D07-RHY-rhythm.md)).

## When to seek a teacher

- You cannot clap along with a steady metronome at 60 BPM after 2 weeks of daily work (rare,
  but a teacher or a rhythm-focused musician can help a lot).
- Complex tala and layakari questions ([RHY-10](../knowledge-tree/D07-RHY-rhythm.md), RHY-11): a tabla player is an excellent teacher for these.
