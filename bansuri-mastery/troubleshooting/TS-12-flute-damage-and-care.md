# TS-12: Flute Damage, Cracks and Care Problems

[Troubleshooting home](README.md) | Related: [INS-03](../knowledge-tree/D01-INS-instrument.md), INS-04, INS-05, INS-06, INS-09, INS-10 | Lesson: [L00](../lessons/L00-day-0-your-flute.md)

## Symptoms

- A visible crack or split line along the bamboo.
- The flute suddenly sounds airy, weak or will not play low notes, when it was fine before.
- A buzzing or rattling sound.
- A loose or moving binding (the thread or band wrapped around the flute).
- The end with the cork/stopper feels loose, or the cork has moved.
- A splinter or rough edge around a hole that hurts your finger.
- Mould, smell, or dark spots inside the bore.

## Quick checks

1. **Visual inspection** in good light: rotate the flute and look along its whole length,
   including inside the blowing hole and around every finger hole.
2. **Leak test:** close all holes and the open end with your palm, then blow gently into the
   blowing hole with your lips sealed around it (not the playing position). Air escaping
   somewhere, or a hissing sound, suggests a crack. (Uncertain: this is a rough test only.)
3. **Compare with history:** if it played well yesterday and badly today without any change
   in you, suspect the flute. If the problem came on slowly, suspect technique first
   (TS-01 to TS-06).
4. **Cork position:** do not move or push the cork. If it has clearly moved or is loose, see below.

## Causes, most likely first

| # | Cause | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Rapid drying or heat** | Sun, heaters, car boots, air-conditioner vents, hairdryers. The most common cause of cracks. |
| 2 | **Humidity swings** | Moving between very dry and humid places; seasonal changes. |
| 3 | **Drops and knocks** | Even small falls can start a crack. |
| 4 | **Moisture left inside** | Condensation not wiped out can encourage mould and swelling. |
| 5 | **Pressure** | Sitting on it, storing it under heavy objects, gripping it in a bag. |

## What to do

### If you find a crack

1. **Stop playing it.** Playing a cracked flute can widen the crack (Recommendation: common
   maker advice).
2. Photograph the crack.
3. **Contact the maker or seller** first. Many makers repair, replace, or advise. Small cracks
   can sometimes be repaired by binding; whether a crack is repairable depends on its position
   and size (Uncertain: needs expert judgement).
4. Do not glue it or wrap tape around it yourself unless the maker advises it.
5. Keep practising on a second flute if you have one, or spend the days on singing, rhythm,
   ear training and theory.

### Never do this

- **Never dry the flute with heat** (no hairdryer, heater, oven, or direct sun).
- Never leave it in a car.
- Never push or pull the cork to "fix" the tuning.
- Never put the flute in water to clean it.

### Daily care routine ([INS-04](../knowledge-tree/D01-INS-instrument.md))

- Clean hands and lips before playing; do not eat while playing ([INS-06](../knowledge-tree/D01-INS-instrument.md)).
- After playing, let moisture evaporate for a few minutes at room temperature, then wipe the
  outside with a soft cloth. To clean the inside, use a soft cloth on a thin rod if your
  maker recommends it, and be gentle near the cork.
- Store in a cloth or padded bag, at room temperature, away from windows, heaters and AC.
- When moving between very different climates, let the flute adjust in its bag before playing.

### Oiling (Disputed)

Some makers recommend occasionally oiling the bore with a light oil to protect the bamboo;
others advise against oiling, or say it depends on the finish. **Follow your own maker's
advice** ([INS-05](../knowledge-tree/D01-INS-instrument.md)). If you cannot get advice, do
nothing rather than experiment: an unoiled flute kept away from heat and dryness is
generally safe.

### Loose binding or rough hole edges

- A loose binding: ask the maker; bindings often help prevent cracks, so keep it in place.
- A sharp edge: ask the maker or an experienced player before sanding anything. Changing a
  hole changes the tuning.

## How to verify

- After any repair, test the flute with the tuner across all registers ([INS-10](../knowledge-tree/D01-INS-instrument.md) checklist).
- Compare a recording of `S .N .D .P` and `.P P` before and after.

## When to seek help

- Any visible crack: maker or experienced repairer.
- The flute plays well for others but not for you: technique; see a teacher.
- The flute plays badly for an experienced player too: flute problem; maker.
