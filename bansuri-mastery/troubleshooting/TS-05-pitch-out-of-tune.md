# TS-05: Pitch Out of Tune

[Troubleshooting home](README.md) | Related: [PIT-02](../knowledge-tree/D06-PIT-pitch.md), PIT-03, PIT-04, PIT-07, PIT-08, [INS-09](../knowledge-tree/D01-INS-instrument.md) | Lessons: [L07](../lessons/L07-long-tones-tuner-and-drone.md), [L16](../lessons/L16-shuddha-ma-and-half-holing.md), [L17](../lessons/L17-intonation-and-drone.md), [L25](../lessons/L25-komal-swaras-and-12-notes.md)

## Symptoms

- The tuner shows the note more than about 15-20 cents away from the target.
- Against the drone you hear a slow "wah-wah" beating instead of a smooth blend.
- Your playing sounds "sour" next to a recording.
- Pitch drifts during a long note (usually going flat as breath runs out or sharp as you push).
- Every note is flat or sharp by the same amount.

## Quick checks

1. **All notes or some?** Play `S R G` and `.P .D .N` against the tuner.
    - All flat by the same amount: flute is cold, or you roll it in too much (cause 1, 2).
    - All sharp: blowing too hard or rolled out (cause 2, 3).
    - Only half-hole notes (`m r g d n`): coverage (cause 4).
    - Only taar notes sharp: overblowing (cause 3).
2. **Warm-up test.** Measure Sa cold, then after 5 minutes of playing. A bamboo flute usually
   rises in pitch as it warms (Fact: warm air in the bore raises pitch).
3. **Drift test.** Hold Sa for 10 seconds watching the tuner. Does it drift at the end of the
   breath? That is air support (cause 5).
4. **Is it you or the flute?** Ask: can you bend the note into tune with angle and air? If
   yes, it is technique. If one note is always 30+ cents off whatever you do, it may be the
   flute (see the end of this guide).

## Causes, most likely first

| # | Cause | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cold flute** | Flat at the start of a session |
| 2 | **Flute angle** | Rolling **in** (covering more of the blowing hole) = flatter. Rolling **out** = sharper. |
| 3 | **Air speed** | Faster/harder air = sharper; slower/weaker = flatter |
| 4 | **Half-hole coverage** | More uncovered = sharper. `m`, `r`, `g`, `d`, `n` are the least stable notes |
| 5 | **Breath support collapsing** | Note goes flat at the end of a breath |
| 6 | **Not listening to the drone** | Watching the tuner but not hearing the beats |
| 7 | **Flute tuning** | Handmade flutes can have individual notes slightly off. Then use alternate fingerings or angle compensation. |

## Fixes and drills

### Fix A: Warm up before tuning (2 minutes)

Play gentle long tones for 2-3 minutes before you judge pitch. Keep the flute at room
temperature, not in a cold car or next to an air conditioner.

### Fix B: Bend-and-centre drill (5 minutes)

On Sa with the drone ([PIT-04](../knowledge-tree/D06-PIT-pitch.md)):

1. Hold Sa in tune for 4 seconds.
2. Roll in slowly until it is about 20 cents flat (tuner). Hold 2 seconds.
3. Return to centre. Hold 2 seconds.
4. Roll out to about 20 cents sharp. Hold 2 seconds. Return.
5. 5 rounds. Repeat on Pa and Ga.

This gives you deliberate control, so you can correct any note in real time.

### Fix C: Drone matching without the tuner (5 minutes daily)

Play Sa against the drone **with your eyes closed**. Adjust until the beating disappears.
Then open your eyes and check the tuner. Then do Pa (blends very clearly with Sa), then Ga.
Over weeks, your ear will need the tuner less and less. See [L17](../lessons/L17-intonation-and-drone.md).

### Fix D: Half-hole tuning (5 minutes, Phase 2+)

For `m` (half-hole on hole 1):

1. Start with the hole fully closed (Ga). Slowly slide the finger off the hole while
   watching the tuner, until it reads F (for `m`).
2. Feel and look at that finger position. Memorise it.
3. Go from `G` to `m` 10 times, landing directly in tune. Check each with the tuner.
Same process for the komal notes in [L25](../lessons/L25-komal-swaras-and-12-notes.md).

### Fix E: Support to the end of the breath (3 minutes)

Hold Sa for as long as you comfortably can and **stop before it goes flat**. Note the time.
Train to keep the last 2 seconds as steady as the first. If you run out, breathe earlier.

### Fix F: Taar notes sharp (3 minutes)

Play the taar note softly first and find the softest air that holds it in tune. Then grow
louder while rolling in slightly to stop the pitch rising ([TON-08](../knowledge-tree/D03-TON-tone.md)).

## How to verify the fix

- Each note in the current scale within **+/-15 cents** on 3 attempts in a row.
- 8-second long tone with no audible beating against the drone.
- A recording of a melody played with the drone sounds "smooth" when played back.

## Is it the flute? (only after checking technique)

Measure each note 3 times on 2 days after warming up, playing at medium volume with your
best angle. If one note is **consistently** more than about 25 cents off while others are in
tune, note it down. Options: angle compensation, an alternate fingering
([FNG-15](../knowledge-tree/D05-FNG-fingering.md)), or contact the maker. Some deviation is
normal in handmade flutes (Uncertain: acceptable tolerance varies by maker).

## When to seek a teacher

- You cannot hear the beating against the drone at all after 3 weeks of trying.
- Raga intonation questions (for example "how low is komal Re in Bhairav?") need
  listening to masters and guidance; a tuner cannot answer them ([PIT-06](../knowledge-tree/D06-PIT-pitch.md)).
