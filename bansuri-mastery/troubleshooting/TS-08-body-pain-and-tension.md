# TS-08: Pain, Tension, Stiffness, Numbness

[Troubleshooting home](README.md) | Related: [BOD-01](../knowledge-tree/D02-BOD-body.md), BOD-03, BOD-04, BOD-05, BOD-06, BOD-07, BOD-08 | Lessons: [L01](../lessons/L01-how-to-practise.md), [L02](../lessons/L02-posture-and-holding.md)

> **Medical caution (read first).** This guide is not medical advice. **Stop playing and see a
> doctor or physiotherapist** if you have:
> - pain that continues after you stop playing, or returns every session;
> - **numbness, tingling or "pins and needles"** in fingers, hands or arms;
> - weakness, clumsiness or loss of control in a finger or hand;
> - swelling, or pain that wakes you at night;
> - jaw pain, clicking or locking that persists;
> - sharp pain (as opposed to mild muscle tiredness).
>
> Playing through these can turn a short problem into months of injury. Mild tiredness that
> goes away after rest is normal for a beginner; pain is not.

## Symptoms

- Aching in the bottom hand (holes 4-6), thumb base or wrist.
- Stiff shoulders, neck or upper back after practice.
- Tired, tight jaw or lips.
- Shaking fingers.
- Back ache from sitting on the floor.

## Quick checks

1. **Video yourself** from the front and the side for 1 minute while playing. Look for:
   raised shoulders, head tilted or pushed forward, bent wrists, white knuckles, elbows
   pressed into your sides or lifted high.
2. **Squeeze test.** While holding `.P`, how hard are you pressing? Reduce pressure until
   the note fails, then add just enough. Most beginners use 3-5 times more pressure than needed.
3. **Thumb test.** Is your bottom thumb pushing hard into the flute to support it? It should
   support, not clamp.
4. **Session length.** Did the pain start after a longer session than usual or after skipping breaks?

## Causes, most likely first

| # | Cause | Where you feel it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Gripping/squeezing too hard** | Fingers, thumb base, forearm |
| 2 | **Bent wrist on the bottom hand** (reaching for holes 4-6) | Wrist, forearm |
| 3 | **Raised shoulders / elbows held up** | Shoulders, neck |
| 4 | **Head and neck twisted** to reach the blowing hole | Neck, upper back |
| 5 | **Sessions too long without breaks** | Everywhere, especially lips and hands |
| 6 | **Poor sitting position** (collapsed back, legs going numb on the floor) | Lower back, legs |
| 7 | **Clenched jaw or pressing the flute hard into the chin** | Jaw, lips |
| 8 | **Finger stretch too wide for your hands** | Bottom hand, between the fingers |

## Fixes and drills

### Fix A: Warm-up (3-5 minutes before every session)

1. Shoulder rolls: 5 backward, 5 forward.
2. Neck: slowly look left, right, up, down; 3 times each. No forcing.
3. Wrist circles: 5 each direction.
4. Gentle finger stretch: open the hands wide, then make loose fists; 10 times.
5. Shake out the hands for 10 seconds.

### Fix B: Tension scan (30 seconds, start and middle of every session)

Ask in order: **Jaw** soft? **Shoulders** down? **Neck** long? **Elbows** loose? **Wrists**
fairly straight? **Fingers** light? Release each one. Do it again halfway through practice.

### Fix C: Posture reset (2 minutes)

Sit on a chair at the front edge, feet flat, or cross-legged on a firm cushion so your hips
are higher than your knees. Lengthen the spine, bring the flute **to your lips** rather than
moving your head to the flute. Tilt the head only slightly. See [L02](../lessons/L02-posture-and-holding.md).

### Fix D: Piper's grip for the bottom hand

If your fingertips cannot reach holes 4-6 without bending the wrist, use the **flat middle
part of the fingers** (the "piper's grip") to cover the bottom holes. This keeps the wrist
straighter and usually seals better on longer flutes. Try it for one week before deciding.

### Fix E: Shorter blocks

Practise in blocks of 15-20 minutes with 2-3 minute breaks, even if you feel fine
([BOD-07](../knowledge-tree/D02-BOD-body.md)). If you are sore, cut the day's playing by half
and spend the rest on listening, theory and singing.

### Fix F: Lighter jaw and lips

The flute should rest lightly against the chin/lower lip, not be pressed into it. Open the
teeth slightly inside the mouth.

## How to verify the fix

- Video shows no raised shoulders and fairly straight wrists.
- You finish a 45-60 minute day (with breaks) with **no pain** and only mild tiredness that
  is gone by the next morning.
- Tension scan becomes quick because there is less to release.

## When to seek help

- **Doctor or physiotherapist:** any item in the medical caution box above.
- **Teacher:** to check your hold and posture in person, especially if Fix D does not help.
  A teacher may also suggest whether a different flute size would suit your hands later
  ([INS-08](../knowledge-tree/D01-INS-instrument.md)).
