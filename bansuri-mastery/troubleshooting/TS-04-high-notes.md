# TS-04: Upper Notes (Madhya `P D N`, Taar Saptak) Will Not Sound Well

[Troubleshooting home](README.md) | Related: [TON-07](../knowledge-tree/D03-TON-tone.md), [FNG-05](../knowledge-tree/D05-FNG-fingering.md), FNG-09, FNG-14, [PIT-07](../knowledge-tree/D06-PIT-pitch.md) | Lessons: [L14](../lessons/L14-tivra-ma-and-madhya-pa-dha-ni.md), [L22](../lessons/L22-taar-saptak.md)

## Symptoms

- You finger madhya Pa (`XXX XXX`) but get mandra Pa (the low one).
- The upper note comes out but is shrill, harsh or airy.
- Taar Re and Ga need so much force that you feel strain in your throat or face.
- The upper notes are very sharp on the tuner.
- You can reach upper notes only by "exploding" into them.

## Quick checks

1. **Air speed vs force.** Do you make the upper note by blowing *more* air (bigger breath,
   puffed cheeks)? You need *faster, narrower* air, which is not the same as more air.
2. **Lip opening.** Watch in a mirror while going from `.P` to `P`. The opening should get
   **smaller** and the lips move slightly forward. If nothing changes, that is the problem.
3. **Seal check.** Same as TS-03: leaks also stop upper notes.
4. **Pitch check.** If the upper note sounds but the tuner shows it 30+ cents sharp, you are
   overblowing and should use less force with a better lip aim (see TS-05).

## Causes, most likely first

| # | Cause | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Air not fast enough** | Usually from a lip opening that is too big. Make it smaller rather than blowing harder. |
| 2 | **Forcing with breath pressure** | Gives a harsh sound, sharp pitch and fatigue. |
| 3 | **Air angle** | For upper notes the air is aimed a little more across the hole. Rolling slightly out or moving the lower lip slightly forward can help; experiment. |
| 4 | **Leak** | A leaking finger makes upper notes unstable or makes them fail. |
| 5 | **Tension in throat/shoulders** | Tension narrows the airway and makes the tone harsh. |
| 6 | **Expecting too much too soon** | Taar Ga and above are genuinely hard; they often take months. |

## Fixes and drills

### Fix A: Octave slurs (5 minutes daily)

Using the **same fingering**, slur from the low note to the high note and back:
`.P - P - .P -`, then `.D - D - .D -`, `.N - N - .N -`, then `S - S' - S -`. 3 rounds, 60 BPM.
Change only the lips and air speed, never the fingers. Listen for a smooth "click" into the
upper register without a squeak or burst.

### Fix B: Whisper upper notes (3 minutes)

Play madhya Pa, Dha and Ni as **softly** as possible while keeping them in the upper
register. This forces you to use speed and focus rather than force. Hold each for 4 seconds.

### Fix C: Ladder to the top (5 minutes, Phase 3)

Play long tones going up one note at a time: `N` (4 s), `S'` (4 s), `R'` (4 s), `G'` (4 s).
Stop at the first note that is strained. That is your current ceiling. Work there with soft
long tones for a week before adding the next note.

### Fix D: Body release (1 minute between sets)

Drop your shoulders, open your throat by yawning gently, and relax your face. Then repeat
the upper note with the same relaxed feeling.

## How to verify the fix

- `.P` to `P` octave slur, **5/5 clean** ([TON-07](../knowledge-tree/D03-TON-tone.md)).
- Tuner: the upper note within +/-15 cents of the target when played at medium volume
  ([PIT-07](../knowledge-tree/D06-PIT-pitch.md)).
- Recording: the upper notes sound like the same instrument as the middle notes, not like a whistle.

## When to seek a teacher

- Upper notes cause pain, dizziness or a feeling of pressure in your head.
- After 6 weeks of daily practice you cannot reliably play madhya Pa.
- You need to know the best third-register fingerings for **your** flute
  ([FNG-10](../knowledge-tree/D05-FNG-fingering.md)); these vary between flutes.
