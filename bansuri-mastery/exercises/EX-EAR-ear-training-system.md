# EX-EAR: Ear-Training System

[Back to Mastery Map](../00-MASTERY-MAP.md) | Objectives: [D10 EAR](../knowledge-tree/D10-EAR-ear.md) | Related: [L05](../lessons/L05-what-is-music-pitch-sa-sargam.md), [L17](../lessons/L17-intonation-and-drone.md), [L18](../lessons/L18-song-learning-system.md)

Your goal of playing any melody by ear rests on this file. Do **5-10 minutes every day**, even on
light days. Ear skills grow slowly and steadily; daily short practice beats occasional long
sessions.

## Why this system exists

The research for this curriculum found **no app that drills Hindustani sargam recognition
against a Sa drone** ([Resource Library gaps](../02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md#gaps-found-during-research)).
Western apps exist, and some are excellent (R-E02 Functional Ear Trainer drills exactly the
skill of hearing notes against a home note), but they use Western note names. So this system
combines three methods:

| Method | How | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Self-recorded dictation** | You record yourself playing items from a numbered bank, wait 2+ days, then identify them from the recording and check with the answer key | Works alone, uses your own flute's sound, sargam-based |
| **B. Partner** | A friend or family member plays (or presses keys on a keyboard app) items from the bank while you answer | Fastest feedback |
| **C. Apps** | R-E02 Functional Ear Trainer (note vs tonic), R-E01 musictheory.net (intervals, chords), R-A02 NaadSadhana (iOS, Hindustani workouts) | Unlimited random questions |

**Converting app answers to sargam:** set the app's key/tonic to **C**. Then C = S, D = R,
E = G, F = m, F# = M, G = P, A = D, B = N, Db = r, Eb = g, Ab = d, Bb = n.

### Method A step by step

1. Open the bank for your level. **Do not read the answer column.** Cover it, or ask someone
   to choose 10 item numbers for you.
2. Turn on the C drone. Record: say the item number aloud, play the item, pause 5 seconds.
3. Label the recording with the date and **do not listen to it for at least 2 days**. (Record a
   new set every day, so after the first 2 days you always have an old one ready.)
4. Later, play the recording, pause after each item, sing it back, then write your answer.
5. Check with the answer key. Log your score.

> **Honest limitation:** you will sometimes remember what you recorded. That is why the gap is
> at least 2 days and why you should record 10 items at a time picked by someone else. Mix
> Method A with Methods B and C to stay honest.

**Score log:** keep a table of date, level, method and score (for example "L3, A, 17/20").

## Level 5: Intervals (from L16; EAR-06)

An **interval** is the distance between two notes. Here, the first note is always Sa, and you
name the second as a swara and as a Western interval. Then do the same drills in R-E01 or R-E02
(random starting notes).

| Pair | Semitones | Western name |
|---|---|---|
| `S R` | 2 | major 2nd |
| `S G` | 4 | major 3rd |
| `S m` | 5 | perfect 4th |
| `S P` | 7 | perfect 5th |
| `S D` | 9 | major 6th |
| `S N` | 11 | major 7th |
| `S S'` | 12 | octave |

**Memory songs (Recommendation):** link each interval to a melody you know well. `S S'` is
the jump at the start of many songs; choose your own examples and write them here.

**Pass:** 17/20 on an app interval quiz (major 2nd to octave, ascending).

**Phrase memory (EAR-09):** daily, play 4 short phrases from a teacher video (any R-V lesson
where the teacher demonstrates phrases), pausing after each and copying it. Pass = 8 of 10
phrases copied correctly on the first or second attempt.

**Recurring-pattern recognition (EAR-08, EAR-09, EAR-12):** most melodies are built from a few
repeated ideas. Learning to spot them makes dictation and memorising much faster. Do one
drill per day, 5 minutes, alongside Level 7:

| Drill | What you do | Pass |
|---|---|---|
| Same or different | Listen to a song section. Each time a phrase starts, say whether it is new (A), an exact repeat of an earlier phrase (A again), or a variation (A'). Write the letters, for example `A A' B A`. | Your letter map matches a second listening, 4 of 5 songs |
| Sequence spotting | Play and record alankars, then mixed phrases where a short shape repeats on different starting notes (for example `S R G`, `R G m`, `G m P`). Later, say whether each recorded line contains a sequence and name the shape. | 8 of 10 |
| Cadence spotting | In songs and ragas, notice the phrases that end sections (often falling to Sa, or resting on Pa). Write down the last 3-4 notes of each section ending. | Endings correctly notated in 3 songs |
| Pakad spotting (Phase 3+) | While listening to a raga you know, raise a hand whenever its pakad or a characteristic phrase appears. | Count matches a teacher's or a second listener's within 1-2 |

Answer key: for the sequence drill, your own recorded list is the key (Method A). For the
other drills, compare with a second, slower listening using a slow-down tool
([R-A14](../02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md#72-tuner-metronome-recording-slow-down), [R-A17](../02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md#72-tuner-metronome-recording-slow-down)).

## Level 8: Major or minor? (Phase 4; EAR-14)

Mainly use R-E01 (chord identification). On the flute you can practise with **arpeggios**
(chord notes one after another): `S G P` sounds major (bright); `S g P` sounds minor (darker).

## Level 9: Raga recognition (Phase 4; EAR-12)

1. Record yourself playing 10 short phrases, 2 each from 5 ragas you have learned (for
   example Bhupali, Yaman, Durga, Khamaj, Kafi), in random order. Write the order on a hidden
   sheet.
2. After 2+ days, name the raga of each phrase.
3. Then do the same with 30-second excerpts of master recordings (R-L01 to R-L05, R-T07).

**Pass:** 9/10 own phrases; 5/5 master excerpts. Check every answer against the [raga cards](../ragas/README.md).

## Level 10: Full song transcription (Phase 3-4; SNG-01)

Use the 12-step process in [L18](../lessons/L18-song-learning-system.md). The test is Milestone
M6. There is no answer key: **verify by playing your notation along with the recording.** Every
mismatch is either a wrong note, a wrong rhythm, or a wrong Sa.
