## Quiz 1: Swaras and saptak (Phase 1, after L05 and L10)

1. Give the full names of the seven swaras.
2. Which two swaras never change (have only one form)?
3. Which swara has a tivra (sharp) form?
4. Which swaras have komal (flat) forms?
5. Name the lower, middle and upper octaves.
6. Counting from Sa as 1, which swara is number 5?
7. What is the fingering for Sa on your flute?
8. How is taar Re written in this curriculum's ASCII notation?
9. Write the descending Bilawal scale starting from taar Sa.
10. What is the relationship between `.P` and `P`?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Shadja (Sa), Rishabh (Re), Gandhar (Ga), Madhyam (Ma), Pancham (Pa), Dhaivat (Dha), Nishad (Ni)
2. Sa and Pa (called *achal*, immovable)
3. Ma
4. Re, Ga, Dha, Ni
5. Mandra, madhya, taar
6. Pa
7. `XXX OOO` (top 3 holes closed)
8. `R'`
9. `S' N D P m G R S`
10. They are the same note an octave apart; `P` vibrates twice as fast (double the frequency) as `.P`. On your flute they even share a fingering (`XXX XXX`); only the breath differs.

</details>

## Quiz 2: Rhythm and tala (Phase 2, after L06 and L15)

1. What is a matra?
2. What is a vibhag?
3. What is an avartan?
4. What is sam, and how is it marked?
5. What is khali, and how is it shown with the hand?
6. Give the beat count and division of Teentaal, and the beat number of its khali.
7. Give the beat count and division of Keherwa and Dadra.
8. At 60 BPM, how long does one Teentaal cycle take? At 120 BPM?
9. In dugun, how many notes are played per beat?
10. What is a theka?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. One beat (a unit of time).
2. A section (subdivision) of a tala cycle.
3. One complete cycle of the tala.
4. The first, strongest beat of the cycle; marked `X`.
5. The "empty" section start; shown with a wave of the hand (no clap); marked `0`.
6. 16 beats, 4 + 4 + 4 + 4; khali on beat 9 (tali on 1, 5 and 13).
7. Keherwa: 8 beats, 4 + 4 (khali on 5). Dadra: 6 beats, 3 + 3 (khali on 4).
8. 16 seconds; 8 seconds.
9. Two.
10. The basic pattern of tabla syllables (bols) that identifies a tala, for example "Dha Dhin Dhin Dha" for Teentaal.

</details>

## Quiz 3: Semitones and intervals (Phase 2-3, after L16 and L28)

Semitone positions from Sa: `S`0 `r`1 `R`2 `g`3 `G`4 `m`5 `M`6 `P`7 `d`8 `D`9 `n`10 `N`11 `S'`12.

How many semitones from the first note **up** to the second?

1. `R` to `P`
2. `G` to `D`
3. `S` to `g`
4. `m` to `S'`
5. `N` to `S'`
6. `R` to `m`
7. `G` to `N`
8. `P` to `S'`
9. `g` to `P`
10. `M` to `P`

Then: 11. Write the Bilawal scale pattern using W (whole step = 2 semitones) and H (half step = 1).

12. Write the Kalyan pattern. 13. Write the Kafi pattern.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. 5 | 2. 5 | 3. 3 | 4. 7 | 5. 1 | 6. 3 | 7. 7 | 8. 5 | 9. 4 | 10. 1
11. Bilawal: W W H W W W H
12. Kalyan: W W W H W W H
13. Kafi: W H W W W H W

</details>

## Quiz 4: Thaats (Phase 3, after L19)

2. Which thaat uses only shuddha notes?
3. Which thaat differs from Bilawal only by tivra Ma?
4. Which thaat has komal Re, shuddha Ga, shuddha Ma, komal Dha and shuddha Ni?
5. Which thaat has komal Re, tivra Ma and shuddha Dha?
6. Which thaat has all four of Re, Ga, Dha, Ni komal?
7. Name one weakness of the thaat system (L19).

## Quiz 5: Raga grammar (Phase 3, after L19-L21)

1. Define aroh and avroh.
2. Define vadi and samvadi.
3. What is a pakad?
4. What does varjit mean?
5. What are audav, shadav and sampurna jati?
6. What is the jati of Bhupali?
7. What is the jati of Khamaj, and why?
8. Which notes are varjit in Durga?
9. A raga uses `S R G P D`. Name two ragas with exactly these notes.
10. A raga uses `S R m P D`. Which raga is it most likely to be?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Aroh: the ascending order of notes allowed in the raga. Avroh: the descending order.
2. Vadi: the most important note; samvadi: the second most important, usually a 4th or 5th away.
3. A short characteristic phrase that identifies the raga.
4. Omitted (not used in the raga, or in one direction).
5. Audav = 5 notes, shadav = 6 notes, sampurna = 7 notes (stated for ascent-descent, for example "audav-sampurna").
6. Audav-audav
7. Shadav-sampurna: Re is omitted in the ascent (6 notes) but all 7 are used in the descent.
8. Ga and Ni
9. Bhupali and Deshkar (they differ in emphasis and movement)
10. Durga

</details>

## Quiz 6: Keys and transposition (Phase 3-4, after L26)

1. What is the lowest note of your flute in sargam and in Western notation?
2. A melody goes down to F4. Can you play it on your flute as written? What can you do?
3. On your flute, which Western note is tivra Ma?
4. If you treat Pa (G) as Sa, which C-flute fingering gives the new "Ni"?
5. A song uses D E F# G A with D as Sa. Write it in sargam.
6. Which C-flute notes (in C-flute sargam) does that song use? Are any half-holes needed?
7. A song is in A major (A B C# D E F# G#). If you play it with A as Sa on your C flute, which notes need half-holes?
8. Which thaat becomes half-hole-free on your flute if you treat Dha (A) as Sa?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. `.P` = G4
2. No; F4 is below G4. Transpose the melody up (for example one octave, if the top still fits), play it with a different Sa, or use a longer flute.
3. F#
4. The new Ni is F#, played with all holes open (`OOO OOO`), which is C-flute tivra Ma.
5. `S R G m P`
6. D E F# G A = C-flute `R G M P D`. No half-holes needed.
7. C# and G#: C-flute komal Re (`r`) and komal Dha (`d`).
8. Kafi (A B C D E F# G = A Dorian, Kafi pattern W H W W W H W). See the [raga index](../ragas/README.md).

</details>
