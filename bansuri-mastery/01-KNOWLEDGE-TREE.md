objectives. Each domain has its own file with the full objective table (ID, what to
understand or perform, why it matters, prerequisites, difficulty, importance, mastery test,
phase, lesson).

Structure: **Domain -> Topic -> Subtopic/Objective (ID) -> Mastery test -> Lesson.**
Symbols and abbreviations: [Conventions](05-CONVENTIONS-AND-NOTATION-KEY.md).

## 3. Dependency spine

The chain below is the critical path. Nothing on the right is taught before the things on its
left are at mastery level. Arrows mean "is required before".

        v                                                                          v

        |                          |                                               |
        v                          v                                               v

Supporting tracks that run **in parallel from the start**: ear training (EAR), theory (THY),
recording and self-assessment (PRF-01..03), practice skills (PRA), listening (PRA-07), and
instrument care (INS-04).

## 4. Why this order? (Recommendations, with reasons)

| Decision | Reason |
|---|---|
| Sa (`XXX OOO`) is the first note | Easiest note on the bansuri: few fingers, middle of the first register |
| Lower notes (`.N .D .P`) come before madhya Pa/Dha/Ni | They use the same gentle breath as Sa; overblowing is a new skill and comes later |
| Tivra Ma comes before shuddha Ma | Tivra Ma is all-open (easy); shuddha Ma needs a half-hole |
| Bhupali is the first raga | 5 notes, no Ma, no Ni, no half-holes |
| Yaman is the second raga | Uses tivra Ma, which is easy on bansuri; it is the classic teaching raga |
| Komal notes come after first ragas | Half-holes need good intonation habits and a trained ear |
| Ornaments start only after clean, in-tune notes | Ornaments built on unstable notes create hard-to-fix habits |
| Ear training and theory start on Day 0 | Your goal of playing by ear needs years of listening; starting early compounds |
| Gamak and fast taans are last | They need breath control, finger speed and rhythm accuracy |
