## Character

Longing, tender, often sad. Very common in Hindi film music and bhajans. Exactly Bhupali with
komal Ga instead of shuddha Ga, and the mood changes completely.

## Notes to treat carefully

- **Komal Ga** must be clearly lower than Bhupali's Ga (Eb, not E). Check with a tuner.
- Glides between `R` and `g`, and from `P` down to `g`, are characteristic. Learn meend first
  ([L23](../lessons/L23-meend.md)).
- `.D S` and `.D .P`-type mandra phrases are common in the lower register.

## On your C flute

| Note | Fingering | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| `g` | `XHO OOO` (half-hole 2) | The only half-hole; often slightly sharp at first |

## Movement and resting notes (sourced)

From the sources linked on each line, written in this course's sargam. Octave marks were added where a
source leaves them out, so check phrases by ear against the recordings below. Paraphrased, not quoted.

**Movement (chalan)**

- Hindustani form: pentatonic S R g P D S' / S' D P g R S ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shivaranjani))
- Uses vakra (zig-zag) phrases ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shivaranjani))
- Aroh S R g P D S'; avaroh S' D P g R S ([ragajunglism](https://ragajunglism.org/ragas/shivranjani/))
- Pakad (variable): S R g P D; P g R S .D S ([ragajunglism](https://ragajunglism.org/ragas/shivranjani/))
- Chalan (Tanarang): S R g P D, P D P g R, R g R S R .D S ([ragajunglism](https://ragajunglism.org/ragas/shivranjani/))
- Vakra motions: .D .P .D S g R; S R g R g R; S R S .D S ([ragajunglism](https://ragajunglism.org/ragas/shivranjani/))

**Resting notes (nyas) and strong notes**

- The sources do not name resting notes for this raga.

**Used sparingly, and how it differs from similar ragas**

- Ma (and Ni) absent; no Ma keeps it away from Kafi-ang ([ragajunglism](https://ragajunglism.org/ragas/shivranjani/))
- Vs Bhupali: Shivranjani is Bhupali with komal Ga ([ragajunglism](https://ragajunglism.org/ragas/shivranjani/))

**Intonation and ornaments**

- The sources say nothing specific about this raga's intonation: tune to the drone and copy the recordings.

*Notation note: Wikipedia's 'Shivaranjani' article is mostly Carnatic; only its Hindustani scale line and two general sentences were used. ragajunglism: lowercase g = komal Ga; octaves inferred (e.g. 'SRSDS' as S R S .D S).*

## Listening

All recordings: [Listening Library: Shivranjani](../08-LISTENING-LIBRARY.md#shivranjani).

- **Start with:** R-L40 (Prasad Bhandarkar, 9:39).
- **Then:** R-L41 (Hariprasad Chaurasia, 10:44) and R-L42 (Rakesh Chaurasia, alap and jod).
- Many film songs use this raga. Try identifying one by ear ([L18](../lessons/L18-song-learning-system.md)).

Check what you hear against this card: the aroh/avroh, the pakad and the notes to treat carefully.

## Practice composition (Original, written for this curriculum; not yet reviewed by a teacher)

**Teentaal, 16 beats per line (one cycle), target 60-80 BPM. Starts on sam.** Each `|` marks a vibhag of 4 beats.

```
S R g - | R g P - | D P g R | g - R S |
R g P D | S' - D P | g R g S | R .D S - ||
```

It uses the characteristic phrases `R g P` and `D P g R` and ends with the mandra cadence `R .D S`. Once you know meend ([L23](../lessons/L23-meend.md)), glide gently from `P` down to `g`. Practise it with a C drone at 50-60 BPM, one note per beat: sing it first, then play it until every phrase is in tune with the drone, then add a teentaal loop and raise the tempo slowly.

**Uncertain:** this composition was written to follow the rules on this card, but its raga grammar has not been checked by a teacher. Have a teacher check it before you perform it, and prefer a traditional composition from a teacher or from [R-T01 / R-T04](../02-RESOURCE-LIBRARY.md#5-theory-and-raga-references) when you have one.

## Practice steps

1. Play Bhupali's aroh/avroh, then the same with `g`. Hear the change.
2. Slow `R g R g` with tuner, then `P g R S`.
3. Characteristic phrases from memory.
