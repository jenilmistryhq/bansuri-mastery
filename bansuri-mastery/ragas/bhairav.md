## Character

Solemn, devotional, majestic. Associated with dawn and with Shiva.

## Notes to treat carefully

- **Komal Re and komal Dha are played with a slow andolan** (gentle oscillation) in
  traditional Bhairav. Wikipedia's article does not discuss this, but it is very widely taught.
  Learn it by ear from good recordings and a teacher ([L33](../lessons/L33-gamak-andolan-advanced-ornaments.md)).
- The exact intonation of `r` and `d` in Bhairav is subtly different from the same notes in
  other ragas (see PIT-06, Disputed in exact value). A tuner gets you close; your ear has to
  finish the job.

## On your C flute

| Note | Fingering | Register |
|---|---|---|
| `r` | `XXH OOO` | 1 |
| `m` | `HOO OOO` | 1 |
| `d` | `XXX XXH` | 2 |

`r` (half-hole 3) is often the hardest komal note on bansuri. Practise `S r S r` and `r G r G`
with a drone, listening for a steady note before adding andolan.

## Similar ragas

| Raga | Difference |
|---|---|
| Kalingda | Same notes; lighter, without andolan |
| Ahir Bhairav | Komal Ni and shuddha Dha in the upper half |
| Ramkali | Adds tivra Ma and komal Ni in some phrases |

## Traditional composition (Bhatkhande, Kramik Pustak Malika)

**What it is:** sargam geet (a composition sung to the note names), jhaptaal, sthayi (first section) only.

**Source:** V. N. Bhatkhande, *Hindustani Sangeet Paddhati: Kramik Pustak Malika*, Part 1 (Hindi edition, Sangeet Karyalaya, Hathras), raga Bhairav, sargam, page 36. Scans read: [older Hathras printing (eGangotri), leaf n39](https://archive.org/details/skPk_hindustani-sangeet-paddhati-kramik-pustak-malika-1st-book-of-vishnu-narayan-bhar/page/n39/mode/1up) and [2013 Sangeet Karyalaya printing, leaf n36](https://archive.org/details/bhatkhande-hindustani-sangeet-paddhati-kramik-pustak-malika-part-1_compress/page/n36/mode/1up). The two printings agree.

It was transcribed from the public-domain book (Bhatkhande died in 1936) into this course's notation. Check it against the source page before you rely on it.

**Jhaptaal, 10 beats per line (2 + 3 + 2 + 3). Starts on sam; khali is the first note of the third vibhag.** Each `|` marks a vibhag.

```
S d | P P d | m P | m G r |
G r | G m P | m G | r r S |
.N S | r r S | .d .d | .N S - |
G r | G m P | m G | r r S ||
```

Practise it with a C drone at 50-60 BPM, one note per beat: sing the note names first, then play it until every phrase is in tune with the drone (watch the half-holed `r` and `d`), then add a jhaptaal loop and raise the tempo slowly.

## Practice steps

1. Long tones on `r` and `d` against the drone, 6 s each.
2. Pakad, slowly, without andolan, then with gentle andolan copied from a recording.
3. Compare with Kalingda phrases (if your teacher can demonstrate).
