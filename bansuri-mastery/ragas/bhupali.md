# Raga Bhupali (Bhoop)

[Raga index](README.md) | Full lesson: [L20 Raga Bhupali](../lessons/L20-raga-bhupali.md) | Objective: RAG-02

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Thaat | Kalyan |
| Notes | S R G P D (Ma and Ni are omitted: *varjit*) |
| Jati | Audav-audav (5 notes up, 5 down) |
| Aroh | `S R G P D S'` |
| Avroh | `S' D P G R S` |
| Vadi / samvadi | Ga / Dha |
| Time | First part of the night (Wikipedia: about 7-9 pm). Sources vary slightly. |
| Pakad (common form) | `G R S .D S R G, P G, D P G R S` |

## Character

Calm, open and devotional. Phrases often rest on Ga and Dha. Movement from Pa to Ga and from Dha
to Pa is smooth, often with a gentle meend once you learn it ([L23](../lessons/L23-meend.md)).

## Notes to treat carefully

- **Ga** is the most important note (vadi). Hold it, return to it, rest on it.
- **Dha** answers Ga. In descent `D P G` and `S' D P` are typical.
- Do not let Ma or Ni slip in, even as a passing note. That would change the raga.

## On your C flute

No half-holes. Every note is a basic fingering:

| Note | Fingering | Register |
|---|---|---|
| `.D` | `XXX XXO` | 1 |
| `S` | `XXX OOO` | 1 |
| `R` | `XXO OOO` | 1 |
| `G` | `XOO OOO` | 1 |
| `P` | `XXX XXX` | 2 (overblown) |
| `D` | `XXX XXO` | 2 |
| `S'` | `XXX OOO` | 2 |

The only technical challenge is the jump from `G` (register 1) to `P` (register 2). Practise
`G P G P` slowly until the register change is clean ([L14](../lessons/L14-tivra-ma-and-madhya-pa-dha-ni.md)).

## Similar ragas

| Raga | Same notes? | Difference |
|---|---|---|
| Deshkar | Yes (S R G P D) | Bilawal thaat; emphasis on Dha and Pa in the upper half, avoids Bhupali's `G R S` resting phrases |
| Shuddha Kalyan | Adds M and N in descent | Bhupali-like ascent, Yaman-like descent |
| Shivranjani | Komal `g` instead of `G` | Very different mood (sad, longing) |
| Mohanam (Carnatic) | Yes | Carnatic counterpart with different ornamentation |

## Practice steps

1. Aroh/avroh at 60 BPM, 1 note per beat, with drone. 3 clean repetitions.
2. Pakad from memory, 5 times, eyes closed.
3. Pentatonic alankars from [EX-ALK Version C-penta](../exercises/EX-ALK-alankar-book.md) (and Versions A/B in L12).
4. 2 minutes of free slow playing that keeps returning to Ga, then to Sa.

**Verification:** Thaat, notes, vadi/samvadi and time checked against [Wikipedia: Bhupali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhupali) on 2026-10-29. The pakad is a commonly taught form and may differ slightly between teachers.
