# Raga Bhairavi

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Thaat | Bhairavi (the main raga of this thaat) |
| Notes | S r g m P d n (all four movable notes komal) |
| Jati | Sampurna-sampurna (with some notes skipped in practice; see below) |
| Aroh | `S r g m P d n S'` |
| Avroh | `S' n d P m g r S` |
| Vadi / samvadi | **Disputed:** Wikipedia lists Ma/Pa as vadi and Sa as samvadi; other sources give Ma / Sa |
| Time | Morning; traditionally the **concluding piece** of a concert |
| Pakad (Wikipedia) | `.n S g m d P g m g r S` |

## Character

Tender, devotional, bittersweet. In light classical music (thumri, bhajan, film) Bhairavi is
treated freely: musicians often bring in other notes (for example shuddha Re or shuddha Dha) as
colour. In strict classical Bhairavi only the listed notes are used. Wikipedia mentions
"Sindhu Bhairavi" (adds shuddha Dha) and "Shuddha Bhairavi" (no added notes) as variants.

## Notes to treat carefully

- Wikipedia notes that **Re and Dha oscillate** and that **Ma is important**. It also notes that
  Re and Pa are sometimes skipped in ascent and Ga in descent.
- The cadence `g m g r S` is very characteristic.

## On your C flute

Five half-hole notes: `r` (`XXH OOO`), `g` (`XHO OOO`), `m` (`HOO OOO`), `d` (`XXX XXH`, register 2)
and `n` (`XXX XHO`, register 2).

> **Recommendation:** strict Bhairavi with Sa = C is very demanding on a 6-hole flute. Bansuri
> players often use a different note as Sa so fewer half-holes are needed. On your flute,
> **Ni (B) as Sa** makes every Bhairavi note a plain fingering: S r g m P d n = B C D E F# G A.
> (F#, the all-open note, becomes Pa and can be weak in tone.) Set the drone to B. See
> [L26](../lessons/L26-keys-transposition-and-limits.md). Learn Bhairavi with Sa = C as a
> half-hole intonation study, and with Sa = B for performance.

## Movement and resting notes (sourced)

From the sources linked on each line, written in this course's sargam. Octave marks were added where a
source leaves them out, so check phrases by ear against the recordings below. Paraphrased, not quoted.

**Movement (chalan)**

- Re and Pa are occasionally skipped in aroh: S g m d P or g m d n S' ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhairavi_%28Hindustani%29))
- In avaroh Re and Pa are held long (deergh): S' n d P or P m g m r r S ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhairavi_%28Hindustani%29))
- In avaroh Ga is skipped (example given as g m r S) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhairavi_%28Hindustani%29))
- Aroh: S r g m d n S' ([ragajunglism](https://ragajunglism.org/ragas/bhairavi/))
- Avaroh: S' n d P m g r S ([ragajunglism](https://ragajunglism.org/ragas/bhairavi/))
- Pakad (variable): S g m P d P; R g m g r S ([ragajunglism](https://ragajunglism.org/ragas/bhairavi/))
- Chalan: S .n S g m d P; m g m P; d m P m g; d n S' r' g' r' S'; g m d n S'; S' r' n S' n d P; d P m P m g; S r g m; g r S ([ragajunglism](https://ragajunglism.org/ragas/bhairavi/))
- The m d P figure is prominent ([ragajunglism](https://ragajunglism.org/ragas/bhairavi/))
- Endings often use both Re's, e.g. R (g m g) r S ([ragajunglism](https://ragajunglism.org/ragas/bhairavi/))

**Resting notes (nyas) and strong notes**

- Pa is a resting tone in descent (g m d P; S' n d P) ([ragajunglism](https://ragajunglism.org/ragas/bhairavi/))
- Ma is an important note ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhairavi_%28Hindustani%29))
- Shuddha Ma is consistently strong ([ragajunglism](https://ragajunglism.org/ragas/bhairavi/))

**Used sparingly, and how it differs from similar ragas**

- Komal Re and Pa can be weak or omitted in aroh ([ragajunglism](https://ragajunglism.org/ragas/bhairavi/))
- Bhairavi allows any of the 12 swaras (mishra use) ([ragajunglism](https://ragajunglism.org/ragas/bhairavi/))
- Scalar neighbour: Asavari is Bhairavi with shuddha Re ([ragajunglism](https://ragajunglism.org/ragas/bhairavi/))

**Intonation and ornaments**

- Komal Re and komal Dha are oscillated (andolan) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhairavi_%28Hindustani%29))
- Komal Ga may be oscillated or ornamented with neighbouring notes ([ragajunglism](https://ragajunglism.org/ragas/bhairavi/))
- A shake on komal Ga unique to Bhairavi ([ragajunglism](https://ragajunglism.org/ragas/bhairavi/))

**Disputed:** Which swara swings: Wikipedia names komal Re and Dha; ragajunglism stresses a shake on komal Ga that it calls unique to Bhairavi. Listen for both.

*Notation note: Wikipedia's Bhairavi examples mix capitals and lowercase without a key (e.g. 'S G m d P', 'S' N d P'); since Bhairavi's Ga and Ni are komal, G and N in these examples were converted to g and n. Wikipedia's 'In Avroh, Gandhar is skipped, like: G m r S' contains Ga in its own example; kept verbatim, meaning unclear (probably Ga is skipped between m and r). ragajunglism's 'R' in R(gmg)rS is shuddha Re (a mishra touch). Octave marks added by inference.*

## Traditional composition (Bhatkhande, Kramik Pustak Malika)

**What it is:** sargam geet (a composition sung to the note names), teentaal, sthayi (first section) only.

**Source:** V. N. Bhatkhande, *Hindustani Sangeet Paddhati: Kramik Pustak Malika*, Part 1 (Hindi edition, Sangeet Karyalaya, Hathras), raga Bhairavi, sargam, page 52. Scans read: [older Hathras printing (eGangotri), leaf n55](https://archive.org/details/skPk_hindustani-sangeet-paddhati-kramik-pustak-malika-1st-book-of-vishnu-narayan-bhar/page/n55/mode/1up) and [2013 Sangeet Karyalaya printing, leaf n52](https://archive.org/details/bhatkhande-hindustani-sangeet-paddhati-kramik-pustak-malika-part-1_compress/page/n52/mode/1up). The two printings agree.

It was transcribed from the public-domain book (Bhatkhande died in 1936) into this course's notation. Check it against the source page before you rely on it. Both printings show line 1 entirely in the middle octave, so `n d - S` drops from Dha down to middle Sa.

**Teentaal, 16 beats per line. The sthayi starts on beat 9 (khali), so sam is the first note of the third vibhag of each line (`n` in line 1, `.n` in line 2).** Each `|` marks a vibhag of 4 beats.

```
S d P d | m P g m | n d - S | - r g m |
g r S - | .d .n S r | .n S m m | g g r S ||
```

Practise it with a C drone at 50-60 BPM, one note per beat: sing the note names first, then play it until every phrase is in tune with the drone, then add a teentaal loop and raise the tempo slowly.

## Practice steps

1. Chromatic half-hole tuning drill (L25), then the Bhairavi scale slowly with drone.
2. The pakad, 5 times from memory.
3. Learn a short Bhairavi bhajan or dhun by ear and identify any "extra" notes it uses.
