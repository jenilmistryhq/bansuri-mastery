# Conventions and Notation Key

Read this once on Day 0, then come back whenever a symbol confuses you. Every file in this
curriculum uses these conventions.

| Item | Value | Status |

## 2. Hole numbering and finger map

Hold the flute horizontally. Count the finger holes **starting from the hole nearest the blowing
hole**.

```
 blow hole                                                    open end
    (o)      [1]   [2]   [3]        [4]   [5]   [6]
             index middle ring       index middle ring
             \___ top hand ___/      \___ bottom hand ___/
```

- Most players use the **left hand on top** (holes 1-3) and the **right hand on the bottom**
  (holes 4-6), with the flute pointing to the right. Many great players do the opposite
  (flute pointing left). Both are correct. Choose one on Day 0 and never switch.
- Thumbs support the flute from underneath. Little fingers rest lightly on the bamboo or float.
- You cover holes with the **soft pad of the finger** (between the tip and the first joint),
  not the fingertip.

## 3. Fingering symbols

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `X` | hole fully closed |
| `O` | hole open |
| `H` | hole **half** closed (slide the finger to uncover about half the hole) |

A fingering is written as holes 1-2-3 then 4-5-6, for example `XXX OOO` = holes 1, 2, 3 closed,
4, 5, 6 open.

### 3.1 Master fingering chart for a 6-hole bansuri (Sa = C)

> **Status:** This is the standard fingering system taught for the 6-hole bansuri. Every flute
> is hand-made, so individual notes, especially half-holes and the top octave, may need small
> adjustments. **Always check new notes with a tuner and a drone** ([L17](lessons/L17-intonation-and-drone.md)).

**Register** means how you blow: register 1 = gentle, slow air; register 2 = faster, narrower
air (same fingers, note jumps up an octave); register 3 = faster again, often with special
fingerings.

| Sargam | Name | Fingering (1-2-3 4-5-6) | Western note (Sa = C) | Register | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `.P` | Mandra Pa | `XXX XXX` | G | 1 | Medium (lowest note, needs a well-sealed grip) |
| `.d` | Mandra komal Dha | `XXX XXH` | G#/Ab | 1 | Hard |
| `.D` | Mandra Dha | `XXX XXO` | A | 1 | Easy-medium |
| `.n` | Mandra komal Ni | `XXX XHO` | A#/Bb | 1 | Hard |
| `.N` | Mandra Ni | `XXX XOO` | B | 1 | Easy |
| `S` | Sa | `XXX OOO` | C | 1 | **Easiest - your first note** |
| `r` | Komal Re | `XXH OOO` | C#/Db | 1 | Hard |
| `R` | Re | `XXO OOO` | D | 1 | Easy |
| `g` | Komal Ga | `XHO OOO` | D#/Eb | 1 | Hard |
| `G` | Ga | `XOO OOO` | E | 1 | Easy |
| `m` | Shuddha Ma | `HOO OOO` | F | 1 | Medium-hard (half-hole) |
| `M` | Tivra Ma | `OOO OOO` | F# | 1 | Easy fingering, can be weak in tone |
| `P` | Madhya Pa | `XXX XXX` | G | 2 | Medium (overblown) |
| `d` | Komal Dha | `XXX XXH` | G#/Ab | 2 | Hard |
| `D` | Dha | `XXX XXO` | A | 2 | Medium |
| `n` | Komal Ni | `XXX XHO` | A#/Bb | 2 | Hard |
| `N` | Ni | `XXX XOO` | B | 2 | Medium |
| `S'` | Taar Sa | `XXX OOO` | C | 2 | Medium |
| `r'` | Taar komal Re | `XXH OOO` | C#/Db | 2 | Hard |
| `R'` | Taar Re | `XXO OOO` | D | 2 | Medium-hard |
| `g'` | Taar komal Ga | `XHO OOO` | D#/Eb | 2 | Hard |
| `G'` | Taar Ga | `XOO OOO` | E | 2 | Hard |
| `m'` | Taar shuddha Ma | `HOO OOO` | F | 2 | Hard |
| `M'` | Taar tivra Ma | `OOO OOO` | F# | 2 | Hard |
| `P'` and above | Taar Pa... | Varies by flute (register 3) | G... | 3 | Advanced, see [L22](lessons/L22-taar-saptak.md) |

Important facts about this chart:

1. **All holes open gives tivra Ma (F#), not shuddha Ma (F).** Shuddha Ma needs a half-hole.
   This surprises many beginners and is the reason the curriculum teaches Raga Yaman (which
   uses tivra Ma) before ragas that need shuddha Ma.
2. **Mandra Pa and madhya Pa use the same fingers.** Only the breath changes. The same is
   true for Dha, Ni and Sa in neighbouring octaves.
3. **Some players use cross-fingerings** (for example `OXX OOO` or `OXX XOO` for shuddha Ma)
   instead of half-holes on particular flutes. Whether a cross-fingering is in tune depends on
   your flute. Test it with a tuner; do not assume.

## 4. Sargam text notation (ASCII)

Written Indian notation (Bhatkhande system) uses dots and lines above and below letters.
On a computer we use this ASCII equivalent. [L10](lessons/L10-reading-and-writing-sargam.md)
teaches it, and [L27](lessons/L27-bhatkhande-notation.md) shows the original Devanagari forms.

### 4.1 Note letters

| Write | Say | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `S` | Sa | 1st note, the tonic (home) |
| `r` / `R` | komal Re / Re | 2nd note, flat / natural |
| `g` / `G` | komal Ga / Ga | 3rd note, flat / natural |
| `m` / `M` | Ma / tivra Ma | 4th note, natural / sharp |
| `P` | Pa | 5th note (never altered) |
| `d` / `D` | komal Dha / Dha | 6th note, flat / natural |
| `n` / `N` | komal Ni / Ni | 7th note, flat / natural |

Rule of thumb: **lowercase = the lower version** of that note. Sa and Pa have only one version.

> Note: other ASCII systems exist. Some websites write tivra Ma as `M'` or `m+`, or use
> lowercase for all notes. Always read a source's legend before trusting its notation.

### 4.2 Octave (saptak) marks

| Write | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `.N` | dot **before** = mandra (lower) octave | `.P .D .N S` |
| `N` | no mark = madhya (middle) octave | `S R G` |
| `S'` | apostrophe **after** = taar (upper) octave | `N S' R'` |

### 4.3 Rhythm marks

| Write | Meaning |
|---|---|
| space | separates beats (each group = one beat) |
| `SR` (no space) | two notes in one beat (each gets half a beat) |
| `SRGm` | four notes in one beat (each gets a quarter) |
| `-` | hold the previous note through this beat |
| `_` | silence (rest) for this beat |
| `|` | division (vibhag) line within a cycle |
| `||` | end of a cycle or line |

Example (8 beats): `S R G - | P - G R ||` = Sa (1 beat), Re (1), Ga (2), Pa (2), Ga (1), Re (1).

### 4.4 Tala marks (written above the beat)

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `X` | sam, the first and strongest beat of the cycle (in notation only; not a fingering) |
| `0` | khali, the "empty" (wave) beat |
| `2`, `3`, `4` | tali, a clapped section start |

### 4.5 Ornament marks

| Write | Meaning | Taught in |
|---|---|---|
| `{G}R` | kan (grace note): touch G very briefly, land on R | [L24](lessons/L24-kan-khatka-murki.md) |
| `G~R` | meend (smooth glide) from G down to R | [L23](lessons/L23-meend.md) |
| `(RSNS)` | khatka/murki: fast turn played inside the time of one note | [L24](lessons/L24-kan-khatka-murki.md) |
| `G^` | andolan (slow oscillation) on G | [L33](lessons/L33-gamak-andolan-advanced-ornaments.md) |
| `,` | a breath mark (breathe here) | [L03](lessons/L03-breathing-basics.md) |

## 6. How claims are labelled

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Fact** | Standard, widely agreed information (for example, the beats in Teentaal) |
| **Recommendation** | The curriculum's teaching choice; reasonable teachers may differ |
| **Disputed** | Sources or traditions disagree; the disagreement is explained |
| **Uncertain** | Not verified, or depends on your individual flute; check it yourself |

## 7. If your flute is different

- **Different key** (for example E or G): the fingering, the sargam and every exercise stay
  the same, because sargam is relative to Sa. Only the "Western note" column changes. See
  [L26](lessons/L26-keys-transposition-and-limits.md).
- **7-hole flute:** the extra (little-finger) hole is normally kept closed for the standard
  fingerings above. It adds a lower note and alternative fingerings. Ask a teacher or check
  your maker's chart.
- **Very long flute** (bass flutes): the finger stretch is wider; posture advice in
  [L02](lessons/L02-posture-and-holding.md) becomes more important.
